# Creative Activities — Mission

**Website:** creativeactivities.org  
**Working tagline:** *Make something together.*  
**Supporting promise:** *Find an activity that fits the person, the moment, and what you have.*  
**Status:** Founding product thesis; hypotheses are not evidence.

## Our mission

**Help anyone caring for someone else turn an ordinary moment into a meaningful shared activity—regardless of age, ability, language, money, or creative confidence.**

We do this by offering free, practical, accessible creative activities that people can start with everyday materials, often without a screen, account, purchase, or special training.

Our purpose is not to keep people in an app. **Our purpose is to get people off the app and doing something together.**

## The problem we exist to solve

A daughter wants something enjoyable to do with her mother living with dementia. A grandparent wants to connect with a child. A parent needs a sensory-friendly afternoon activity. Someone spending most of the day in bed wants to make something rather than simply consume media.

They do not need another endless feed of ideas. They need an answer to a situational question:

> *What can we do together, right now, that this particular person can enjoy?*

Existing choices often demand too much: searching, comparing, adapting instructions, finding supplies, interpreting assumptions about ability, or filtering activities designed for someone else. The burden falls on people who may already be tired or time-constrained.

Our job is to remove that burden **without reducing a person to a diagnosis or treating care as a productivity metric**.

## Who we serve

**Primary chooser:** a family member, friend, caregiver, educator, or community volunteer looking for something appropriate to do *with* another person.

**Primary participant:** the person engaging in the activity. Their preferences, autonomy, dignity, safety, and enjoyment come first.

**Initial underserved situations to investigate:**

1. Families seeking enjoyable activities with older adults, including people living with dementia.
2. People with limited mobility or energy who want satisfying seated or bedside creative activities.
3. Parents and caregivers seeking adaptable sensory-friendly and creative activities for children.
4. Grandparents and children looking for shared creative experiences across generations.

These are **discovery segments**, not labels that determine what an individual can or cannot do. Never assume that age, disability, or diagnosis determines someone's interests or capabilities.

## The product promise

A visitor should be able to:

1. **Find** an activity through search or a clear situational guide.
2. **Fit** it to the person's interests, communication, mobility, sensory preferences, energy, and available materials.
3. **Start** in roughly a minute, when practical, with one readable page or printable card.
4. **Share the moment** with another person—or enjoy it independently if preferred.
5. **Adapt or stop** without pressure, judgment, or a false promise of therapeutic benefit.

The outcome is not “content consumed.” The outcome is **an activity attempted, adapted, enjoyed, or learned from**.

## What makes us different

**1. Startable, not scrollable.** Every activity is actionable, with an estimated setup time, duration, materials, instructions, and an easy exit. No infinite inspiration feed as the default.

**2. Fit the moment, not the demographic.** Search and filters prioritize *sitting or standing, visual or tactile, calm or energetic, one-on-one or group, 5 minutes or 30*, not simply “for seniors” or “for autism.” A diagnosis can be relevant context, never the entire personalization model.

**3. One idea, many adaptations.** An activity such as collaborative collage may have large-grip, low-vision, nonverbal, low-mess, bedside, or intergenerational variations. This reduces fragmentation while preserving useful search pages for distinct needs.

**4. Offline human connection is the product.** We measure whether the guidance helps people do something meaningful away from the screen—not time-on-site.

**5. Accessible by default.** Plain language, large type, strong contrast, keyboard access, print support, clear images, simple steps, low-bandwidth delivery, and translations reviewed for usability.

**6. Evidence and humility.** We describe activities as creative, recreational, supportive, or engaging. We do **not** claim they treat dementia, autism, stroke, anxiety, or any condition without appropriate evidence and clinical review. “Art therapy” is a professional practice, not a marketing synonym for crafts.

**7. Free core value.** A person can read, print, and use the activity without a login or subscription wall. Business models must never make essential activities contingent on a vulnerable person's data.

## Our non-negotiables

- **Dignity:** Never infantilize adults or frame disability as personal failure. Show people with agency and varied interests.
- **Consent:** Offer choices. If a participant declines, respect the answer; do not pressure them to complete the activity.
- **Safety:** Label scissors, heat, choking risks, skin-contact materials, allergens, fall risks, ingestion hazards, and supervision requirements where relevant. Adaptations are not medical advice.
- **Privacy:** No sensitive health profile is required to get recommendations. Collect the minimum data necessary; explain why; provide deletion controls where personal data exists.
- **Accessibility:** Build and test with people who use assistive technologies and with real caregivers, not only with designers.
- **Cultural openness:** Avoid assuming one holiday, family structure, income, or cultural tradition is universal.
- **Provenance:** Identify content authors, reviewers, dates, and evidence limitations. Credit creators; do not scrape or republish others' work without permission.
- **No manufactured urgency:** No guilt-driven notifications, streak shame, fear-based marketing, or claims that using the app makes someone a better child or parent.

## What we are not

- Not a medical device, rehabilitation program, or replacement for professional art therapy.
- Not a general AI chatbot competing on breadth of answers.
- Not a social network, engagement feed, or advertising trap.
- Not a collection of lightly rewritten SEO pages with nearly identical activities.
- Not a company that equates disability, old age, or neurodivergence with a problem to be “fixed.”

## The founding question

> **Can we become the most useful answer on the internet to “What can we do together today?”—especially when ordinary activity sites don't fit someone's real circumstances?**

We will let search behavior, observed use, and respectful conversations with participants and caregivers—not founder enthusiasm—decide whether the answer is yes.
