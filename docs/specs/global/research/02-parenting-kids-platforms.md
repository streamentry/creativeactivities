# 02 Parenting, early-childhood and kids' creative-activity platforms

**Scope and date:** Research as of 2026-11-14. Covers parenting and early-childhood activity sites, kids' subscription boxes, kids' learning games, printables marketplaces, screen-time guidance and children's privacy rules. Relevant sources are numbered in section 8 and accessed 2026-11-14.

**Evidence limits.** Several primary sites refused automated fetches (Lovevery, KiwiCo subscription pages, Khan Academy Kids on learningapps, PBS KIDS parents, WHO and AAP pages were 404 or redirected). Where that happened, figures come from search snippets or secondary aggregators and are marked "unverified". Pricing in particular must be rechecked on the live site before anything is published. Montessori and Reggio-inspired sites, and the National Autistic Society, were not researched in this pass and are listed as gaps.

**Relevance to us.** The mission is situational: "what can we do together, right now, with what we have?" Parents and grandparents with 5 to 20 minutes, children who dislike messy textures, and families on a basic phone are the target. Clinical claims are out of bounds (MISSION, VISION guardrails). Sensory, developmental and screen-time framing must stay descriptive, not diagnostic.

## 3. Feature patterns worth adopting

1. **Age tabs as the primary navigation (Busy Toddler, KiwiCo, Lovevery).** Age stages are easier to parse than "difficulty." Adopt age bands with an explicit "adjust for ability" control, not a diagnosis-based filter.
2. **A "what you need" line in every activity (Busy Toddler, Hands On As We Grow).** Our signature experience (DESIGN section 1) already requires this. Keep it.
3. **Category tiles with clear verbs (Busy Toddler).** Sensory, Fine Motor, Art, Outdoor, Bath Time. These are everyday words, not clinical labels.
4. **Free weekly plan by email (Hands On As We Grow).** A printable one-page weekly plan with three to five activities is a low-commitment entry point that does not require an account. Adopt, without paywalling it.
5. **Free content with a clear paid edge (Artful Parent, Busy Toddler, Hands On As We Grow).** Free depth first, then paid structure (full sequences, printables packs, offline bundles). Our VISION says the library stays free; paid extras must be clearly separated.
6. **Seasonal collections and gift guides (Busy Toddler).** These capture time-bound search. Cheap to produce from the activity library.
7. **Game design that respects the child (Khan Academy Kids, CBeebies Learn, PBS KIDS).** No ads, no in-app purchases, no tracking across apps. This is the trust benchmark for any kids' surface (section 5).
8. **Parent-facing materials alongside child content (PBS KIDS for Parents, CBeebies Parenting, Sesame for Families).** Co-play guidance is a standard feature of kids' platforms. We should show adults how to join, not only what the child does.
9. **Developmental framing as "what this builds" in plain terms (Lovevery, Sesame).** Lovevery's stage-based framing is effective. Adopt the stage language, but describe activities as "playful practice" and never as treatment or skill-remediation.
10. **Outdoor, seasonal "leader" format (Tinkergarten).** Adopt the idea of short, repeatable outdoor sessions with a simple weekly rhythm. Reject the leader-licensing model for now (it adds operational load).

## 4. Content patterns

- **Format.** Short activity posts (Busy Toddler, Hands On As We Grow) with photo steps, a materials list and a time estimate. Printables (Twinkl, Activity Village, PBS KIDS) for paper-based activities. Video (Artful Parent club, CBeebies Parenting) for technique. Our default should be the one-page card (DESIGN signature experience).
- **Volume.** Busy Toddler and Hands On As We Grow publish frequently; Twinkl and Activity Village rely on very large libraries. Volume is not our strategy. Our advantage is situational fit, so 200 well-tagged activities with real adaptations beat 2,000 generic ones.
- **Cadence.** Weekly plans and seasonal collections are the common cadence. Adopt both, but keep them optional.
- **SEO and structure.** Activity posts are organized by age, category and season. Titles follow query language ("sensory activities for toddlers at home"). Each page answers a single question. Note the caution in VISION: keyword data must be exported for specific queries in one defined geography, and Google Ads competition is not SEO difficulty.
- **Safety labelling.** Choking hazards must be labelled. In the US, the CPSC small-parts rule bans small-parts products for children under 3 that present a choking hazard, and certain products carry a warning label (16 CFR 1500.19; labelling wording in 1500.20) [17][18]. Secondary sources disagree on which age ranges the label covers, so check eCFR before writing rules. For our activities, the safe default is: flag small items (beads, buttons, dried beans, small craft pieces), state "adult supervision required", and mark "not for under 3" where materials are small. This is a product-safety choice, not a regulatory claim.

## 5. Games and interactive patterns

- **No ads, no in-app purchases, no tracking across apps.** Khan Academy Kids states this directly ("no ads, no subscriptions", no third-party tracking of children, no email needed) [6]. PBS KIDS games are free with no in-app purchases [7]. CBeebies Learn is described as free and ad-free [9]. This is achievable and is the trust baseline.
- **Co-play design.** Parent-plus-child activities should show the adult role on the same screen (e.g., "Adult: say the word. Child: find the color"). PBS KIDS and Sesame both pair games with off-screen activities.
- **Offline first.** CBeebies Learn works offline. For us, the printable card is the offline primary path.
- **Progress without shame.** Avoid streaks that punish missed days. Khan Kids and PBS KIDS show progress without loss framing. Our product should record "activities done together" as a positive count only.
- **Screen-time framing.** WHO guidance for under-5s sets sedentary screen limits: none under 1, no more than one hour a day at age 2 (secondary summary; the WHO document is ID 9789241550536) [19]. AAP's 2016 policy, summarized in secondary sources, is no more than about one hour a day of high-quality programming for ages 2 to 5, with co-viewing [20]. Our site should recommend off-screen activities and never ask a child to use a screen for longer than these limits.

## 6. Gaps no one serves well (our openings)

1. **Situational, multi-age, no-purchase activities.** Most kids' platforms assume one child, one stage, and a purchase or account. Few answer "my four-year-old, my grandmother, twenty minutes, nothing bought."
2. **Adaptation for the adult as well as the child.** Platforms adapt for the child's age. Few adapt for a tired parent, a grandparent with limited mobility, or a sibling. The intergenerational angle is largely unserved outside craft kits.
3. **Sensory-friendly activities that are not clinical.** Understood and OT-led sites offer sensory-friendly indoor activities and home strategies [21][22], but these are written for diagnosed children and parents seeking intervention. There is little for families who simply want low-mess, low-noise, texture-flexible play with a clear "choose your level" option.
4. **Honest "what this is not" statements.** Few sites state clearly that activities are recreational and not therapy or assessment. We can be the trustworthy example.
5. **Free, printable, multilingual, low-literacy cards.** Printables marketplaces are large but paid and teacher-facing. Free, plain-language, picture-led cards for families on basic phones are rare.
6. **Adult-led co-play guidance for kids' games.** Parent tips exist (PBS KIDS, Sesame) but are secondary to the game. A product that puts the adult role first is unclaimed.
7. **Transparent cost.** Subscription pricing is hard to find or compare across Lovevery, KiwiCo and similar. Clear free and paid lines, shown before sign-up, would stand out.

## 7. Anti-patterns and risks to avoid

- **Subscription traps and price opacity.** Several boxes and memberships require sign-up to see prices, or rely on prepay discounts (secondary sources). Show price and cancel terms before any account is created.
- **Paywalling the basics.** Activity Village-style membership gates on core printables undermine the "free core" rule. Keep the core library free.
- **Ad-supported kids' surfaces.** PBS KIDS, CBeebies and Khan Kids are ad-free by design. Any ads on family pages must not run next to children's content and must not use child data (COPPA amendments require separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties, including for targeted ads; compliance was due 22 April 2026) [23][24].
- **Children's design code.** The UK ICO Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code) bars nudge techniques that push children to weaken privacy or share unnecessary data (nudge standard, numbered 12 or 13 depending on source; check the ICO PDF) and requires a detrimental-use assessment [25]. Do not collect child data beyond what the activity needs. No child accounts unless strictly necessary.
- **Clinical language creep.** Sensory and developmental terms ("therapeutic", "treats", "improves [condition]") are the fastest route to a claim we cannot support and to a regulator's attention. Use "sensory-friendly", "calming", "supports play".
- **Developmental over-promise.** Lovevery-style "builds X skill" claims need evidence, or should be framed as "playful practice of X".
- **Streak and guilt mechanics.** Duolingo-style streaks and "you missed three days" messages are unsuitable for parents and carers, who already feel time pressure.
- **Engagement-first screen time.** Autoplay, endless feeds and notification bait conflict with WHO and AAP limits and with our mission to get people off the app.
- **Shallow generated variants.** Twinkl-scale volume produced by templating would repeat the "misleading page proliferation" risk in VISION. Each age and adaptation needs real content.
- **Safety omissions.** Printed or app activities with small parts and no supervision line are a real harm. Every material list needs a safety line.
