# CARE: older adults, dementia and caregivers

This file is the content program for the **CARE** pillar: activities for older adults, including people living with dementia, and for the people who spend time with them. It covers the settings where that time happens (home with family, living alone, care home, hospital bedside, day center, remote family), the activity families that work in them, late-stage and bedside activity, the care-home activity-coordinator program, caregiver wellbeing, the four existing search hubs, the R5 ranked clusters 1 to 11 as content programs with target counts, the review process, and where the 88 existing guides fit. Items use the `CNT-900s` block. Creative wellbeing for adults in general is in [CALM](10-pillar-creative-wellbeing.md). Children and grandchildren are in [GROW](08-pillar-parenting-kids.md) and shared-age activities in the TOGETHER lens.

## 1. The job

Someone in this pillar is usually not looking for "dementia content". They want to do something with a particular person, now, in a particular place: a daughter who visits her father on Sunday afternoons, a care assistant with twenty minutes before lunch, a hospital volunteer at a bedside, an older person who lives alone and wants to make something with their hands. MISSION asks one question for all of them: *What can we do together, right now, that this particular person can enjoy?*

The pillar succeeds when a person starts an activity that fits, adapts it when it does not, and stops without guilt when that is the right call. It does not succeed by producing a dementia-specific page for every query. DESIGN.md §4A warns against "25 hubs that are the same list with 'senior' swapped for 'dementia' or 'child'"; R5 §6 says the same about duplicate intent. We write fewer, better pages with real adaptations.

Two use contexts from the [senior-first spec](../senior-first/README.md) apply here too: **independent** (the person chooses and does it alone) and **shared or supported** (someone sits with them and keeps it comfortable). Most activity pages should work in both.

## 2. Ability, not diagnosis

Pages in this pillar describe what a person can do and enjoy. They do not sort people into stages or diagnoses.

**Rules for every CARE page**

1. **No diagnosis or stage input.** No finder question, filter or tag asks for diagnosis, disease stage, cognition score or age (DESIGN.md §3; senior-first). Facets are energy, sitting or bed use, sensory preference, communication mode, help level, group size and mess.
2. **Stage is context, never a filter.** Existing guides named `early-stage`, `middle-stage` and `late-stage` may explain how needs change in prose. Activity cards do not carry a stage label. A card says "good when a person tires quickly", not "for late-stage dementia".
3. **Dementia is named when it helps the search.** Use the word on a page when the reader needs it to find the page (R5 cluster 7). Do not write it into every adaptation.
4. **No recall tests.** Use "Tell me about this," not "Do you remember?" (the [reminiscence guide](../../../site/src/content/guides/activities/reminiscence.md) already says this; the rule applies to every card and game).
5. **Adult design.** Adult language and adult-looking materials. No babyish motifs, no "fun for grandma" framing (DESIGN.md §1; R4 §3 reviews of "toddler-like" word search).
6. **Choice is explicit.** Every activity offers at least two ways to take part, including "watch and talk" and "no drawing, just sit together" where it fits (see [CALM](10-pillar-creative-wellbeing.md) for the same principle).
7. **No claims of benefit.** Use "enjoyable", "calming for some people", "a way to spend time together". Do not write "improves memory", "slows decline", "reduces agitation" or "keeps the brain sharp" (R4 §5 and §7; R3 §5 wording rules). Benefit sentences need a row in the evidence table in R3 §5 and a dated source.

**Say / avoid (CARE copy)**

| Say | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "Many people with dementia enjoy making things together. Check with their care team." | "Proven to help dementia." |
| "Tell me about this photo." | "Do you remember who this is?" |
| "If they tire, stop. You can come back to it tomorrow." | "Patients must complete the task." |
| "Adapt the grip, the table or the length to suit them." | "Dementia-friendly craft kit" as a product claim |
| "Seated version" or "bed version" | "Late-stage version" |

## 4. Activity families

Each family is a set of activity types. A family is not a page. Cards and variations live under it; families help editors avoid gaps (for example, no spiritual or faith-and-culture activities at all) and help the finder group results.

| CNT | Family | What it is | Adaptation axes | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CNT-920 | Reminiscence (non-quiz) | Photos, objects, memory boxes, songs and stories, with comments and open invitations | Number of items; single photo or spread; seated or bed | Quizzing; photos of people the reader does not know; grief when a memory is sad (comfort first, then move on) |
| CNT-921 | Music | Playing, singing, choosing a playlist, making a songbook, tapping a rhythm | Sing, hum, clap or hold a shaker; listen only; small or large speakers | Loud or sudden audio; music that is only the family's taste (ask); no "music therapy" claim |

| CNT-924 | Art (adult, seated) | Watercolor washes, tearing paper, collage, color sheets, shared pictures | Large grip, pre-cut shapes, fewer colors, low mess, low vision (high contrast) | Drawing tests; "make it look right"; paints that stain clothing |

## 5. Late-stage and bedside

Late-stage and bedside activity needs its own rules. Some people who live with dementia communicate mostly without words, have limited movement, or spend most of the day in bed. Some people in hospital have the same needs for a short time. The rules below apply to any activity with a bedside or late-stage variation. They are written as product rules; each activity still needs review by an occupational therapist or nurse.

**CNT-940 · Bedside and late-stage variation rules** · P0 · Phase S1 · Status new · Pillars: CARE (TOGETHER)

Why: R5 cluster 1 has the strongest fit with the mission and low competition; bedside appears in the existing hub and pack (MISSION, "someone spending most of the day in bed"). [R5 §6]

What: every card with a bedside or late-stage variation carries a "Bedside version" block that states: the position it works in; what is on the bed and what must stay off it; what needs a second person; what to avoid (sharp tools, water near electrical equipment, small parts, anything swallowable); how to wash hands and clean materials; how to stop; and what to do if the person becomes distressed. Materials are single-use or wipeable where possible. Hospital and care settings get a "check with the nurse or staff member first" line.

Acceptance:
- No bedside card uses water, flame, knives, scissors, or small parts (under 3 cm) without a supervised variant stated.
- Each bedside block names what to do if the person coughs, chokes or becomes distressed, and says "stop and ask the nurse or your care team" for any clinical sign. The page never tells a visitor how to assess health.
- The variation is reviewed by an occupational therapist or nurse (CNT-961) and the review is recorded on the page.
- Touch activities (hand massage, texture) include a consent line and an easy exit; touch is never the only route in.
- Accessible: works one-handed, with a high-contrast print version, and with sound off.

Depends on: CNT-961 review record; [TSA](14-trust-safety-accessibility-privacy.md) for the general safety rules (owned there).

Risks / not doing: no clinical advice, no transfer or positioning instructions, no claim of comfort or pain relief. Do not put this block on pages that were not reviewed.

## 6. The care-home activity-coordinator program

Activity coordinators in care homes and day centers plan the same kind of group session repeatedly, often with little budget and a shared room. They are a natural partner and a natural feedback loop. R5 §4 recommends a free sheet for them. This program turns that into three products.

**CNT-941 · Activity-coordinator kit** · P1 · Phase S2 · Status new · Pillars: CARE, TOGETHER

Why: coordinators need repeatable group plans; partner adoption is one of the measures of #1 in the README ("care homes ... recommend us"). [R5 §4; README "Definition of #1"]

What: a kit for coordinators, free and with no login, made of three printable items:

1. **Monthly calendar** (A4 and US Letter, large type): one theme per week, three activities per week, each marked with a group or one-to-one tick, the materials and the time. Themes are seasonal and offered as a choice (for example "garden, home, music, memories"), not a fixed holiday list.
2. **Group session plan** (one page, 30 to 45 minutes): opening question, three levels of participation (sing, hum, clap; match pictures, point; watch and talk), one clear step at a time, a closing, and a quiet exit. No scores, no winners, no timers visible to participants.
3. **Printable program** for families and visitors: what happened, what to try at a visit, and a line that says the session is designed for the people taking part, not for the family to judge.

Acceptance:
- Every session plan has a no-winners rule and a quiet exit for a person who wants to watch or leave.
- Each plan states group size range, seating layout and the staff or volunteer count needed to help one or two people each.
- No resident is named or shown in a photo unless the home has written consent for that image. The kit ships with no photos of residents.
- The kit says it does not replace the care plan or the coordinator's judgement and does not describe the home's regulated services.
- Coordinators can give feedback in one step ("worked as written", "worked with a change", "did not suit this group"), with no account. This feeds [COM](11-community-ugc.md).
- Reviewed by at least one experienced activity coordinator and one dementia specialist (CNT-961) before first use.

Depends on: CNT-961; the printable pack format in [06](06-content-library.md); feedback in COM-006 (see [11](11-community-ugc.md)).

Risks / not doing: no branded endorsement by a care provider without written permission (R5 §4); no inspection or compliance claims; no paid coordinator subscription in the free core ([16 business model](16-business-model.md) owns any paid tier).

## 7. Caregiver wellbeing

Caregivers are a primary chooser in MISSION. They are also the people most likely to be told to "look after yourself" and least likely to have time. The pillar takes a small, honest stance.

**CNT-943 · Caregiver wellbeing in activity pages** · P0 · Phase S1 · Status new · Pillars: CARE, CALM

What:
- A short "For you too" note on situation pages and the dementia activity cards: an activity can be short, can be done sitting down, can be stopped, and can be done by a person who has already had a long day. No guilt, no "good caregiver" language (DESIGN.md §9).
- Every caregiver page links to the existing [caregiver self-care guide](../../../site/src/content/guides/caregiving/caregiver-self-care.md) and to the other caregiving guides for hard moments (repeated questions, pain, when they say no).
- Activities for two, where the caregiver is also a participant: shared picture, shared songbook, a letter written together. The caregiver's enjoyment is part of the design, not a cost.
- A visible, plain line that the site is not a crisis service, and a link to the crisis routes in [CALM](10-pillar-creative-wellbeing.md) for caregivers who are in distress.

Acceptance:
- No streak, no daily reminder, no "you have not done anything today" prompt on the caregiver path.
- No caregiver health or burden questions. No "how stressed are you" score.
- Crisis route is visible on caregiver hubs without a keyword match.
- Caregiver pages pass the wording rules in section 2.

Risks / not doing: we do not offer respite-care matching, legal or financial advice, or a "burnout" score. Those belong to other services that the site links to after review.

## 10. Review

Care content makes the strongest claims about safety, comfort and people's abilities, so it needs the strongest review.

**CNT-961 · Dementia and older-adult review panel** · P0 · Phase S1 · Status new · Pillars: CARE, CALM

Why: DESIGN.md requires named author, reviewer, date and scope; R3 §7 says a reviewer's name only with written permission and a stated scope; the baseline says every byline reads "not yet reviewed by an outside expert". [R3 §7; DESIGN.md §5; senior-first baseline]

What:
- **Panel roles (at least one each before first publication of a CARE card):**
  - Dementia specialist (a clinician or practitioner who works with people living with dementia, such as a dementia nurse specialist or a dementia-practice psychologist).
  - Occupational therapist for seating, grip, adaptation, bedside safety and energy.
  - Experienced activity coordinator (care home or day center) for group plans.
  - Family caregiver with lived experience, paid for their time, for tone and "what worked for us".
  - Music therapist, only when a page makes a music claim (not for ordinary music activities).
- Reviewers confirm their own registration or credential from the body that issues it (for example the UK HCPC register or the relevant US board). The site does not show a credential that has not been checked.
- Each review record says: reviewer role, name (only with written permission), date, what was reviewed, what the activity is **not** for, which people should not use it without supervision, and the stop signals to watch for.
- No "clinically reviewed" badge unless the review is documented in the record. A review of an activity does not make it therapy, and the page must say so where relevant.
- A reviewer may decline a page. A declined page is not published.

Acceptance: no CARE card goes live without a review record; the record is visible on the page; a correction link is present (DESIGN.md §4B); the review is repeated at least yearly or after a material change.

Risks / not doing: paid "clinical endorsements"; reviewers who are employed by a product being listed; collecting reviewers' personal data beyond name and credential.
