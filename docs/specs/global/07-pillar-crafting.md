# 07 · MAKE: crafts, art and making

MAKE is the pillar for doing something with the hands and the materials to hand: paper, paint, clay, thread, leaves, cardboard, buttons and whatever is in the recycling. It is the largest pillar and the one that already has the most content (38 cards, most of them tagged here). Its job is narrower than the incumbents'. It does not try to be the biggest craft library. It answers "what can we make together with what is here, in a way this person can manage?" and it always shows the adaptation next to the activity. The content types, quality bar and phase rules are in [06](06-content-library.md); this file sets the MAKE taxonomy, the adaptations, the safety notes, the substitution program and how we beat the platforms that already rank.

**Read with:** [06 content library](06-content-library.md) · [08 GROW](08-pillar-parenting-kids.md) · [09 CARE](09-pillar-older-adults-dementia.md) · [10 CALM](10-pillar-creative-wellbeing.md) · [13 localization](13-localization.md) · [14 trust, safety, accessibility](14-trust-safety-accessibility-privacy.md) · [R1 craft platforms](research/01-craft-diy-platforms.md) · [R5 search and trust](research/05-search-scale-trust.md)

## 1. Why this pillar, and what it is not

- MAKE has the clearest demand and the most competition. R5 ranks broad craft queries ("crafts for kids at home", "easy crafts for adults") at the bottom because Pinterest, retailers and publishers dominate them ([R5 §6](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#6-ranked-content-clusters)). We do not win those with volume.
- We win where the adaptation is the product: "easy crafts for seniors with arthritis or limited dexterity" (R5 rank 2), seated and bedside making (R5 rank 1, shared with CARE), and low-mess versions for people who do not want a mess (R1 §6).
- MAKE is not a craft-kit shop, a skills course, or a feed of ideas. Projects end. A finished thing can be a small one.

## 2. Taxonomy

Sixteen categories. Each card is tagged with one primary category and may list a second. Counts below are approximate: a card can sit in two categories, so they do not sum to 38.

### CNT-701 · Paper

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** fold a paper boat (exists); make a paper chain garland (exists); weave paper strips (exists); tear-paper mosaic (exists); fold a paper fan; paper snowflake; paper-plate sun; folded paper bookmark; paper lantern with a battery tea light.
- **Adaptations:** creases pre-scored for the first fold; thick card for grip; tape in place of folds for one-handed use; larger sheets for low vision; a finished sample to copy.
- **Safety:** safety scissors for under-fives; no craft knife on paper projects; check for paper cuts on fingers and warn about dry fingers.
- **Target:** existing approx. 4 · S2 4 · S4 10.

### CNT-702 · Painting

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** paint a garden stone (exists); paint a shared picture (exists); paint with watercolors (exists); decorate a plant pot (exists); decorate a keepsake box (exists); sponge-paint a sunset; dip-dye coffee filters; marble paper with shaving cream and food-color (skin-sensitivity check); paint a paper plate sunflower.
- **Adaptations:** sponge or roller in place of a brush; chunky brushes with built-up grips; paint pre-poured into a shallow lid; washable paints on a wipe-clean mat; one color only for less choice and less visual load; a stencil for a shape.
- **Safety:** non-toxic, labelled paints only; no solvent or spray paint indoors; for shaving-cream marbling, check for skin reactions first; wash hands after; keep paint away from mouths and the eyes of young children.
- **Target:** existing approx. 5 · S2 6 · S4 12.

### CNT-703 · Drawing

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** draw repeating patterns (exists); draw to music (exists); sketch what is on the table (exists); blind contour drawing of a hand or a fruit; draw the house you grew up in (reminiscence; see [09](09-pillar-older-adults-dementia.md)); continuous-line drawing of a partner.
- **Adaptations:** a large marker or a thick crayon; a board to rest the paper on; a light grid drawn in pencil for those who want a frame; drawing with the non-dominant hand as a playful choice; a picture to trace for someone who wants a guide.
- **Safety:** non-toxic crayons and markers; for people who may mouth tools, use larger, non-toxic pieces.
- **Target:** existing approx. 3 · S2 4 · S4 10.

### CNT-704 · Collage

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** make a color collage (exists); make a memory collage (exists); collage from old greeting cards; fabric scrap collage; seed-packet collage; leaf-and-paper collage on a tray.
- **Adaptations:** pre-cut shapes; a glue stick in place of liquid glue; a tray with raised edges to stop pieces falling; a large sheet for the center and small pieces only at the edge; a single theme.
- **Safety:** glue stick or white craft glue; small paper pieces are a choking hazard for under-threes; keep them in a tray.
- **Target:** existing approx. 2 · S2 3 · S4 8.

### CNT-705 · Clay and dough

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** shape a pinch pot (exists); air-dry clay coaster; salt-dough hand print keepsake; press-moulded clay tile; roll and cut shapes from modelling clay; clay bead threading (see CNT-711).
- **Adaptations:** soft clay for low grip strength; rolling pin with large handles; tools with large grips; a board with a lip so pieces stay put; a pre-wetted sponge in place of water spray.
- **Safety:** salt dough is inedible and not for mouthing; use non-toxic, labelled clay; check allergies (wheat flour in dough); wash hands after; air-dry clay is dusty, so use a damp cloth, not a sweep.
- **Target:** existing approx. 1 · S2 3 · S4 8.

### CNT-706 · Textiles, yarn, sewing and knitting

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** wind a ball of yarn (exists); make a lavender sachet (exists); finger knitting; pompom making; a sewing card with a large-eye needle and yarn; no-sew fabric bunting with fabric glue; a dishcloth in chunky yarn for people who knit; winding wool for a friend who knits.
- **Adaptations:** chunky yarn on large needles (10 mm or bigger) for grip; ergonomic needle handles; a yarn winder or a bottle as a tool; finger knitting for one hand; sewing cards with large holes; a tabletop clamp to hold fabric.
- **Safety:** cut yarn lengths for under-threes or avoid loose yarn near them, since loops and strands can wrap; blunt needles for young children; count pins and needles in and out; no sewing with a person who may mouth small items.
- **Target:** existing approx. 2 · S2 4 · S4 12.

### CNT-707 · Nature crafts

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** arrange flowers together (exists); make a pine-cone bird feeder (exists); press leaves and flowers (exists); plant seeds in a pot (exists); watch birds from a window (exists); make a texture board (exists); leaf rubbing; a dried-grass bundle; a stone cairn in a garden.
- **Adaptations:** pre-cut stems; a vase on a stable base; pressing kits that need no tools; a table-height garden or a windowsill; a seat outdoors with shade; a plant that is easy to water.
- **Safety:** pick nothing protected and nothing you cannot identify; never taste or handle unknown berries or fungi; wash hands after soil and sap; check for pollen and plant allergies; check for ticks after outdoor sessions; keep seeds and small nuts away from under-threes.
- **Target:** existing approx. 6 · S2 6 · S4 12.

### CNT-708 · Printmaking

Priority P2 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** print with leaves (exists); potato stamp (adult cuts, the group prints); sponge-stamp prints; bubble-wrap printing; string printing on card; foam-sheet stamps; vegetable prints using a cut-in-advance halved pepper.
- **Adaptations:** pre-cut stamps; a stamp with a handle; a larger pad or tray; a roller in place of stamping; prints on a fixed template to limit choice.
- **Safety:** an adult cuts any stamp with a knife, always away from the body and the hands of the person printing; non-toxic ink; wash hands and surfaces; not for people with cut or dye sensitivities without a check.
- **Target:** existing approx. 1 · S2 2 · S4 6.

### CNT-709 · Recycled and upcycled

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** make a sealed shaker (exists); make a button picture (exists); decorate a plant pot (exists); cardboard-tube bird; egg-carton flowers; cereal-box picture frame; yoghurt-pot planter; bottle-cap mosaic.
- **Adaptations:** a pre-cut template; a lid with a large grip; a sealed container with no small loose pieces; a wide, stable base; fewer parts.
- **Safety:** no glass; check for sharp edges and tin lids; a craft knife only with an adult; seal anything that holds small items (see CNT-711); buttons and bottle caps are small parts.
- **Target:** existing approx. 3 · S2 5 · S4 10.

### CNT-710 · Card-making

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** make a greeting card (exists); make a photo postcard (exists); write a letter together (exists, also writing); a simple pop-up card; a thank-you card with a pressed flower; a seed-paper card.
- **Adaptations:** a pre-folded card; a stamp in place of drawing; a template for the message; large lettering guides; a card with a window cut for a photo.
- **Safety:** paper cuts; craft glue or a glue stick; pressed flowers only from plants that are safe to handle.
- **Target:** existing approx. 3 · S2 3 · S4 9.

### CNT-711 · Beads and jewelry

Priority P3 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** bead bracelet on stretchy cord (large beads, seated); paper-bead necklace (rolled paper strips with glue); clay-bead threading; a pressed-flower pendant under a laminating sheet; a beaded keyring.
- **Adaptations:** large beads with big holes; a bead threader or a bead board; contrasting cord and beads for low vision; a bead tray with raised edges; a pre-strung base for one-handed use.
- **Safety (strict):** beads and small pieces are a choking hazard for children under three and for anyone who mouths things. The default is no beads for under-threes and for any participant who mouths objects; use paper beads or large beads only where the person's safety has been considered. Never use resin or epoxy (fumes, skin). Keep loose beads in a tray and count them out and in.
- **Target:** existing 0 · S2 3 · S4 8.

### CNT-712 · Woodless simple construction

Priority P2 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** decorate a keepsake box (exists); cardboard-box house; shoebox diorama; tissue-box garden; paper-tube tower; a cardboard village; a card frame stand.
- **Adaptations:** pre-scored card; tabs in place of fixing; a base that already stands; one piece to fill rather than a whole build; tape or glue dots for one-handed use.
- **Safety:** no saws, no hammers, no power tools (this is what "woodless" means here); craft knives adult-only and never with a person who may hurt themselves; hot glue only with an adult and a burn warning; cardboard edges can cut.
- **Target:** existing approx. 1 · S2 3 · S4 8.

### CNT-713 · Seasonal and holiday, across cultures

Priority P1 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** paper chain garland (exists, holiday); pine-cone bird feeder (exists, winter); paper lantern for a mid-autumn festival; papel picado (cut-tissue banners, Mexico); Chinese paper-cut window decoration; a rangoli made from dry rice or paper for Diwali; a red-envelope card for Lunar New Year; a paper bat for Halloween; a leaf turkey for Thanksgiving.
- **Adaptations:** seated and low-dexterity versions by default (R5 cluster 6); pre-cut patterns; large prints; a finished sample.
- **Cultural rule:** every seasonal card is written with a reviewer from or familiar with the tradition ([13](13-localization.md)). Religious symbols are not used as decoration without that context. We do not present a tradition as one thing, and we do not assume a visitor celebrates.
- **Safety:** LED lights only in lanterns; no candles; check decorations for small parts.
- **Target:** existing approx. 2 · S2 4 · S4 12.

### CNT-714 · Photography and phone crafts

Priority P2 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** make a photo postcard (exists, also card-making); make a memory collage (exists, also collage); a photo hunt in the home for one color; a paper-figure stop-motion film (adult-led, no child's face published); a photo flip book from printed prints; a tablet photo collage with large buttons.
- **Adaptations:** large-print photos; photos on a tablet with a stand; a single photo at a time; a printed picture where a screen is hard to see; voice-led choices.
- **Privacy:** photos of family stay on the device or in the family's hands. Never publish a photo of an identifiable person without consent, and never a child's photo without a parental release ([06 §4](06-content-library.md#4-media-rights-and-provenance)).
- **Target:** existing approx. 2 · S2 3 · S4 6.

### CNT-715 · Writing and bookmaking

Priority P2 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** write a letter together (exists); start a family recipe book (exists); make a personal songbook (exists, also music); a folded paper zine; a life-story card deck; a bookmark with a short saying; a word-picture journal; a handmade book with stapled pages.
- **Adaptations:** dictated writing with the helper writing; a card for each prompt; a large-print template; a pre-cut book cover; a pen with a thick barrel.
- **Safety:** stapler and bone folder: adult use with a stable table; sewn books only with a blunt needle; no ink that is not labelled non-toxic.
- **Target:** existing approx. 3 · S2 3 · S4 9.

### CNT-716 · Music-making with household items

Priority P2 · Phase S2

- **Examples:** tap a rhythm together (exists); make a playlist together (exists); make a personal songbook (exists, also writing); draw to music (exists, also drawing); a rice shaker in a sealed tin; a pot-lid drum; a cup song with plastic cups; a cardboard-tube rain stick (sealed).
- **Adaptations:** one-beat rhythms; a large beater or soft mallet; instruments on a stable base; sitting or standing; music picked by the person.
- **Safety:** hearing: keep volume moderate; no glass or bottles, use plastic or metal; seal anything holding small items; no balloons near under-threes.
- **Target:** existing approx. 4 · S2 3 · S4 10.

## 4. Materials you already have, and substitutions (CNT-720)

- **Priority P0 · Phase S1 · Status new.**
- **Why:** the MISSION promise is "find an activity that fits... what you have". R1 records that Ravelry's "stash" lets people match projects to supplies they already own, which reduces buying pressure ([R1 §5](research/01-craft-diy-platforms.md#5-games-and-interactive-patterns)).
- **What:** each card lists its materials and up to three substitutions; a shared substitution table covers the common swaps. A "what I have" checklist ([06 CNT-007](06-content-library.md#cnt-007--printable-template)) lets a visitor tick what is at home. The filter that uses it is owned by [03](03-features-web.md).
- **Starter substitution table** (each swap is tested before it goes in):

| Need | Swap | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| White craft glue | Glue stick, or double-sided tape | Glue sticks hold less weight |
| Glue dots | Tape or a glue stick | Tape shows on the picture |
| Cardstock | Cereal box, paper plate, thick envelope | Thin boxes bend |
| Paintbrush | Sponge, cotton bud, fingers | Fingers need washing |
| Watercolor paints | Diluted food color on paper | Food color stains |
| Scissors | Tearing paper | Tear lines are less tidy, which is fine |
| Buttons | Large bottle caps, paper circles, felt shapes | Caps are heavier |
| Yarn | Strips of old cloth or string | Less stretch |
| Beads | Pasta shapes for a sensory bracelet (adult only; not for mouthing) | Choking risk; see CNT-711 |
| Plant pot | Yoghurt pot, tin with holes, box lined with plastic | Drainage |
| Rice shaker | Dried beans in a sealed tin | Small parts; seal well |

- **Rules:** a substitution is listed only if it has been tested; a substitution that adds a safety risk carries its own safety line; no substitution asks someone to buy something.
- **Acceptance:** every MAKE card has a materials list and at least two substitutions; the substitution table is reviewed each year.

## 5. Adaptations that apply to every category (CNT-721)

- **Priority P0 · Phase S1 · Status partial** (the cards have variation blocks; this is the shared list the variation writers use).
- **Low dexterity and arthritis:** larger grips and handles; pre-cut and pre-folded; adhesive dots or tape in place of a bottle of glue; a clamp or non-slip mat; tools that open with a palm.
- **Seated:** all MAKE cards state whether they work seated. "Seated" is a default test, not a variation.
- **Low vision:** high contrast between materials; large print on instructions; one color choice; a light box or lamp; a finished sample visible.
- **One-handed:** tape or a clamp holds the paper; a tray with raised edges; a base that stands alone; a pre-loaded brush or stamp.
- **Less mess:** a tray or a cloth; washable materials; a drop cloth; a mess-free tool (sponge, dry collage); a clear stop point.
- **Less sensory:** fewer colors and textures; quieter tools; a plain tray; no shaving cream or wet sand.
- **Nonverbal:** hold up two options and watch for the reach or look; pointing and nodding count as answers.

## 6. Safety notes by risk (CNT-722)

- **Choking:** small parts, beads, buttons, dried beans, seeds, paper pieces. Not for under-threes; keep loose pieces in trays; count in and out.
- **Sharp tools:** scissors (blunt for young children), craft knives (adult only), bone folders, staplers. Keep one tool at a time on the table.
- **Heat:** hot glue (adult only, with a burn warning), heat-set transfers, lanterns (LED only).
- **Chemicals:** non-toxic and labelled materials; no solvents, spray paint or epoxy indoors; check skin reactions for shaving cream and food color.
- **Allergens:** wheat flour in dough, latex in some gloves, pollen and plant sap, dyes.
- **Outdoors:** pick nothing protected; never taste unknown plants; ticks, sun and insect bites after nature sessions.
- **Mouthing:** the default for any participant who mouths objects is no small parts.

## 8. Beating the platforms that already rank (CNT-740)

- **Priority P0 · Phase S1 · Status new.**
- **Honest position:** Pinterest (661M global MAU, Q3 2026, per [R1 §2](research/01-craft-diy-platforms.md#2-landscape-table)), YouTube craft channels, Instructables, wikiHow, The Spruce and retailer project libraries have far more volume. We will not match that. We do not need to.
- **Where we are different, and what we will do:**
  - **Fit, not inspiration.** Every card says who it suits, what it needs, and what to change, and results are filtered on time, materials, energy and setting. Pinterest's pins start with an image; ours start with "can I do this now?" ([R1 §6, gap 1](research/01-craft-diy-platforms.md#6-gaps-no-one-serves-well-our-openings)).
  - **Adaptation for ability and energy.** Seated, low-dexterity, low-vision and less-mess versions are required fields in the variation block, not add-ons.
  - **Free, printable, no account.** A one-page print card with no sign-up ([R1 §6, gap 3](research/01-craft-diy-platforms.md#6-gaps-no-one-serves-well-our-openings)).
  - **"It is fine to stop."** Every stop note is written as a real option, not a sign of failure ([R1 §6, gap 5](research/01-craft-diy-platforms.md#6-gaps-no-one-serves-well-our-openings)).
  - **Honest safety.** Choking, heat and allergen lines sit beside the materials, not in a footer.
  - **Substitutions.** Tested swaps for what is not in the house ([CNT-720](#4-materials-you-already-have-and-substitutions-cnt-720)).
  - **Tested by people.** Each card carries a `testedWith` record and no card is marked reviewed unless a named person reviewed it.
- **What we will not copy:** ad-funded feeds; pins as a paid placement surface; paid kit upsells in the body of a card; "therapeutic" or "brain-boosting" claims ([R1 §7](research/01-craft-diy-platforms.md#7-anti-patterns-and-risks-to-avoid)); AI-generated steps or images presented as tested.
- **Measurement:** start rate per card, prints and saves per card, returns to the same card by a household, and the share of cards with a seated or low-dexterity version. Defined in [17](17-metrics-and-evidence.md).

## Open questions

- Which MAKE clusters have measured demand in the target geography? R5 ranks are judgment, not measurement, and the first Keyword Planner export decides the S2 order.
- Is the beads category (CNT-711) worth keeping at all, given the choking risk and the adult-only rule? It may be better as an adult-only category with no under-threes.
- Who reviews the seasonal and cultural cards (CNT-713)? We need named reviewers from the traditions, not only translators ([13](13-localization.md)).
- Do the seated and low-dexterity defaults need an occupational therapist's review before the S2 cluster for arthritis goes live ([R5 rank 2](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#6-ranked-content-clusters))?
- Should "stash" (what I have) be a web filter, an app feature, or both? Owned by [03](03-features-web.md) and [04](04-features-app.md).
