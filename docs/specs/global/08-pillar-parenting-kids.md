# 08 · GROW: parenting, kids and family

GROW is the pillar for children and the adults who share time with them: parents, grandparents, carers, teachers and older siblings. It answers "my four-year-old, my grandmother, twenty minutes, nothing bought, what do we do?" It is also the pillar with the strictest privacy and safety rules, because the audience includes children and the content is read by adults who are often tired and short of time. For that reason GROW is gated. Child-facing content does not start until S3, when its own evidence and its privacy design are in place. Adult-facing, intergenerational content can start earlier. The content types, quality bar and phase targets are in [06](06-content-library.md); the MAKE overlap is in [07](07-pillar-crafting.md).

## 1. Why GROW is gated

- **Demand is broad and crowded.** R5 ranks "rainy day activities for kids" and "indoor activities for families" at the top of the list by demand, but with very high competition. Their opening is low on its own ([R5 §6, rank 13](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#6-ranked-content-clusters)).
- **Crafts for kids is the lowest-opening cluster.** "Easy crafts for kids" and "crafts for kids at home" rank 15 and are "gated behind the children's privacy work" ([R5 rank 15](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#6-ranked-content-clusters)).
- **Sensory activities for children are gated by evidence and design.** Rank 12 needs "COPPA-safe design and no child accounts" and is held by Tier C ([R5 rank 12](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#6-ranked-content-clusters)).
- **VISION says so.** Stage 3 expands "by adjacency, not by enthusiasm": a new audience such as "sensory-friendly child crafts" is tested only after its own search-gap and usability evidence ([VISION, stage 3](../../../VISION.md#product-stages-with-evidence-gates)).
- **The legal bar is higher.** The US amended COPPA Rule and the UK Age Appropriate Design Code apply to a site like ours (see §9 and [R5 §5.1](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#51-childrens-privacy)).

**Gate for child-facing GROW content (S3):** all of §10 is met, not just one item.

## 2. Age bands

Each band describes what a child is typically doing, in plain language. These are play descriptions, not developmental claims. We do not promise that an activity "builds" a skill (R2 §7).

| Band | Typical play | Supervision default | Size and choking rule | Example families |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 (split into 0–12 months and 12–24 months when content exists) | Exploring with mouth and hands; repetition; face-to-face; reaching and grasping | Adult within arm's reach at all times; never alone with water | Nothing that fits inside the small-parts cylinder (about 32 mm across); no balloons; no cords or strings | Sensory, music and movement, calm-down, travel |
| 2–3 | Stacking, pouring, simple matching, pretend with objects | Adult hands-on | Same as above; no beads, buttons or small craft pieces | Sensory, music and movement, pretend, outdoor |
| 3–5 | Longer make-and-do play; pretend with roles; blunt scissors; drawing | Adult nearby; hands-on for water and heat | Small parts still a risk for under-fives; check each material | Art and craft, pretend, outdoor, early literacy and numeracy |
| 5–7 | Making with some help; following picture steps; reading single words | Adult nearby | Standard hazard checks | Art and craft, science-ish making, rainy day |
| 7–10 | Independent making; reading instructions; rule-based games | Adult nearby, co-playing | Standard hazard checks | Science-ish making, outdoor, early literacy and numeracy |
| 10–12 | Multi-step projects; reading and writing; teaching a sibling | Adult available; child leads | Standard hazard checks | Science-ish making, art and craft, screen-free swaps |
| Teens (13+) | Choosing and leading; own space; social making | Adult available, not present | Standard hazard checks | Art and craft, music, science-ish making, leading for younger children |
| Mixed ages | One activity with roles for two or more bands | Adult takes the most supervision for the youngest | Use the youngest band's rules | All families, with roles (§6) |

**Rules for the age data:**

- The band is a content choice made in the browser. It is not stored, not sent to analytics, and never asked for as a child's personal data.
- Every GROW card states its band in words and says "not for under 3" where small parts or choking risk applies.
- Bands are never a diagnosis filter. "For 5–7" is about play, not ability.

## 3. Activity families

Eleven families. Each has example activities, the adult's role (see §6), the bands it suits and its safety notes. Examples are candidates for cards; each becomes a canonical card in [06 CNT-001](06-content-library.md#cnt-001--activity-card-canonical) before it is published.

### CNT-810 · Sensory play

Priority P1 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** warm and cool bowls (water checked by an adult); a fabric feely bag with objects too large for the cylinder; bubble-wrap stomping; a sensory bottle (sealed plastic, glitter and water, glue-sealed cap; not for under-3s); a dry-bean tray for 5+ only, with an adult.
- **Bands:** 0–2 for the water and fabric; 2–5 for the bottle with supervision.
- **Adult role:** narrate textures, offer two choices, watch for overload and end early.
- **Safety:** no small parts for under-3s; no edible play (flour, cloud dough and salt dough are not for mouthing); water supervised within arm's reach; sensory bottles sealed.

### CNT-811 · Art and craft (GROW versions)

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** finger painting on a window (washable, non-toxic); torn-paper collage (3+); a cardboard robot (5+, adult cuts); printing with a sponge or potato (adult cuts); make a family tree of hands (exists, in the library).
- **Bands:** 0–2 for finger paint on a window; 3–10 for most.
- **Adult role:** set up the tray, then step back; ask "what is this part?" rather than "what is it?"
- **Safety:** see [07 §6](07-pillar-crafting.md#6-safety-notes-by-risk-cnt-722); no glue or paint near mouths of under-threes; no craft knife for children.

### CNT-812 · Pretend play

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** a post office from cardboard boxes; a paper-money shop; a café on a picnic blanket; paper-bag puppets (3+); a vet clinic for soft toys; a family-history role-play with an older relative (see §7).
- **Bands:** 2–10 at most; mixed-age roles work well.
- **Adult role:** take a small role, then give it away; accept the child's rules.
- **Safety:** cardboard edges; small props (coins, tokens) are small parts.

### CNT-813 · Outdoor play

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** a leaf-sorting walk; puddle temperatures; chalk paths; a bird-spotting step; a mud kitchen with a bowl and a spoon; shadow tag.
- **Bands:** all bands; the youngest with adult in reach.
- **Adult role:** ask one question per stop ("what is the same?"); let the child lead the route.
- **Safety:** check for plants before tasting or touching (never taste); ticks and insect bites after; sun and water safety; keep to paths near water.

### CNT-814 · Music and movement

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** freeze dance; clap-back rhythms; a pot-lid band (see [07 CNT-716](07-pillar-crafting.md#cnt-716--music-making-with-household-items)); follow-the-leader actions; move like the weather (wind, rain, sun).
- **Bands:** all bands; movement is useful for the youngest and for children who prefer to move while they listen.
- **Adult role:** join in and lead for one song, then swap.
- **Safety:** no glass or bottles as instruments; keep volume moderate; keep space clear of furniture corners.

### CNT-815 · Early literacy and numeracy through play

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** sort by color and count to ten; an alphabet hunt in one room; a shopping-list game with picture cards; a story box with three objects; measure a shoe with paper strips; stack and count bricks.
- **Bands:** 2–7 mostly; the older children can write the list.
- **Adult role:** ask the question, then wait for the answer; use the child's words back to them.
- **Language:** cards are written in plain language and translated under [13](13-localization.md). No claim is made that an activity improves reading or maths scores.
- **Safety:** small objects (counters, letters, bricks) are small parts for under-threes.

### CNT-816 · Science-ish making

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** a sink-or-float boat; a paper-towel color-spread with washable markers; shadow play with a torch; a balloon rocket (adult-led; balloons kept away from under-threes); a gentle baking-soda and vinegar fizz in a tray (eyes covered, adult-led).
- **Bands:** 5–12 most; 3–5 with adult-led steps only.
- **Adult role:** ask "what do you think will happen?" and then "what happened?"; no right answers.
- **Safety:** eye protection for fizzing; balloons are a choking hazard for young children; no batteries (button batteries are never used in any card); magnets (strong magnets are a serious ingestion risk) are not used.

### CNT-817 · Calm-down activities

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** tear paper slowly into strips; bubble breathing with a straw in a cup of water (supervised); color together with quiet music; a sensory bottle for holding (see CNT-810); a hand massage with lotion (adult and child).
- **Bands:** 2–12; calm activities are offered as options, never as punishment.
- **Adult role:** offer, do not prescribe; the child can say no; stop when the child stops.
- **Language:** "calm-down" and "wind down" are fine as everyday words. No "treats anxiety", "regulates", "therapy" or similar (VISION guardrails; [10](10-pillar-creative-wellbeing.md)).
- **Safety:** straws in cups only with supervision; no lotion near eyes.

### CNT-818 · Rainy-day and indoor

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** an indoor treasure hunt with a picture list; a den made from sheets and chairs; a cardboard-box theatre; indoor bowling with plastic bottles and a ball; a puppet show.
- **Bands:** 3–10 mostly; the den is a family-size activity.
- **Adult role:** build the first part, then let the child change it.
- **Safety:** chairs and sheets: keep den structures stable and low; no cords; small objects out of reach of under-threes.

### CNT-819 · Travel and waiting-room

Priority P2 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** I spy with colors and shapes; a paper figure that spins; drawing in a notebook on a clipboard; find-the-letter bingo on a card; a sticker map of the journey (non-toxic stickers; small parts for under-threes).
- **Bands:** 2–10.
- **Adult role:** keep the bag ready; start the game when the wait begins, not when it is already tense.
- **Safety:** pen caps, stickers and small pieces are small parts; keep out of the mouth; no car-seat unbuckling for play.

### CNT-820 · Screen-free swaps

Priority P1 · Phase S3

- **Examples:** "make the show" with paper puppets instead of watching one; card-based story making instead of a tablet game; a video call with a grandparent where both play the same card game.
- **Bands:** all bands; the swap is always offered as an option, not a rule.
- **Adult role:** join; do not police screens.
- **Screen-time framing:** the public site may recommend off-screen activities. Specific limits (the WHO and AAP guidance R2 summarizes) must be checked against the primary documents before any number is printed, because R2 records that its sources are secondary summaries ([R2 §5](research/02-parenting-kids-platforms.md#5-games-and-interactive-patterns)).

## 4. Adaptations for sensory needs and different ways of playing (CNT-830)

- **Priority P1 · Phase S3 (for child-facing content) · Status new.**
- **Why:** the MISSION names "parents and caregivers seeking adaptable sensory-friendly and creative activities for children". R2 finds that sensory guidance is written for diagnosed children and for intervention, and that families who simply want low-mess, low-noise, texture-flexible play have little (R2 §6, gap 3).
- **Principle:** describe the activity and the choices, never the child. Do not label an activity for a diagnosis or a group of people. Do not use "for autistic children" or similar titles ([VISION](../../../VISION.md)). Use "sensory-friendly", "low-mess", "quiet" and "choose your level".
- **Choose-your-level options, on every family card where they apply:**
  - **Texture:** dry, wet, or tools only; a glove or a spoon if touch is hard.
  - **Noise:** quiet version; a version with a shaker that the child controls.
  - **Predictability:** a three-picture sequence for the steps, shown in the card and printable.
  - **Space:** floor, table, standing, or a cushion; a choice of where to sit.
  - **Breaks:** built-in stop points; the stop note is part of the card.
  - **Movement:** it is fine to rock, tap, walk around or stand while doing the activity.
  - **Participation:** a child can watch or hold the materials instead of joining. Watching counts.
- **Acceptance:** each choose-your-level option has been tested with a parent or carer who uses it; no card implies that a child has a condition.

## 5. Safety (CNT-850)

- **Priority P0 · Phase S1 · Status partial** (the existing cards show safety lines; GROW needs its own rules before any child card is published).
- **Choking:** the small-parts cylinder (about 32 mm across) is the test. Anything that fits inside it is not for under-threes. US small-parts rules restrict such items for toys for under-threes and set labelling duties for older ages. Check the current text on eCFR before writing any labelling rule ([R2 §4](research/02-parenting-kids-platforms.md#4-content-patterns)); secondary sources disagree on the age ranges.
- **Supervision:** under-threes hands-on; under-fives within arm's reach for water, heat and sharp tools; older children with an adult nearby.
- **Water:** arm's reach for under-fives. This is general guidance; confirm with the local authority before publishing a rule.
- **Allergens:** no nuts or peanuts in play; no edible play in the core library (flour, egg, dairy and wheat in dough are allergens); no food-based sensory play without a food-safety review.
- **Cords and strings:** yarn loops and cords around the neck; keep long cords away from under-threes.
- **Magnets and batteries:** no strong magnets; no button or coin batteries in any card.
- **Plastic bags and balloons:** suffocation and choking; not for under-threes; balloons adult-led for older children.
- **Sun and heat:** outdoor cards carry sun, water and tick lines.
- **Mouthing:** the default for any child who mouths objects is no small parts, whatever the age band.

## 6. Co-play roles: what the adult does (CNT-840)

- **Priority P1 · Phase S3 (for child-facing content) · Status new.**
- **Why:** kids' platforms put the adult role second (R2 §6, gap 6). In GROW the adult role is on the same card, first.
- **Rule:** every GROW card has a "who does what" line: the child's part, the adult's part, and where an older sibling or grandparent fits. Teen roles are "lead" or "teach", not "help".

| Family | What the adult does | What the child does | Where others fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory | Narrates textures; offers two choices; watches for overload | Explores and chooses | Older sibling sets up |
| Art and craft | Sets up; asks "what is this part?" | Makes | Grandparent works alongside on their own piece |
| Pretend | Takes a small role, then gives it away | Directs the play | Older child plays the shopkeeper |
| Outdoor | Asks one question per stop | Leads the route | Teen chooses the route |
| Music | Joins for one song, then swaps | Chooses the rhythm | Grandparent sings |
| Early literacy and numeracy | Asks, then waits | Answers and counts | Older child checks |
| Science-ish | Asks "what do you think?" | Predicts and tests | Teen runs the test |
| Calm-down | Offers, does not prescribe | Accepts or declines | Grandparent reads quietly |
| Rainy day | Builds the first part | Changes it | Siblings build together |
| Travel | Keeps the bag ready; starts the game | Plays | Other passengers join |
| Screen-free | Joins in | Chooses | Remote grandparent on a call |

## 7. Intergenerational and remote versions (CNT-870)

- **Priority P1 · Phase S2 for adult-facing intergenerational content; S3 for child-facing · Status partial** (one grandparent–grandchild hub exists).
- **Why:** "grandparent and grandchild activities" is R5 rank 5 and "shared-moment framing and adaptation for the older person" is the opening ([R5 rank 5](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#6-ranked-content-clusters)). The adult-facing pages can start earlier because the adult is the reader and the adult's adaptation is the content.
- **Shared, in person:** a two-role card; the older person's adaptation sits beside the child's step. The shared card is adult-facing in S2 and adds child-facing steps in S3.
- **Remote, together:**
  - Print the same card for both places; print-first, screen second.
  - One camera, a stand, and a clear space; the adult on the call keeps an eye on the child.
  - Pause-and-show moments: "hold up your bead" or "show me your leaf".
  - The session ends on the card's stop note.
  - No recording by us. No chat feature for children. No photos shared by us of child participants.
  - For under-eights, a parent or carer is present on the call.
- **Remote with a grandparent who is not confident with technology:** a printed card and a phone call in the same way, with no account needed.

## 8. School and early-years educator program (CNT-860)

- **Priority P2 · Phase S3 · Status new** (pilot in S2 for the adult side; see [06 CNT-014](06-content-library.md#cnt-014--educator-and-care-setting-program)).
- **What:** a term of 10-, 20- and 30-minute sessions; class-size and group variations; a staff summary with setup and clean-up time; a family note to take home that contains no child data; a permission template the school uses with its own families.
- **Rules:** screen-free by default; no child accounts, logins or child-facing data collection; no classroom use of the public website's analytics; partner names shown only with written agreement; no curriculum or outcome claim unless a credentialed early-years educator has reviewed the mapping against the framework named. Wording is "playful practice", never "skill-building" or "achievement".
- **Acceptance:** a teacher can run one session from the printed card in under five minutes of preparation; a reviewer from early years is named on each program page.

## 9. Privacy and design constraints (CNT-880)

- **Priority P0 · Phase S1 for the rules; applies to every GROW page · Status new.**
- **Law and rules (summary; counsel to confirm):**
  - **US COPPA (amended rule):** full compliance required from 22 April 2026. Separate verifiable parental consent is needed before disclosing children's personal information to third parties, including for targeted advertising. The FTC's February 2026 policy statement is narrow relief, not a safe harbour ([R5 §5.1](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#51-childrens-privacy)).
  - **UK Age Appropriate Design Code:** fifteen standards, statutory, for services likely to be accessed by under-18s. Relevant standards include best interests of the child, high privacy by default, data minimization, detrimental use and no nudges ([R5 §5.1](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#51-childrens-privacy)). Verify the full list against the ICO text before quoting it.
  - **YouTube made-for-kids:** every child-directed video is labelled accurately ([R5 §5.1](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#51-childrens-privacy)).
  - **EU and UK online-safety duties** for minors may apply to user features. Check with counsel ([R5 §5.5](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#55-user-generated-content-and-moderation)).
- **Design rules for GROW pages:**
  - No ads, no ad tech, no third-party trackers on any page a child can reach or that is child-directed.
  - No accounts for children; no collection of persistent identifiers from children.
  - No child's name, face or school in any public asset without a written parental release (see [06 §4](06-content-library.md#4-media-rights-and-provenance)).
  - No child comments, uploads or profiles (see [11](11-community-ugc.md)); adults can share a completed activity without a child's identity.
  - No "share with friends", push-notification or email prompts on child-directed pages.
  - Child-directed determination (cartoon characters, music, visual style, child-oriented subject) is decided by counsel before a child-facing design is built. This is an open question below.
  - Age-band selection is a browser-side choice and is not sent to analytics.
- **Acceptance:** an automated check confirms no third-party scripts load on GROW child-facing pages; a counsel memo is filed before S3.

## 10. Gate and targets (CNT-890)

- **Priority P0 · Phase S3 gate · Status new.**
- **Gate for child-facing GROW content:** all of the following.
  1. The `ages` field and the `pillars` field are added to the schema ([06 CNT-001](06-content-library.md#cnt-001--activity-card-canonical)).
  2. Counsel has signed off the design rules in §9, including the child-directed determination.
  3. A search gap is measured for the specific child-facing queries in R5 (ranks 12 and 15) with a US-targeted export and a SERP audit ([R5 §7](research/05-search-scale-trust.md#7-next-measurements-in-order)).
  4. Usability sessions with parents and carers (not children) show that a visitor can find and start an age-appropriate card unaided ([VISION stage 1 and 3](../../../VISION.md#product-stages-with-evidence-gates)).
  5. An early-years or child-development educator and an occupational therapist (for sensory adaptations) have reviewed the family cards.
  6. Safety checks in §5 are recorded on every card.
- **Adult-facing intergenerational content** (§7) does not wait for the gate, provided it has no child-facing data collection and no child-directed design.
- **Targets (from [06 §6](06-content-library.md#6-target-library-sizes-by-phase)):** GROW canonical cards: 0 at S1, 0 at S2 (intergenerational adult-facing pages excluded), 30 at S3, 80 at S4. These are targets, not commitments.

## Open questions

- Counsel: is any GROW design child-directed under the COPPA factors and the UK Children's Code? That decides whether cartoon characters, music or child-oriented styling is allowed.
- Which child-development reviewer is named for the early-years cards, and is there a framework they will map to?
- What is the correct current text of the US small-parts rule for each age band? R2 flags disagreement; this must be checked on eCFR before labelling rules are written.
- Does the site need a parent-only area for family content, or is every GROW page public and adult-facing?
- The age-band filter stays in the browser. Confirm with the platform owner that no server log captures it ([15](15-platform-data-architecture.md)).
- Is "sensory-friendly" acceptable to the occupational therapy reviewers as a term, given the MISSION's avoidance of diagnosis framing?
