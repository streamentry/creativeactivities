# 01. Positioning and audiences

This file says which question we own, who asks it, in what situations, and how we differ from the leaders. It is the reference that every other file uses to decide whether something belongs. The question is not "what is creative?" but "what can we do together, right now, that this particular person can enjoy?" Everything here follows from [MISSION](../../../MISSION.md) and [VISION](../../../VISION.md); where this file is more specific, it is a proposal to be tested, not a fact.

## 1. The question we own

> **What can we do together, right now, that this particular person can enjoy?**

The biggest sites each own one question at global scale. Wikipedia answers "what is it?". wikiHow answers "how do I?" in task-shaped steps [R5 §2.1]. Pinterest answers "what could I make?" with a feed of saved ideas and ads [R1 §2]. Duolingo and Khan Academy answer "how do I learn this a little each day?" and "how do I learn this for free?" [README].

The question above is not yet owned by anyone. It is narrower than "ideas for crafts" and more specific than "activities for seniors". It has four parts, and an answer owns the question only if it meets all four:

1. **Right now.** It is something a person can start in about a minute, with what is in the room [MISSION, "Startable, not scrollable"].
2. **Together.** It involves at least one other person, or is built to be shared, printed or sent [VISION, "Share the moment"]. Solo use is allowed, but it is not the default.
3. **This particular person.** It fits energy, mobility, senses, communication and interests, not only an age group [MISSION, "Fit the moment, not the demographic"].
4. **Enjoyable, not treated.** The person can stop at any point. We never promise a treatment outcome [MISSION, "Evidence and humility"].

A competing answer that is a long list of ideas, a how-to guide with no adaptation, or a clinical exercise plan does not meet the test. We will measure whether we own the question with the metrics in [17](17-metrics-and-evidence.md), not with traffic.

## 2. Audiences

There are two kinds of user. **Choosers** search, pick and print. **Participants** do the activity. The chooser is usually the first visitor. The participant's preferences, dignity and right to stop come first when they conflict [MISSION, "Primary participant"].

| Audience | Role | Pillars | Typical moment | Main friction today | What success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Parents** of toddlers and school-age children | Chooser; often also participant | GROW, PLAY | A rainy afternoon, bedtime wind-down, a sibling fight | Ideas need shopping, too much setup, or assume one child at one age | An activity started in under five minutes with what is at home |
| **Grandparents** | Chooser and participant | TOGETHER, GROW, CARE | A visit, a video call, a school holiday | Ideas are written for a child or for a care setting; nothing for "both of us" | A shared activity both can join, at the grandparent's pace |
| **Adult children of older parents** (including parents living with dementia) | Chooser, usually a daughter or son | CARE, CALM | Weekend visits, a hard afternoon, a quiet evening | Generic lists assume memory or mobility the parent no longer has; care advice dominates | A short, gentle activity that the parent can enjoy, with a clear way to stop |
| **Professional carers and activity coordinators** (care homes, day centers, home care) | Chooser for groups | CARE, TOGETHER | Weekly program planning for mixed-ability groups | Planning takes hours; ideas do not scale to eight people with different needs | Printable weekly programs with group-level and one-to-one versions |
| **Teachers and early-years educators** | Chooser for classes | GROW | Twenty minutes before lunch, rainy days, mixed-age groups | Materials are not available; lesson plans are curriculum-bound | Low-prep, whole-class cards with supervision notes |
| **Librarians and community programmers** | Chooser for drop-in sessions | TOGETHER, MAKE | A weekly all-ages session with no registration | Need printable, free, inclusive activities with clear supervision needs | Printable packs and a facilitator note they can use without training |
| **Therapists and health professionals as referrers** (occupational therapists, speech and language therapists, music therapists, social prescribers) | Referrer only, not a user of our clinical claims | CARE, CALM | Between sessions, as a suggestion for a client | They need a handout that does not claim treatment | A printable card with a scope note; they recommend, we do not claim endorsement [R3 §2] |
| **Individuals for themselves** (older adults, people in bed or with low energy, people with low vision, people who want to make something) | Chooser and participant | MAKE, CALM, CARE | Alone, in bed, on a tired day | Crafts sites assume a table, two hands and good eyesight | A card they can read, hold and stop |
| **Children** (participants only) | Participant, never chooser or account holder | GROW, PLAY | With a parent, grandparent or teacher | Kids' apps push purchases and data collection | An activity an adult can start with them; no child account, no child data |

**Rules that apply to every audience:**

- Children are never choosers, account holders or data subjects in our analytics [R2 §7; TSA requirements in [14](14-trust-safety-accessibility-privacy.md)].
- Professionals referring to us do not get a separate clinical product. They get a handout and a scope note [R3 §4.2].
- Age, diagnosis and disability are context, never the whole personalization model [MISSION, "Who we serve"]. We never ask for a diagnosis to give a recommendation.

- Most situations are about **energy, mobility, senses, communication and time**, not age. Age appears only as context.
- Six rows (5, 6, 8, 10, 16, 35) involve a **second person whose needs differ**. These drive the group and intergenerational features in [03](03-features-web.md).
- Several need **no purchase and no screen** (rows 1, 17, 23, 29). They set the free-core rule.
- Rows 19, 21 and 30 touch **emotion, grief and care** and need a reviewer and a scope line. They are not self-guided "treatment" content [R3 §4.2–§4.3].

## 4. Jobs to be done

Each job is written as the chooser's goal, the struggle that stops them now, and the outcome that means the job is done.

1. **Find something that fits this moment.** When I have a few minutes and a specific person in mind, I want an activity that suits their energy and setting, so that I do not have to adapt a generic idea myself. *Today:* search returns lists written for someone else.
2. **Start without a shopping trip.** When I have only what is in the house, I want to know whether I can begin now, so that I do not postpone it. *Today:* many craft ideas list kits or specialist items.
3. **Adapt without becoming an expert.** When the usual version is too hard, too messy or too loud, I want a tested alternative, so that the person can still take part. *Today:* adaptations are rare, and when they exist they are buried or unexplained.
4. **Share one thing with two different people.** When I am with a child and an older relative, or a group with mixed ability, I want one activity with roles for each, so that nobody is left out. *Today:* intergenerational ideas are scattered across video and pin boards [R1 §6].
5. **Plan a week or a group session on paper.** When I run a care home, a class or a library session, I want printable sheets I can hand out, so that I spend time with people, not on preparation. *Today:* printable packs are often paid or teacher-facing [R2 §2].
6. **Know it is safe and that I can stop.** When I am unsure whether a material or a step is safe, or whether the person is ready, I want clear warnings and an easy exit, so that I do not push past their limits. *Today:* safety notes are missing or buried in long articles.
7. **Trust the advice.** When I read a page about a person with dementia or a child with additional needs, I want to know who wrote it, who checked it and when, so that I can decide how much to rely on it. *Today:* most sites show no reviewer and no date [R5 §2.2].

## 5. Competitive position

The table shows who wins each job today, where we can beat them, and where we will not compete. "Deliberately not" is a product decision, not a gap we hope to fill later.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Pinterest** [R1 §2, §7; R5 §4] | Discovery at scale, saves, visual planning. 661M MAU, Q3 2026 (company figure) | Activity fit (energy, setting, group, adaptation); no feed; a printable card, not a board | Volume of ideas and ad-funded feeds. We use Pinterest as a distribution channel, not a rival |
| **wikiHow** [R1 §3, §6; R5 §2.1] | Task-shaped how-to steps, broad topic coverage, SEO | Adaptations, safety flags, "fine to stop", reviewer and date on each card | Breadth of how-to topics. We do not publish "how to" articles outside the situations we serve |

| **Khan Academy Kids, PBS KIDS, CBeebies** [R2 §5] | Trust benchmark: free, no ads, no tracking of children | Match their trust standard; add the adult co-play role and off-screen cards | Learning games and literacy curriculum |

| **Tovertafel** [R4 §2, §5] | Group play on a projected table, care-home use | Free group versions of activities, with no hardware | Hardware and device subscriptions |
| **Lumosity, Elevate** [R4 §2, §5] | Brain-training scores and progress charts | Nothing. Cognitive outcome claims are rejected [R4 §5] | Scored brain training and any "keeps the brain sharp" claim |
| **NYT Games (Wordle, Connections)** [R4 §3] | One shared daily puzzle; spoiler-free share | A daily card with an archive and no missed-day penalty | Subscription bundles and loss-framed streaks |
| **Duolingo** [R4 §3, §7] | Streaks and habit loops | Collections and "tried" counts that never shrink | Streak shame and guilt notifications |
| **Solitaire, Jigsaw Planet, Chrome Music Lab** [R4 §3, §5] | Free, no-account play | Free play plus adaptation and print pairs | Ads in play |
| **Healthline, Khan Academy, Wikipedia** (trust layer) [R5 §2.1–§2.2] | Named reviewers, review dates, citation discipline, volunteer translation with review | Copy the trust layer at small scale; state partial review honestly | Medical advice. We do not give health guidance |

**Summary of the position.** We do not beat anyone on volume, on video, on kits, on brain claims or on clinical authority. We win on **fit to the situation**, **adaptation for the person in front of you**, **a printable, no-account, free card**, and **a visible trust layer**. Those are the claims the features in [03](03-features-web.md) must prove.

## 6. Positioning statements per pillar

Each statement names the chooser, the job, and what is different. Each pillar also has a "never say" list. Claims must stay within the scope line: creative, recreational, supportive, enjoyable. Pillars are in [README](README.md#pillars).

### MAKE (crafts, art, making)

**For** a person who wants to make something with their hands and has only what is at home, **MAKE** is the place to find a craft that fits their energy, grip, eyesight and space, **unlike** kit-led craft sites, because every activity starts from materials most homes already have and shows a tested easier version.

- *Proof we need:* adaptation notes on every card; a "without a kit" substitution list.
- *Never say:* "therapeutic", "healing", "Zentangle" as a verb [R3 §7], "improves dexterity".

### GROW (kids and parenting)

**For** a parent, grandparent or teacher with a child and twenty minutes, **GROW** gives an activity that works with the child's current mood and sensory limits, **unlike** age-stage kit programs, because the adult's role is written into the card and nothing is sold or tracked.

- *Proof we need:* co-play role on every child card; "not for under 3" flags where materials are small [R2 §4].
- *Never say:* "builds [skill]" without evidence [R2 §7]; "for autistic children" as a headline (use situations, not labels).

### CARE (older adults, dementia, bedside, caregivers)

**For** an adult who cares for someone at home, in bed or in a care setting, **CARE** is a short, gentle activity that the person can enjoy and stop at any moment, **unlike** care-advice sites and dementia apps that sell subscriptions or claim cognitive benefit, because it asks for enjoyment and dignity, not performance or memory.

- *Proof we need:* reviewer with credential and scope on every dementia-related card; "stop" note on every card; no quizzes or timers for participants [R4 §3].
- *Never say:* "slows dementia", "reduces agitation", "keeps the brain sharp", "care plan" [R4 §5; R5 §5.2].

### CALM (creative wellbeing)

**For** someone who wants to make something quietly, to feel calmer for a while or to notice how they feel, **CALM** offers creative activities presented honestly as recreation, with a clear line that they are not art therapy, **unlike** wellbeing apps that blur creative activity and clinical therapy or use streaks and scores [R3 §2, §7].

- *Proof we need:* the boundary page ([03](03-features-web.md)); reviewer for any page touching grief, trauma or mental health; crisis route on disclosure-inviting pages [R3 §4.4].
- *Never say:* "art therapy" for our activities; "reduces anxiety"; "proven"; "treats"; any mood score.

### PLAY (games, on screen and on paper)

**For** anyone who wants a game they can share or play alone, with or without a screen, **PLAY** offers free games with no account, no ads, adjustable difficulty and a printable partner for each, **unlike** brain-training and subscription games, because the game is a shared moment and not a score.

- *Proof we need:* difficulty controls; print pairs for each game [R4 §6]; no scores shown as failure.
- *Never say:* "brain training", "cognitive", "memory improvement".

### TOGETHER (intergenerational and group lens)

**For** two or more people with different abilities, ages or distances, **TOGETHER** gives one activity with a role for each person and a version for groups, **unlike** single-audience sites, because the card is built around the shared moment and both people's comfort.

- *Proof we need:* group and remote versions on at least one card per pillar; roles written for each person [R1 §6; R4 §6].
- *Never say:* "family-friendly" as a substitute for specific roles; any assumption about one holiday or household structure [MISSION, "Cultural openness"].

## Open questions

1. **Which situations go first in S1?** VISION's wedge names four clusters: dementia activities at home, simple crafts for limited dexterity, seated and bedside activities, and parent–grandparent–child activities. Rows 2, 4, 10 and 17 test the first two; should S1 cover only those, or also row 5?
2. **Does the chooser/participant split need its own page?** Parents and older adults use the finder in different ways. Test before splitting navigation.
3. **How do we describe a dementia situation without a diagnosis field?** Row 2 and row 10 use "father with dementia" and "late-stage dementia" as context. Confirm wording with the first credentialed reviewer [README](README.md#open-questions).
4. **Which professions get a referrer page?** Occupational therapists, speech and language therapists and social prescribers are the candidates [R3 §4.5]. Who reviews the scope note?
5. **Which languages match the situations in rows 24 and 31?** Language order is decided in [13](13-localization.md) and needs search and review evidence.
