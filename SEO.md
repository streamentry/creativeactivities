# CreativeActivities.org — SEO & GEO Strategy

**Status:** Working execution specification, version 1.0  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-13  
**Scope:** Organic web search, image search, AI-powered discovery (GEO/AEO), international search, and web-to-app acquisition  
**Companion documents:** [MISSION.md](MISSION.md), [VISION.md](VISION.md), [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md)

> **Win a real-world moment, not a ranking.** Help a person find an activity that genuinely fits someone they care for. Search is how people discover us; the successful activity is why we deserve to be found.

---

## 0. Executive decision

**The positioning:** The most practical, accessible source of **creative and meaningful activities for people you care for**—starting with family caregivers of older adults, especially dementia-related situations, then expanding only where demand and real-world usability justify it.

**The acquisition model:** An underserved situation generates a specific search → an exceptionally useful and indexable guide answers it → the visitor starts, prints, saves, or shares an activity → actual use and feedback improve the activity → original material earns recommendations and independent links → stronger search presence. Recommend a focused iOS app only when it adds value beyond the web.

**The defensible asset is not articles.** It is the reusable, evidence-informed **Activity Fit Library**: original instructions and media; tested adaptations for time, energy, mobility, dexterity, vision, sensory preference and setting; culturally useful substitutions; clear safety boundaries; and feedback on what worked in practice.

**Order of operations:**

1. Own one coherent job: *“What can I do with my older parent today?”*
2. Become useful for 3–5 high-intent search clusters, not 100 broad topics.
3. Turn each strong guide into one canonical activity, one excellent printable card, and selective images/video.
4. Establish quality and relevance before adding audiences or languages.
5. Use search data and real caregivers to decide expansion, not a publishing quota.

**What we will not do:** Build a diagnosis-by-age-by-country page factory; claim crafts are clinical therapy; invent experts, reviews or outcomes; use artificial backlinks or fake AI citations; or sacrifice a usable answer to force an install.

### Success definition

- **North star:** Monthly *reported activities started* in the real world, with qualitative checks on appropriateness and enjoyment. This is a useful proxy, not a clinical outcome.
- **Growth:** Qualified nonbranded organic sessions from relevant queries, not raw traffic from words such as `art`, `therapy`, or `activities` alone.
- **Distribution:** Prints, saves, shares, repeat useful visits, earned links, and web-to-app installs where the app creates distinct value.
- **Quality guardrails:** Zero known serious uncorrected safety issues; honest sourcing; privacy; accessibility; proportion of original and verified content.

## 1. Evidence ledger: what is known, and what is not

### Known starting facts

- Founder-reported baseline: an existing dementia activity iOS app earns roughly **400 organic downloads per month**, predominantly through App Store Search, despite few ratings. Treat this as an **ASO distribution signal**. It does **not** establish equivalent Google web search demand, nor guarantee traffic to a new domain.
- The user-provided Google Keyword Planner CSV covers **November 2025–October 2026**. Its geography, language targeting and network settings are not established from the export alone. The file reports broad head terms and a small number of niche art-therapy phrases; it is not a validated worldwide demand map.
- Examples present in the export include `art therapy activities for seniors` at **50 average monthly searches** and `art therapy activities for adults` at **500**, *under that export's unknown targeting*. The apparent precision of estimates should not be mistaken for exact demand.
- Relevant exact phrases such as `dementia activities`, `activities for seniors`, `autism sensory activities`, and `activities for bedridden elderly` are **not present as direct entries** in the provided dataset. **Do not invent volumes, keyword difficulty, or worldwide totals for them.**
- Keyword Planner's **Competition** is competition for Google Ads placements, **not organic SEO difficulty**. App Store Search demand is another separate measurement.

### Unknowns to resolve before prioritizing

- Search volume by target *country × language* for each exact cluster and variants.
- The actual organic top 10: who ranks, why, what they omit, and whether small sites can compete.
- Which queries have featured/AI answers, image packs, videos, forums, or highly authoritative medical publishers; which still lead to clicks.
- Search-to-activity conversion rate and whether web content is preferable to an app.
- Translation cost, real demand by locale, and ability to review adaptations safely.

**Evidence rule:** Every keyword score keeps its **data source, locale, period, measurement method, confidence and last-check date**. A blank cell is unknown—not zero.

## 2. The positioning wedge: underserved moments, not demographic stereotypes

People do not search for a content vertical; they search because something is difficult right now.

| Searcher and situation | Likely question | Fit the site should solve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adult child caring for a parent with dementia | “What can we do together for 15 minutes?” | Familiar, low-pressure, adaptable activity without a cognitive-performance promise |
| Family caregiver of an older adult with limited mobility | “What activities can Dad do sitting down?” | Seated or bedside options with realistic setup and safety considerations |
| Relative looking for low-mess ideas | “Easy crafts for elderly parents at home” | Simple household materials, large-print steps, no specialist supplies |
| Parent of an autistic child | “Sensory activities without messy textures” | Options based on stated sensory preferences, choice and consent, not assumptions about all autistic children |
| Grandparent and grandchild | “What can we make together?” | Two-generation participation, adjustable steps, low cost |
| Family preparing for a visit | “Printable activities for seniors” | One-page cards that work offline, with a short materials list |

**Entry wedge:** Older-adult family caregiving, particularly creative, seated, familiar, and low-mess activities. **Potential adjacencies:** Grandparent–child creative activities; sensory-friendly children’s activities; inclusive activities for limited mobility. Every new segment must pass its own evidence gate.

**Strategic warning:** `therapy` is a dangerous vanity keyword. *Art therapy* generally refers to a professional therapeutic discipline. We may discuss it accurately with qualified sourcing, but do not market general craft ideas as clinical treatment. Prefer `activities`, `art activities`, `crafts`, `sensory play`, `creative activities`, or `reminiscence activities` where these match the page.

## 3. Search demand discovery and competitive intelligence

### 3.1 Keyword research protocol

Work in one comparable market at a time, starting with **English-language US search** as a practical measurement baseline, **not** because the site excludes anyone else. Run equivalent checks for other locales after identifying the wedge.

1. **Seed with actual jobs:** audience + activity + constraint + setting + outcome. E.g. `dementia + art activities + easy + at home`.
2. **Collect actual query language:** Google Search Console once live; Google Keyword Planner; Google Trends (relative only); Bing Webmaster Tools; search autosuggest and related questions; community discussions; caregiver interviews; competitor navigation and reviews. Label third-party estimates as estimates.
3. **Validate exact clusters:** match type, country, language, 12-month range, search network, seasonality and intent. Record both exact expressions and natural synonyms; avoid false precision from rounded volumes.
4. **Manually inspect 10 relevant results** for each leading query, using the same location and device assumptions. Score their *usefulness*, not just domain authority.
5. **Identify an information-to-action gap:** the search results show lists or generic AI summaries, but not suitable adaptations, materials, clear steps, printability, or evidence of actual use.
6. **Test before writing a series:** one high-quality guide + 3–5 original activities + one printable. Observe users; check indexation and impressions. Expand only if the gap is real.

### 3.2 Candidate clusters to validate (not measured keyword winners)

**Tier A — First wedge: older adults and dementia**

- `dementia activities`; `activities for dementia patients`; `dementia art activities`
- `activities for seniors`; `activities for elderly parents`; `senior activities at home`
- `arts and crafts for seniors`; `easy art activities for seniors`
- `seated activities for seniors`; `activities for elderly with limited mobility`
- `activities for bedridden elderly`; `bedside activities for seniors`
- `reminiscence activities`; `reminiscence activities for dementia`
- `printable activities for seniors`; `easy crafts for seniors with arthritis`

**Tier B — Direct adjacencies**

- `grandparent and grandchild activities`; `crafts for grandparents and grandchildren`
- `intergenerational activities`; `simple crafts for adults with limited mobility`
- `music activities for seniors`; `storytelling activities for elderly parents`

**Tier C — Test separately before entering children’s categories**

- `sensory activities for autistic children`; `low mess sensory activities`
- `sensory friendly crafts`; `fine motor activities for kids`
- `creative activities for kids at home`; `quiet activities for children`

**Tier D — Sensitive or uncertain**

- `art therapy activities for dementia`; `stroke recovery art activities`; `Parkinson's activities`
- These may involve medical implications, rehabilitation instructions, or a better-funded, professionally reviewed competitive set. Require stronger review and a clear nonclinical scope.

### 3.3 Opportunity scorecard (a decision tool, not a magical SEO score)

For each cluster, give each factor **1–5 with a written reason and source**:

| Factor | What a high score means |
| --- | --- |
| Demand evidence | Verified relevant searches in a defined locale; repeated real-user phrasing |
| SERP weakness | Existing results are off-target, outdated, inaccessible, or impractical |
| Original advantage | We can supply tested steps, unique media, useful adaptation, or real printables |
| Ease to serve safely | We can review accuracy and accessibility without unearned medical claims |
| Organic acquisition fit | Realistic ability to win qualified impressions and clicks, not only instant-answer traffic |
| Expansion value | One original activity can responsibly support several distinct, nonduplicated needs |

Then separately record **research/production effort**, **legal/clinical risk**, **expected click opportunity**, and **confidence**. A useful rank formula for triage is:

`Priority = (Demand × SERP weakness × Original advantage × Acquisition fit) / (effort × risk factor)`

All inputs are **ordinal judgments**; the output ranks experiments, it does not forecast traffic. Use it only to choose the next test. Avoid multiplying arbitrary numbers into fabricated precision; written evidence takes precedence.

**Kill a candidate early** if it has no meaningful observed demand, if authoritative results already solve the full job, if safe activity guidance cannot be produced, or if search engines satisfy the need without any meaningful user interaction remaining.

### 3.4 Competitor audit for each query

For each of the top 10 organic pages, capture: domain, URL, page type, topical focus, search intent match, last substantial update, original assets, materials/time, accessibility adaptations, print quality, UX/ads, authorship, external references, evidence of use, and whether an app is forced.

Do not interpret a competitor's low number of app ratings as proof of low web competition or low downloads. Measure each distribution channel separately.

**Preferred competitive gap:** a result where a caregiver asks “Will this work for *this person*?” and the current answer is a generic list that provides no way to decide.

## 4. Site architecture: search entry points + canonical activities

### 4.1 URL contract

Use stable, readable, English URLs at the root initially. Do not rename them simply to stuff more keywords into paths.

```text
/
/activities/                         # Browse + filters; only index if substantial unique utility
/activities-for-seniors/             # Differentiated audience hub
/dementia-activities/                # Differentiated contextual hub
/senior-art-activities/              # Distinct art/craft intent, not a clone
/seated-activities-for-seniors/       # Distinct mobility/setting intent
/bedside-activities/                 # Distinct bedside adaptation and safety scope
/printables/                         # Useful indexed collection when curated
/activities/memory-collage/          # Single canonical activity
/activities/familiar-songs-collage/  # Another original activity
/about/
/editorial-policy/
/safety-and-scope/
/accessibility/
/corrections/
```

A single activity can appear in several hubs. **Do not create a nearly identical URL for every audience** (`/activities/memory-collage-for-dementia/`, `/...-for-seniors/`, `/...-for-arthritis/`). Instead:

- Maintain **one canonical activity page** as source of truth.
- Explain genuine audience-specific fit and adaptations on differentiated **hub pages**.
- If a variation requires substantially different materials, safety, steps or outcome, consider a separate independently useful activity page.
- Parameterized filter states (`?time=10&seated=true`) should usually not be indexed unless a particular state becomes a genuinely distinct, curated landing page with stable demand. Configure canonicals and crawling deliberately; do not blindly canonicalize materially different pages.
- Keep print views printable and accessible; usually canonicalize `/print/...` to the canonical activity URL if it is a duplicate HTML representation. PDFs can be freely downloaded; avoid PDF copies competing with HTML where a canonical response/header is practical.

### 4.2 Internal linking: a graph of genuine help

**Homepage → validated hubs → individual activities → relevant alternatives → other hubs.**

Activity pages link to: (a) parent hub, (b) adaptations or printable, (c) 2–4 truly related activities, and (d) one next step for visitors whose first choice did not fit. Hubs link to activities with descriptive anchors such as **“Seated paper collage with large-grip supplies”**, not twenty identical **“Learn more”** links.

Use HTML anchor links that crawlers can follow. Browsing and filtering must not hide all useful activities behind JavaScript, login, or an unlinked search form.

### 4.3 Search page contracts

**Hub page:**

1. Unique H1 matching a real job; an immediate, useful answer.
2. Clear selection criteria: time, energy, materials, mobility, sensory preferences and supervision.
3. 5–12 **actually appropriate** activities, each with why it fits, and real differences between options.
4. Practical adaptation notes and realistic safety limits; no blanket claims for an entire diagnosis.
5. Answers to genuine questions, transparent author/editor, substantiated sources where needed, dates.
6. Direct links to full guides and printables; optional relevant app link.

**Canonical activity page:**

1. Clear title and one-sentence outcome; authentic image.
2. Prep time, activity time, materials, cost, energy level, mess, help needed.
3. One-step-at-a-time instructions with photos/diagrams when warranted.
4. At least one genuinely useful variation, plus lower-effort / seated / low-sensory variants *if appropriate*.
5. A clear **stop or switch** condition. No pressure to finish, perform, or improve a score.
6. Print/save/share controls; text remains available without a login or app.
7. Byline, reviewed-by where warranted, change date, provenance and correction route.
8. Links to related activities, only where they are relevant to the reader's context.

**Original differentiation checklist:** A page must answer at least one question competitors leave unanswered, use a real illustration/photo or uniquely useful printable where feasible, and offer a concrete adaptation that has been reviewed or tested. Generic ten-item lists fail even if their prose sounds polished.

## 5. On-page SEO: honest relevance, not keyword theater

### 5.1 Title, H1, description

- **Title:** Lead with the exact task or natural phrase, then a specific differentiator and brand. Avoid title templates that produce dozens of near-duplicates.
- **H1:** A human-readable statement of the actual task, not a keyword inventory.
- **Meta description:** Explain why this answer is especially useful; understand Google may generate a different snippet. Meta descriptions are not a direct ranking lever.
- **Opening answer:** Let a stressed caregiver immediately know who this is for, materials and suitability. Avoid a lengthy SEO preamble.
- **Subheadings:** Organize real decisions, steps, adaptations, alternatives and safety; don't add FAQs only because a tool suggested keywords.
- **Dates:** Display genuine reviewed/updated dates, never auto-refresh them without substantive work.

Example:

```text
Title: Easy Art Activities for Seniors at Home | Creative Activities
H1: Easy art activities for older adults at home
Description: Find gentle, low-cost art activities you can do together, including seated options, everyday materials, clear steps and printable cards.
```

### 5.2 Images, video, and printables as search assets

- Use original, rights-cleared step photos with useful descriptive filenames and alt text (e.g. `large-grip-paper-collage-step-2.webp`). Alt text explains the image, not every target keyword.
- Include real materials, work in progress, and multiple acceptable outcomes. Don't imply a medical benefit from an attractive photo.
- Compress and resize assets; set dimensions; include important images in crawlable HTML; keep a descriptive caption where it helps.
- Publish a concise, captioned video **only when it genuinely reduces confusion**. Provide transcript or equivalent text, not a video-only guide.
- Provide printable activity cards under stable URLs and keep a useful HTML source page. Print/share assets may generate independent referrals when they are actually worth using.
- Test the right to use photos of people; obtain permission and avoid exposing health or identifying details about vulnerable participants.

### 5.3 Helpful-content integrity

- The editorial process records who researched, created, tested, reviewed and updated an activity; AI assistance does not count as human testing.
- Present clinical evidence proportionally: activity enjoyment is not proof of cognitive improvement or disease treatment.
- Link to reliable primary sources when making factual health, accessibility or safety statements. Identify uncertainty explicitly.
- No fabricated caregiver quotes, fake professional endorsements, invented outcomes, auto-generated “reviews”, or stock-photo demonstrations presented as field tests.
- No blind mass localization or paraphrase farm. AI may help research, format, translate drafts, or identify missing steps; a qualified human validates meaningful changes and sensitive claims.

## 6. Technical SEO: ship an indexable, accessible website

### 6.1 Baseline requirements

- HTTPS; one canonical host (`https://creativeactivities.org/` unless an operational reason dictates otherwise); correct 301/308 redirects from aliases and noncanonical variants.
- Public HTML available on first response by server-side render or static generation. Searchable primary content **not dependent on client JavaScript**.
- Useful URLs return `200`; moved pages redirect; permanently removed pages return real `404`/`410`; no fake `200` error pages.
- Every indexable page: unique title, meaningful H1, correct canonical, appropriate `robots` directives, crawlable internal links and navigation.
- Generate XML sitemaps containing only **canonical, indexable, working URLs**. Include accurate `lastmod` only after meaningful page changes; split by locale if helpful. `robots.txt` references sitemap.
- Avoid orphan pages, infinite calendar/filter URLs, duplicate print/search pages, soft 404s, accidental `noindex`/CDN/WAF crawler blocks.
- Do not disallow a URL in `robots.txt` merely to remove it from search; use `noindex` on a crawlable page when exclusion is intended.
- Use correct HTTP caching, image optimization and incremental static regeneration or equivalent, but never sacrifice accessibility for JavaScript effects.
- Track **Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile of real users**, aiming for LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1; audit mobile first.
- Respect canonicalization of alternate representations and language variants; do not canonicalize a full translation to English.

### 6.2 Example `robots.txt` (review before deployment)

```robots
User-agent: *
Allow: /

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

Sitemap: https://creativeactivities.org/sitemap.xml
```

This file **does not** decide whether OpenAI may use content for model training. OpenAI documents **OAI-SearchBot** (search discovery) separately from **GPTBot** (training). Choose a GPTBot policy explicitly after weighing reach, licensing and the site's values; don't quietly equate allowing search with consenting to training. Also verify CDN/WAF access for legitimate crawlers and published IP ranges where appropriate. There is no guarantee of inclusion in ChatGPT Search even when crawling is allowed.

Example opt-out *only if independently decided*:

```robots
User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /
```

Do not block Googlebot or Bingbot when organic visibility is the objective. Keep authentication, private notes and user data off public URLs; `robots.txt` is **not** access control.

### 6.3 Structured data: useful labels, no fabricated rich-result promises

Use JSON-LD that matches visible content, where appropriate:

- `Organization` and `WebSite` for site identity (accurate logo/URL/contact and verified sameAs links only).
- `BreadcrumbList` for a visible or otherwise authentic navigation hierarchy.
- `Article` for editorial guide/activity pages with truthful author, datePublished, dateModified and image fields where applicable.
- `ItemList` **only** for genuine visible curated collections. Schema.org types can provide semantics without yielding Google rich results.
- `VideoObject` when the page actually provides a qualifying video and metadata.

**Do not:** mark crafts up as `Recipe` unless they are literally recipes; use fake `Review`/`AggregateRating`; assume `FAQPage` will produce rich results on a normal content site; or expect `HowTo` rich results—Google deprecated that search appearance. There is no required special “AI schema.” Validate with Google Rich Results Test where relevant, Schema Markup Validator for general semantics, and inspection of production HTML.

Minimal example for a guide (replace all demonstration values with real metadata):

```html
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Article",
  "headline": "Easy Art Activities for Seniors at Home",
  "mainEntityOfPage": "https://creativeactivities.org/senior-art-activities/",
  "publisher": {
    "@type": "Organization",
    "name": "Creative Activities",
    "url": "https://creativeactivities.org/"
  }
}
</script>
```

Add actual author, publish/update dates, and relevant images when known; never publish empty or fictitious fields to satisfy a checklist.

### 6.4 Shipping and monitoring

Before public release: verify in **Google Search Console** and **Bing Webmaster Tools**; submit sitemap(s); inspect representative live URLs; run mobile CWV and structured-data checks; check image and print flows; test WAF/crawler access and canonical chains. Use **IndexNow** for participating search engines such as Bing when URLs are truly added, changed, or removed; **do not treat IndexNow as a Google indexation guarantee**.

## 7. GEO / AI discovery: be a useful source, not an AI-SEO trick

**Working definition:** Improve the probability that AI-assisted answer systems can discover, correctly understand, and reference our pages when someone asks for a practical caregiving activity. **A citation is not guaranteed, and an AI mention may create no visit.**

### 7.1 What is grounded in official guidance

- Google's AI Overviews/AI Mode rely on established Google Search foundations; eligible sources must be indexed and snippet-eligible. There are no additional AI-specific technical requirements.
- Google's 2026 guidance explicitly favors **non-commodity, expert-led, original information**, real images/media and sound technical SEO. It does **not** require `llms.txt`, manufactured content “chunks,” a special schema, or artificially obtained brand mentions.
- Bing/Copilot likewise stresses crawling, indexing, clear sources and content. Its **AI Performance** dashboard reports citations, URLs and sampled grounding queries where available.
- For ChatGPT Search, allow **OAI-SearchBot** and check host/CDN blocks; search crawling and foundation-model training controls are independent.

**Practical conclusion:** Build the same best-in-class content we would be proud to show a family caregiver. Present factual, bounded answers clearly enough for humans and systems to reuse, without creating hundreds of near-identical pages for hypothetical AI prompts.

### 7.2 What our pages can uniquely contribute to AI answers

AI can already list “ten craft ideas.” It often cannot verify **fit in a constrained real-world setting**. Our original data should answer:

1. **Situation:** Who wants to participate, what setting, how much energy and assistance?
2. **Requirements:** Materials, prep, cost, time, supervision, clean-up.
3. **Steps:** What someone can do immediately.
4. **Adaptations:** e.g. large-grip tools, low-vision contrast, bedside tray, seated use, no adhesives, lower sensory input—only when appropriate and safely reviewed.
5. **Boundaries:** When to pause, modify, seek professional advice, or avoid an activity.
6. **Provenance:** Who created/reviewed it, what was tested, and what relevant claim relies on which source.

This creates an **original, cite-worthy unit of knowledge** rather than another listicle. Format for readability using headings and simple tables where they help; there is no fixed paragraph length or “AI chunk size” required.

### 7.3 AI discovery operations

- Keep key activity instructions and claims in accessible, public HTML, with concise factual headings and durable URLs.
- Verify that text visible to people matches structured data; no hidden keyword blocks or “answer-engine” variants.
- Publish truthful `About`, editorial standards, safety scope, bylines, corrections, review methods and original assets.
- Reference reputable external work where it supports a claim; distinguish professional guidance from our own activity suggestions.
- Earn genuine independent mentions from caregivers, educators, librarians, community groups, and accessibility organizations by providing a **useful resource**. Do not buy low-quality AI citation placements.
- Offer printable cards and simple share URLs with original attribution. A printed card can create word-of-mouth even without a web click.
- If an `llms.txt` file is ever created for interoperability experiments, treat it as **optional**, subject to actual demand, and never as a Google visibility tactic.
- Do not attempt to fingerprint or manipulate AI-answer bots, inject prompts into page text, or create misleading machine-only content.

### 7.4 GEO measurement (observational, imperfect)

**Supported tools:**

- **Google Search Console:** Normal search performance and, where available to the property, its **Generative AI performance** report.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools:** **AI Performance** (citations, cited URLs, trends and sampled grounding queries in supported surfaces).
- **Analytics/server logs:** Search referrals and known AI referrals (e.g. ChatGPT), with the important limitation that origin/referrer data can be incomplete.
- **Manual fixed-prompt benchmark:** 20–30 representative caregiver questions per locale, on a scheduled basis. Record whether the result cites our page, cites a competing page, offers an inaccurate recommendation, or gives the complete answer without a link. This is a noisy diagnostic, not a stable ranking measurement.

Track AI-assisted sessions and their usefulness **separately** from citations and brand mentions. Do not turn a vendor's opaque “AI visibility score” into a business KPI.

## 8. Global SEO without global content dilution

**Serve globally on day one; localize only with evidence.** English pages can be internationally accessible immediately. Localization is a product-quality obligation, not a button.

### 8.1 Language architecture

- Keep initial English root URLs stable. Add real translated pages under language paths such as `/es/activities/...`, `/vi/activities/...`, `/fr/activities/...` when reviewed translations exist.
- Use human-readable translated titles/headings/steps, real material substitutions and suitable accessibility/safety guidance. Avoid translating only navigation while retaining English activity content.
- Implement **self-referencing canonical** for each genuinely localized page and **reciprocal `hreflang`** links for exact equivalents; include an `x-default` fallback only where useful. Use accurate supported language/region codes, e.g. `en`, `es`, `vi`, `fr`, `pt-BR`.
- Provide a visible language switcher; **do not force-redirect visitors based on IP** or hide other language pages from crawlers.
- Keep translated activity data and printable assets together; a Spanish guide linked to an English-only PDF is not a complete translation.
- Translate a hub only when enough appropriate translated activities exist to make it useful.

Example language set (only after both pages really exist):

```html
<link rel="canonical" href="https://creativeactivities.org/activities/memory-collage/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en" href="https://creativeactivities.org/activities/memory-collage/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="es" href="https://creativeactivities.org/es/activities/memory-collage/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://creativeactivities.org/activities/memory-collage/">
```

On the Spanish page the canonical is the **Spanish URL**, while `hreflang` lists both versions reciprocally. Implement by content ID mapping, not heuristic string replacement.

### 8.2 Locale expansion decision

Choose the next locale by combining **observed relevant impressions/sessions**, local search phrases, real user feedback, language review capability and availability of cultural adaptations. The lowest translation cost is not automatically the best market. No need for 18 languages at launch.

**What global means here:** content can be used without paywalls or accounts, with low-cost materials and culturally flexible alternatives. It does not mean asserting that every family, disability, or care practice is the same everywhere.

## 9. Off-site distribution: earn search authority without expensive marketing

The best potential referrer is **someone who actually used the activity**.

### 9.1 Distribution surfaces worth testing

- Family-caregiver organizations, community centers, libraries, care homes, school/parent support organizations and occupational/recreational activity specialists.
- Relevant newsletters, practitioner resource lists, volunteer groups, nonprofit resource pages, and local-language community blogs.
- Image-oriented discovery (including image search and Pinterest where suitable), but offer practical, accessible activities rather than clickbait images.
- Original printable packs for community programs, with a discreet URL/QR and license terms that permit genuine sharing.
- Existing iOS app users: contextually link relevant free activities and optional app features **only where appropriate and permission-compliant**.

### 9.2 The unconventional, defensible link asset

Publish a small number of **open, printable, adaptable Activity Packs** with permission for noncommercial families, teachers, libraries and care centers to use them, subject to clearly stated licenses. Each pack includes safety and accessibility notes, material substitutions and a source URL.

This can create *offline-to-online discovery* and natural backlinks from institutions. **Hypothesis:** a thoroughly tested printable toolkit may earn more genuine recommendations than fifty generic SEO posts. Test it; don't assume it.

### 9.3 Founder-led outreach

Start with **10–30 highly relevant contacts**, offering one resource tailored to their actual community, asking for criticism or permission to observe usability—not requesting a link in exchange. Record response, resource usage, referrals, and actual feedback. Never purchase links, orchestrate fake forum discussions or demand reciprocal linking.

## 10. SEO → real-world activity → optional iOS app

An informational search should be completely satisfiable **on the web**. Only recommend an install when the app uniquely provides:

- offline collections or caregiver-configured activity plans;
- accessible interactive features not reproducible well on a web page;
- private, local saves and repeat planning without extra login friction;
- tangible benefits the user has expressed, not vague claims of personalization.

Suggested contextual CTA: **“Prefer an offline set of activities? Try the free app.”** Not: “Install to see the next step.”

Instrument `activity_page → app_link_click → store_page_view` where measurement is actually possible; App Store installs must be measured through appropriate Apple attribution or supported analytics. Do not claim a one-to-one relationship between clicks and installations. Distinguish Google Search, AI referral and Apple App Store Search in reporting.

**Causal test:** For a relevant page cohort, compare a modest app CTA versus none. Keep the primary web activity available. If the CTA reduces activity-start proxy or harms usefulness, remove it.

## 11. Measurement system and decision thresholds

### 11.1 Events and privacy

Suggested **minimal** events, with no health diagnosis, participant name, child identity or sensitive free text in analytics payloads:

```text
activity_view             {activity_id, locale, page_type}
activity_print            {activity_id, locale}
activity_save             {activity_id, locale}
activity_share            {activity_id, locale, share_surface_optional}
activity_started_confirmed {activity_id, locale}   # opt-in; self-report only
activity_not_a_fit        {activity_id, locale, reason_category_optional}
app_cta_click             {activity_id, locale, app_id}
```

Collect only necessary consent-aware data; document retention and deletion policies. Do not infer conditions from URLs and send them to advertising platforms as user-level traits. If a page targets a medical context, avoid leaking its full sensitive URL unnecessarily through event tools, pixels or third parties.

### 11.2 Dashboard: one screen per decision

| Layer | Metric | Source | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discoverability | Indexed canonical pages / submitted pages | GSC, Bing | Indexation ≠ ranking |
| Demand match | Impressions for *relevant nonbranded* query clusters | GSC | GSC query samples can be incomplete |
| Search value | Qualified organic landings, CTR by query + device | GSC + analytics | CTR depends on SERP layout and intent |
| AI discovery | AI citations, cited pages, sampled grounding queries | Bing + GSC AI reports if available | Coverage differs by provider |
| Usefulness | Activity-start confirmations; print/save/share | First-party events | These are proxies, not proven outcomes |
| Human quality | % successful start in observed sessions; why not fit | Interviews/tests | Small samples, directional |
| Technical | CWV, index coverage, redirects, accessibility defects | CrUX/RUM, audits | Lab metrics differ from field performance |
| Production | Original assets/guide, time per maintained guide | Editorial tracker | More pages ≠ more value |
| App bridge | App CTA click-through; attributable installs where possible | First-party + Apple tooling | Attribution incomplete |

### 11.3 Practical thresholds — proposed experiments, not forecasts

**Days 1–14 (validation):** 20–40 keyword candidates researched, top 10 SERP audits for the strongest 8–10, 10 caregiver conversations or usability observations, and at least **one defensible search gap**. If no gap, **do not build 40 pages**.

**Days 15–60 (first publication):** Three differentiated hubs, **20 original activity pages**, and practical printables. Aim for ≥80% of observed unassisted users to find and begin an appropriate activity within approximately three minutes; improve safety issues regardless of score. These are internal experimental thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

**Days 61–90 (proof):** Check relevant GSC impressions and indexation, inspect URLs not indexed, improve weak pages, repeat usability tests, and publish up to 20 more activities **only if** search/query evidence and real-world usefulness support them.

**Scale one cluster if:** relevant impressions are increasing across several queries, real users can start activities independently, feedback demonstrates a need existing results miss, and editorial maintenance is sustainable.

**Pause or pivot if:** pages are indexed but consistently receive no relevant impressions after a reasonable observation period (taking site age and search volatility into account); no measured need for an app; most visitors only want a simple printable; or differentiation depends on unsafe/unverifiable claims.

Do not interpret “no ranking by day 30” as proof of no demand for a brand-new domain. Separate **failure to index**, **failure to rank**, **failure to attract clicks**, and **failure to help after a click**; each has different causes and remedies.

## 12. Ninety-day operating plan

### Days 1–7: discover, instrument, decide

- Verify domain ownership and technical access; set up GSC/Bing Webmaster Tools and privacy-conscious analytics.
- Confirm Keyword Planner **geo/language/network** settings; refresh exact candidates in US English plus one contrasting market.
- Audit SERPs for 8–10 queries and interview/observe 10 caregivers. Write one-page evidence cards with actual gaps and competitor examples.
- Select the first **three** coherent search clusters and identify their unique jobs, not just keyword variants.
- Build canonical site skeleton, activity content model and page templates from DESIGN.md.

**Gate:** A searcher has a specific problem; existing choices have a concrete failure; we can safely and uniquely solve part of it.

### Days 8–30: build proof of usefulness

- Publish first 3 hubs and 8–12 **original** activity pages with at least several real photographs/illustrations and printable cards.
- Implement clean HTML, navigation, canonical tags, sitemap, schema where eligible, mobile performance and accessibility tests.
- Observe first-time caregiver activity selection. Repair confusing wording and unsafe or impractical steps immediately.
- Invite a handful of librarians, caregivers or practitioners to critique the materials. No backlink request as the primary ask.

**Gate:** A visitor can act without needing to install anything or ask for instructions.

### Days 31–60: establish a coherent cluster

- Expand to approximately 20 quality activity pages *if user testing and search evidence justify them*.
- Add unique adaptation matrices and printables, not repetitive content.
- Use GSC to discover actual emerging query phrasing; update titles, internal links and hub descriptions where the intent match is wrong.
- Run image and print-sharing experiments; track genuinely helpful referrals and return use.

**Gate:** Several pages earn relevant impressions and measurable useful actions; failure modes are understood.

### Days 61–90: scale or stop

- Update underperforming pages before publishing more; up to ~20 additional pages only for confirmed gaps.
- Conduct an AI discovery benchmark and compare with Bing AI Performance / Google AI data where available.
- Pilot one carefully chosen second locale **only if** evidence and review capacity exist; otherwise remain English-first and globally accessible.
- Review 10 recent user journeys and 10 search result comparisons; decide scale, narrow, or stop for each hub.

**Gate:** We can point to a repeatable chain of **query → helpful page → activity started**, not merely a dashboard of visits.

### Weekly founder review (30 minutes)

1. Which 3 queries led to the most qualified activity starts, not just visits?
2. Which page was cited or recommended by independent sources? What original value earned it?
3. Which activity failed in the real world, and how did we change it?
4. What unnecessary page, keyword, feature or locale should we stop pursuing?
5. What's the smallest next experiment that could overturn our current priority?

### Brief before drafting

```yaml
content_id: senior-art-activities-v1

editor: "assigned person"

```

Do not publish placeholder fields as factual content. A human must replace all `TBD`/demonstration values before release.

### Editorial quality gate

- [ ] Solves one observable user job, with a direct answer near the top.
- [ ] Is materially distinct from any existing page, not an audience-word substitution.
- [ ] Contains practical materials/time/steps and at least one real fit consideration.
- [ ] Uses original assets where the activity would otherwise be ambiguous.
- [ ] Has credible, accurately attributed creator/reviewer information.
- [ ] Separates recreation from treatment; health/safety assertions are sourced and reviewed.
- [ ] Can be used on a phone and from a monochrome printout.
- [ ] Has correct title/H1/canonical/index directives and helpful links.
- [ ] Works with keyboard, zoom, screen reader, and low-bandwidth experience.
- [ ] Has a measurement hypothesis and clear owner for corrections/updates.

### Technical release gate (CI + scheduled audit)

- [ ] No broken canonical, accidental `noindex`, wrong status, redirect chain or sitemap mismatch.
- [ ] Locale pages have correct reciprocal `hreflang` mappings if translations exist.
- [ ] Structured data matches visible page; no misleading clinical or review markup.
- [ ] Images optimized with alt text and licensing recorded; print CSS tested.
- [ ] No sensitive PII in analytics, URLs, logs or third-party embeds.
- [ ] Sitemap and internal-link graph updated; orphan-page check passes.
- [ ] Representative real-user CWV and accessibility checks reviewed.

## 14. The strongest objections, and what would falsify the strategy

| Objection / failure mode | Strongest test | Decision response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Google, YouTube and Pinterest already satisfy this need.” | Watch 10 people use those results, then our guide; observe ability to start and adapt an activity. | If no improvement, abandon or narrow the job. |
| “Search demand is too small.” | Verified exact-query research across defined geographies, plus GSC impressions and adjacent intents. | Drop unsupported clusters; do not add pages to manufacture traffic. |
| “AI answers remove the reason to click.” | Compare answer completeness versus need for images, printables and hands-on selection; monitor referrals and uses. | Optimize for original assets and usable tools rather than text-only answers. |
| “The site becomes a generic content farm.” | Quarterly editorial uniqueness audit; compare repeated steps, assets and practical adaptations. | Consolidate or remove weak pages; reduce publishing velocity. |
| “Caregiving advice causes harm.” | Qualified review of sensitive adaptations, feedback channel, safety incidents. | Correct immediately; narrow claims and remove unsafe recommendations. |
| “Global translations distort activities.” | Native-language usability tests and cultural/material review. | Stop rollout until reviewers and localized assets exist. |
| “We earn traffic but no meaningful benefit.” | Track opted-in activity starts, print use and observed sessions—not dwell time. | Rebuild UX or stop chasing that traffic. |

**The nonconsensus bet:** A small, deeply useful activity library with tested adaptation and printable distribution can outperform a much larger generic article catalog for a specific caregiver job—even if it attracts fewer total visits. That remains a **hypothesis** until real-world usage, independent referrals and search results support it.
