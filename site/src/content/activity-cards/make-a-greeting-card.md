enjoy: "People who like writing to friends, sending cards on birthdays or keeping in touch with the people they care about. Good for someone who enjoys making something for another person."

mess: low

supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A sheet of thick paper or card, folded in half
  - Pictures cut from old cards, magazines or seed packets
  - A glue stick
  - A thick felt-tip pen or marker
  - An envelope
substitutions:
  - "No card? Fold a sheet of printer paper twice to make a smaller, sturdier card."
  - "No pictures? Press a leaf flat, draw a simple shape, or use a stamp or sticker."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Talk together about who the card is for, such as a grandchild, a neighbor or an old friend."
  - "Lay out four or five pictures and ask which one suits that person."
  - "Glue the chosen picture to the front of the folded card."
  - "Open the card and ask what they would like to say. A few words are plenty."
  - "Write the message, or let them write it if they prefer."
  - "Ask whether they would like to sign it, add a mark, or have you sign for both of you."
  - "Put the card in the envelope and agree together when to send or deliver it."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Glue the picture on before you start, so the only choice is what to say or who to send it to."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use bright card, one large picture, and a thick black marker so letters stand out clearly."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child writes out the message while the older adult decides the words and chooses the picture."
  - kind: for-a-group
    text: "Make one large card together for a shared friend or staff member, with each person adding a picture or a word."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Offer two finished messages written on slips of paper and let them point to the one they like."
safety:
  - "If you cut pictures, keep the scissors with the helper."
  - "A glue stick is less messy than liquid glue; check for skin sensitivity first."
  - "Do not ask anyone to write if it is tiring or upsetting. Dictating the message counts fully."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop with just a picture on the front. Keep the card in a safe place and finish the message another day, or send it as it is."

A greeting card is small, quick to make, and goes out into the world to someone real. Choosing who it is for gives the activity a purpose, and the person who receives it often replies, which can start a new conversation.
