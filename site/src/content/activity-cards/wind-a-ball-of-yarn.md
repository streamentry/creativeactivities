summary: Wind a skein or tangle of yarn into a neat ball together, taking turns holding and winding.

settings: [seated, bedside, tabletop]
groups: [one-to-one, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A skein or loose length of soft, chunky yarn
  - A small bowl or basket for the finished ball
substitutions:
  - "No yarn? Use a long strip of fabric, an old scarf, or a ball of string."
  - "A cardboard tube or a clothespin makes a good core to start winding around."
senses: [touch, movement, sight]
steps:
  - "Sit facing each other or side by side, with the yarn between you."
  - "Ask if they would prefer to hold the yarn or wind it."
  - "Find the loose end of the yarn together."
  - "Wrap the end around two fingers a few times to start the ball."
  - "Slide it off your fingers and keep winding around that small bundle."
  - "Turn the ball a little every few wraps so it stays round."
  - "Swap jobs whenever either of you would like to."
  - "Tuck the end under the last wrap and drop the ball in the bowl."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Start the ball beforehand around a cardboard tube. The only job is turning the tube slowly while the helper feeds the yarn."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Rest the ball on a tray or pillow and keep the loose yarn in a basket on the lap. Wind for a few minutes at a time."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Choose smooth cotton yarn in a single soft color instead of fluffy or brightly variegated yarn."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use thick yarn in a strong color against a plain dark lap blanket so the strand is easy to follow."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child holds the skein on outstretched hands while the older adult winds, or the other way around."
safety:
  - "Loose yarn is a trip and tangle hazard. Keep it in a basket and gather it up before anyone stands or walks."
  - "Never wrap yarn around a wrist, finger or neck tightly enough to press, and keep long strands away from the neck."
  - "Choose soft yarn that does not shed fibers; some wool can itch or irritate sensitive skin."
  - "Put the yarn away when the activity ends so nobody catches a foot in it later."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop with a small ball and plenty of yarn left. Put it all in the basket together and pick it up again another day, or not at all."

Winding yarn is a familiar, useful job with a steady rhythm. It works well with two people, one holding and one winding, and the finished ball can go to a knitter, a craft drawer or back into the basket for next time.
