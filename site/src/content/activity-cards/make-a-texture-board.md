summary: Glue different textures onto a sturdy board to make something interesting to touch and look at.
enjoy: "People who enjoy touching fabrics, knitting, quilting or the feel of different materials. Good for someone who likes exploring things by touch in a calm, unhurried way."
type: sensory
prepMinutes: 15
activityMinutes: [15, 40]
cost: low
mess: low
energy: low
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated, bedside]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A piece of sturdy cardboard, about letter size
  - "Six to ten textured scraps: corduroy, felt, velvet, burlap, ribbon, sponge"
  - A few household textures, such as a bottle cap, a button or a strip of bubble wrap
  - White craft glue or a hot glue gun for the helper
  - Scissors for the helper
substitutions:
  - "No fabric scraps? Cut squares from old clothes, towels, socks or a dish sponge."
  - "No cardboard? Use the lid of a shoebox or the back of a notepad."
  - "No glue? Double-sided tape holds light fabrics well."
senses: [touch, sight]
steps:
  - "Lay the textured scraps out on the table in a row."
  - "Pass each one over and feel it together, saying what it is like if you wish."
  - "Ask which texture they would like to put on the board first."
  - "Spread glue on the back of that piece. The helper can do this."
  - "Press it onto the board together and hold for a few seconds."
  - "Add more pieces, taking turns choosing where each one goes."
  - "Leave the board flat to dry, as the glue bottle suggests."
  - "Run your hands across the finished board together and talk about favorites."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Cut all the pieces to the same size beforehand and offer two at a time. Choosing is the main job."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Glue the pieces in advance and bring the finished board to the bedside. Explore it together by touch, one square at a time."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use strongly contrasting colors and very different textures, such as smooth satin next to rough burlap."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Use only soft, quiet fabrics and leave out anything crinkly, scratchy or noisy like bubble wrap."
  - kind: more-collaborative
    text: "Each person adds one texture to a shared board, then you pass it around and compare favorites."
safety:
  - "Glue all pieces firmly. Buttons, caps and beads are choking risks if they come loose and might be put in the mouth."
  - "If small pieces might be mouthed, leave out buttons and caps and use only large fabric pieces."
  - "Keep any hot glue gun and scissors with the helper. Let hot glue cool fully before the board is handled."
  - "Avoid sharp edges such as cut plastic, wire or sandpaper; check the board by hand before handing it over."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one or two pieces. Feeling the scraps together is the activity too. Set the board aside to finish later, or keep it as it is."

A texture board turns a pile of scraps into something to explore by hand. Making it together is a chance to compare what feels soft, rough or smooth, and the finished board can be picked up again whenever someone wants to.
