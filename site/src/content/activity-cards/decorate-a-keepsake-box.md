type: craft

activityMinutes: [30, 60]

energy: medium
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - "An empty shoebox or small cardboard box with a lid"
  - "Wrapping paper, wallpaper scraps or light fabric, cut a little larger than each side of the box"
  - "A glue stick or double-sided tape"
  - "Scissors for the helper"
  - "A few small items chosen by the person, such as a ribbon, a shell or a copy of a photo"
substitutions:
  - "No shoebox? Use a tea tin, a cereal box cut down, or a sturdy gift box."
  - "No wrapping paper? Use old greeting cards or the pages of a calendar."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Set the box on the table with the lid off, and lay out the paper, the glue and the small items."
  - "Choose the paper or fabric together. Any choice is fine."
  - "Lay the paper on one side of the box, and ask the helper to trim it to a little larger than that side."
  - "Glue the back of the paper and press it onto the side of the box, then fold the extra paper over the edges and smooth it flat with the hand."
  - "Turn the box and repeat on each side, then cover the lid the same way."
  - "Set the box aside for a few minutes so the glue holds."
  - "Look through the small items together and choose a few to keep inside."
  - "Place the chosen items in the box and put the lid on."
  - "Agree on a place to keep the box, such as a shelf or the bedside table."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Start with a box that is already covered or painted, and add only a ribbon and a few small items."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Use a small box on a lap tray and cover only one side, with the items chosen to go on top."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Choose bold, high-contrast paper, and place the items where they are easy to see."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Use one large sheet of paper to wrap the whole box, so there are fewer pieces to handle."
  - kind: more-collaborative
    text: "Two or three people each decorate one side of the same box."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child smooths the paper onto each side while the grandparent chooses the paper and the things to keep inside, and says why each one matters."
safety:
  - "Scissors are for the helper only, and keep the glue away from the mouth."
  - "Use a glue stick or double-sided tape rather than spray glue."
  - "Choose items too large to swallow, and leave out pins, buttons or anything sharp."
  - "Use copies of photos or papers, and keep the originals somewhere safe."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one side. Put the box and its paper aside, and carry on another day, or simply enjoy a few of the items together."

A keepsake box gives small, meaningful things a home. The covering is a steady, satisfying task, and choosing what goes inside often leads to stories without anyone having to ask a direct question.
