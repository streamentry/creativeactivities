  - "A comfortable chair or bed positioned by a window with a view outside"

steps:
  - "Settle into a chair or bed by the window, with a blanket and a drink within reach."
  - "Ask what they can see outside, or invite them to look for birds for a minute."
  - "Wait quietly until a bird lands or flies past."
  - "Ask what color the bird is, or what it is doing."
  - "Trace the bird's path in the air with one finger, slowly, as it moves."
  - "Draw a simple shape of the bird on the paper, using the color they name."
  - "Listen for calls or song, and talk about how they sound."
  - "Close the curtain if the glare is strong, and stop when they are ready."
variations:

safety:
  - "Window glare can be strong. Use a sheer curtain or move the chair so the sun is not in the eyes."
  - "Glass can be cold in winter or hot in summer. Keep a blanket on the lap and the room at a comfortable temperature."
  - "Keep the window closed or fitted with a safety stop. Never let anyone lean out to see a bird."
  - "Check glasses are clean and on before watching, so details are easier to see."

Watching birds needs nothing more than a window and a few minutes of patience. The person decides what to notice, whether that is color, movement or song, and a drawing can be as simple as a shape. It suits a quiet afternoon at the bedside as well as a visit with the family.
