seoTitle: "Family Recipe Book: Write Down Favorite Dishes Together"
description: "Start a family recipe book together by talking about favorite dishes and writing them down. Seated at a table, no cooking, low cost, with easier versions."
summary: Talk about favorite dishes and write them down together, one page at a time.
type: conversation
prepMinutes: 5
activityMinutes: [15, 40]
cost: low
mess: none
energy: low
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A notebook, binder or a few sheets of lined paper
  - A pen that writes easily
  - Old recipe cards or cookbooks, if you have them
  - A few pictures of food from magazines or packaging (optional)
  - A folder or envelope to keep loose pages together
substitutions:
  - "No notebook? Staple a few sheets of printer paper together or use index cards."
  - "Writing is tiring? They talk and you write, or type it and print it later."
senses: [sight, touch, hearing]
steps:
  - "Sit together at a table with the notebook open to a blank page."
  - "Ask about a dish they liked to make or eat, such as a Sunday meal or a holiday treat."
  - "Write the name of the dish at the top of the page."
  - "Ask what went into it and write the ingredients as a simple list. Rough amounts are fine."
  - "Ask how it was made and write the steps in their own words."
  - "Invite a story, such as who made it, where it was eaten, or what it smelled like."
  - "Read the page back aloud and change anything they want changed."
  - "Stop after one page, or carry on with another dish if you both want to."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Choose the dish together from two or three food pictures, and write only the name and one sentence about it."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A grandchild writes or types while the older adult tells the recipe. The child can draw the dish beside it."
  - kind: for-a-group
    text: "Each person shares one dish. Collect the pages into a shared group book and make a copy for everyone."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Write in large, dark letters on unlined paper, one ingredient per line, and read each line aloud as you go."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Show pictures of foods and let them point to favorites. Write down what they chose, in their own book."
safety:
  - "This is a writing and talking activity, not cooking. Keep it away from a hot stove or oven, and do not test recipes during the session."
  - "Food memories can bring up people who have died or hard times. Pause, listen, and let them lead, or move to another dish."
  - "There are no wrong answers. If amounts or steps sound unusual, write them as told rather than correcting them."
  - "If you later cook a recipe together, check for allergies, swallowing needs and diet advice first."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after a single dish name. Close the book, leave it somewhere easy to find, and offer to add another page on a different day."

A recipe book grows a page at a time, and every page is a small story. Talking about food is familiar and easy to join, and the finished book becomes something the whole family can keep and cook from later.
