  - "A tune they choose, saved on a phone or player, or a song you can sing together"

  - "No scarf? Wave a napkin, a tea towel or just an open hand."
  - "No music player? Hum or sing a song they know and move to that."
senses: [hearing, movement, sight]
steps:
  - "Set a sturdy chair with no wheels on a flat, clear floor, with nothing to trip over around it."
  - "Ask what tune they would like, offering two or three choices or letting them pick their own."
  - "Help them sit back with both feet flat on the floor, and play the tune at a comfortable volume."
  - "Start with something small, such as tapping one foot or a hand on the knee, and let them copy or lead."
  - "Try one movement at a time: sway side to side, roll the shoulders, or reach a scarf up and down."
  - "Move at their pace and in their way, with no right shape and nothing to count."
  - "Move along with them, and follow any movement they come up with."
  - "Let the song end, then sit quietly together and offer a drink of water."

safetyFlags: [fall-risk]
safety:
  - "Keep them seated in a stable chair with no wheels, feet flat on the floor. Do not ask them to stand up and dance."
  - "Pain means stop. Never ask for more movement than they offer, and do not count repetitions."
  - "This is not exercise or rehabilitation. Check with their doctor or care team before any new movement."
  - "Stay within arm's reach and keep the space around the chair clear. Keep the scarf away from the neck."
  - "Pause if they look tired, short of breath or unsteady, and offer a drink of water."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one song, or after a few bars. Turn the music down, sit quietly together and say that was lovely, or simply that is enough for now."

This is moving to music for the pleasure of it, not exercise. Nothing is counted and no movement is the correct one. They choose the tune, they choose how much to move, and a tapped foot is as good as a sway.
