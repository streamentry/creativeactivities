name: Make a button picture
seoTitle: "Button Picture: A Colorful Craft to Make Together"
description: "Make a button picture together by sorting and gluing buttons onto card. Seated at a table, low cost, with easier versions. Buttons are a choking hazard."
summary: Sort buttons by color or size and glue them onto card to make a simple picture.
type: craft
prepMinutes: 10
activityMinutes: [15, 40]
cost: low
mess: low
energy: low
supervision: hands-on
settings: [tabletop, seated]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A small number of large buttons, about the size of a quarter or bigger
  - A sheet of thick card, about letter size
  - White craft glue or a glue dot sheet
  - A pencil for a simple outline, such as a tree or a flower
  - A shallow tray or plate to hold the buttons
substitutions:
  - "No buttons to spare? Use large bottle caps, cut-out paper circles or felt shapes."
  - "No card? The inside of a cereal box or a paper plate works well."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Count the buttons onto the tray before you start and note the number."
  - "Draw a simple outline on the card together, or choose a shape you like."
  - "Look through the buttons together and sort a few by color or size if they enjoy it."
  - "Ask where the first button should go. Any place is the right one."
  - "Add a dot of glue and press the button down together."
  - "Keep adding buttons, taking turns choosing and placing."
  - "Leave the picture flat to dry somewhere safe."
  - "Count the loose buttons back into their container and check the number matches."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Glue only three to five buttons, or glue them for them and let the person choose each spot."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use bright buttons on dark card, or dark buttons on white card, so each one stands out clearly."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Offer only a few buttons in one or two colors on a plain tray, so there is less to look at and choose from."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child applies the glue while the older adult picks the buttons and the spots. Keep buttons away from young children."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Hold up two buttons and watch which one they reach for or look at. Pointing and nodding are full answers."
safety:
  - "Buttons are a choking hazard. Skip this activity if the person may put small objects in their mouth; use paper circles instead."
  - "Use large buttons only, keep the number small, and count them in and out so none go missing."
  - "To reduce the risk further, glue the buttons onto the card yourself first and use the activity for arranging and talking."
  - "Stay at the table the whole time; do not leave loose buttons within reach of the person or young children."
  - "Use a glue stick or white craft glue rather than hot glue, and check for skin sensitivity first."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one or two buttons. Count the loose ones away, leave the picture to dry, and offer to add more another day."

Buttons are colorful and pleasant to handle, and arranging them needs no particular skill. Sorting, choosing and placing them can be relaxing, and many people have stories about a button tin at home.
