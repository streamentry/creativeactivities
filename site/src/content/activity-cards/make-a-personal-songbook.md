summary: Collect favorite songs, a few lines of words and the stories behind them in one book.

activityMinutes: [15, 40]

supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated, bedside]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A notebook or a few sheets of paper in a folder
  - A pen or a thick marker
  - A phone, radio or music player to play songs
  - Printed lyrics for a few songs (optional)
  - Old record sleeves, sheet music or concert programs, if you have them
substitutions:
  - "No printer? Copy one verse or the chorus by hand in large letters."
  - "No music player? Sing or hum together, or simply write the titles and talk about them."
senses: [hearing, sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Sit together and open the book to a blank page."
  - "Ask about a song they enjoy, or offer a choice of two familiar songs."
  - "Play the song at a comfortable volume and listen together."
  - "Write the song title at the top of the page."
  - "Add the singer or a favorite line, if they want to."
  - "Invite a story about the song, such as where they heard it or who they danced with."
  - "Write a few words about the story, in their words."
  - "Play the song again if they would like, or choose the next one."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Prepare pages ahead with the title and chorus already printed. Together you only listen, sing along and add a sticker or a word."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Use a clipboard and play songs quietly from a phone. The helper writes; the person listens and chooses."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Print lyrics in very large type with high contrast, one verse per page, or skip reading and enjoy listening."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A grandchild finds the songs on a phone and plays them, then writes the titles while the older adult shares the stories."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Play a short part of two songs and watch for a smile, tapping or humming. That reaction is the choice; write it down."
safety:
  - "Keep the volume comfortable and check it with them. Sudden loud music can startle or hurt."
  - "Check hearing aids are in, switched on and not whistling. Some people prefer headphones off and the speaker nearby."
  - "Some songs bring back sad memories or people who have died. Pause, listen kindly, and offer another song or a break."
  - "Avoid songs with lyrics or associations that may upset them; follow their lead on what to play."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one song. Leave the book open at that page, play something gentle, or simply sit and listen together for a while."

A songbook is a collection of favorites, not a test of words or tunes. Listening comes first and writing is optional, so anyone can take part, and the book gives family and visitors an easy way to share music later.
