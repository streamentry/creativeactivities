materials:
  - "Thick kitchen sponges, cut by the helper into big, simple shapes such as a circle, a square or a leaf"
  - "Washable, non-toxic poster paint in two or three colors"
  - "Two or three shallow plates to hold the paint"
  - "Thick paper or card, plain or folded into a card"
  - "Newspaper or a plastic cloth, and paper towels"
  - "Scissors, for the helper to use beforehand only"
substitutions:
  - "No kitchen sponges? Use thick pieces of foam packaging or a clean, dry car-washing sponge."
  - "No plates? Use the lids of food tubs or a plastic tray."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Before you start, the helper cuts the sponges into big, simple shapes and puts the scissors away."
  - "Cover the table, then set out the paper and the sponge shapes."
  - "Pour a thin layer of paint onto each plate, one color per plate."
  - "Choose a shape and a color together. Any choice is a good choice."
  - "Press the sponge flat into the paint, then press it onto the paper."
  - "Lift the sponge straight up and look at the print."
  - "Print again in the same place or somewhere new, and change shape or color whenever you like."
  - "Leave the paper flat to dry and choose where to show it."
plainSteps:
  - Before you start, the helper cuts the sponges into big, simple shapes.
  - The helper puts the scissors away.
  - Cover the table.
  - Set out the paper and the sponge shapes.
  - Pour a thin layer of paint onto each plate.
  - Use one color per plate.
  - Choose a shape and a color together.
  - Any choice is a good choice.
  - Press the sponge flat into the paint.
  - Then press it onto the paper.
  - Lift the sponge straight up.
  - Look at the print.
  - Print again in the same place or somewhere new.
  - Change shape or color whenever you like.
  - Leave the paper flat to dry.
  - Choose where to show it.

A thick sponge is easy to hold, forgiving to press, and gives a bold print every time. Each press stands on its own, so the activity can pause or stop after any print.
