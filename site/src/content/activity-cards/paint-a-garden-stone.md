name: Paint a garden stone
seoTitle: "Paint a Garden Stone Together: An Outdoor Craft"
description: "Paint a smooth stone together to place in a garden, window box or path. Non-toxic acrylic paint, low cost, at a table indoors or outside in the shade."
summary: A smooth stone painted with simple colors or patterns, then placed outdoors where you both can see it.
type: craft
prepMinutes: 15
activityMinutes: [20, 40]
cost: low
mess: medium
energy: low
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated, outdoors]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A few smooth, flat stones about the size of a palm
  - Non-toxic, water-based acrylic or poster paint
  - Brushes with thick handles, or cotton swabs for dots
  - A jar of water and paper towels
  - Newspaper or an old cloth to cover the table
  - A water-based outdoor varnish, applied by the helper (optional)
substitutions:
  - "No stones nearby? Use a flat piece of wood, a terracotta tile or a large shell."
  - "No acrylic paint? Paint pens or poster paint work; poster paint will fade outdoors without varnish."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Wash the stones and let them dry before you start."
  - "Set out two or three stones and ask which one they would like to paint."
  - "Pick one or two colors together."
  - "Paint a base color over the top of the stone, or leave the natural stone showing."
  - "Add a simple pattern if you both like, such as dots, stripes, a flower or a single initial."
  - "Leave the stone to dry for about an hour, then add a second coat if you want stronger color."
  - "Choose a spot together where the stone will sit, such as a flower bed, a doorstep or a window ledge."
  - "Carry the stone out and place it, or have it carried out if walking is tiring."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Paint the base coat ahead of time and offer cotton swabs for making dots of one bright color."
  - kind: seated
    text: "Bring the stone, paint and a tray to a garden chair in the shade and paint it on a lap board."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Choose a pale stone and dark paint, or paint the stone white first so later colors stand out."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "Children gather and wash the stones, and everyone paints one to set out together in a row."
  - kind: for-a-group
    text: "Each person paints a stone and the group arranges them along a path or around a planter."
safety:
  - "Use non-toxic, water-based paint; wash it off skin with soap and water, and check for skin sensitivity first."
  - "Choose stones too large to fit in the mouth, and lift heavy ones for the participant."
  - "Outdoors, sit in the shade, offer water and a hat, and keep sessions short on hot or cold days."
  - "If you use varnish, the helper applies it in a ventilated space with no one else nearby."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one color. A plain painted stone looks good in a garden, and you can add a pattern another day or leave it exactly as it is."

A painted stone is solid, smooth to hold, and ends up outside where it can be seen every day. Choosing where it goes is as much a part of the activity as painting it.
