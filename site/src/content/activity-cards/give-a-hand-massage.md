summary: Gently massage one hand with a little plain lotion, asking first and stopping whenever they wish.

activityMinutes: [5, 15]

settings: [seated, bedside]
groups: [one-to-one]
materials:
  - "A small bottle of plain, unscented lotion or hand cream"
  - "A clean, soft towel to rest the hand on"
  - "A chair with arms, or a pillow to support the arm"
  - "A bowl of warm water and a towel for washing hands first (optional)"
substitutions:
  - "No lotion? Use a dry, light touch, or wrap a warm towel gently around the hand."
  - "No soft towel? Use a clean pillowcase folded in half."
senses: [touch]
steps:
  - "Wash and dry your hands, then sit beside them with the lotion and towel in reach."
  - "Ask if they would like a hand massage, and wait for a clear yes."
  - "Ask which hand they would like to start with, and rest it on the towel."
  - "Warm a small drop of lotion between your palms."
  - "Rest your hands lightly around theirs, without gripping."
  - "Stroke slowly from the wrist toward the knuckles, on the back of the hand."
  - "Move to each finger in turn, with light, slow pressure."
  - "Ask how it feels, and stop straight away if they pull away."
  - "Wipe any extra lotion with the towel, and let the hand rest."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Hold the hand in both of yours and make a few slow strokes on the back of it, or simply hold it for a moment."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Sit on the edge of the bed or on a chair beside it, with the hand resting on a pillow. Keep the session to a few minutes."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Skip the lotion and use dry hands with very light touch, or wrap a warm towel around the hand instead."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Offer an open palm and wait for the hand to come toward you. Stop if it pulls away or the face looks uneasy."
safety:
  - "Ask permission before touching, and stop as soon as they pull away, tense up or say to stop."
  - "Test a small drop of plain lotion on the inside of the wrist first, and check for known allergies or skin reactions."
  - "Do not press on red, swollen, bruised, broken or very thin skin. A dry, gentle wipe is enough there."
  - "Avoid sore joints, and never bend fingers past the point where they move easily."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one hand, or even one finger. Wipe the hand dry, rest it on the towel, and sit quietly together."

A hand massage is simple, close and shared, and the person decides whether and how long it happens. Asking first and stopping at any sign of discomfort matters more than the technique. It fits a quiet visit as easily as a moment at the bedside.
