energy: low
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - Colored paper, old magazine pages or wrapping paper
  - A sheet of card, about letter size
  - A glue stick
  - A pencil for the outline
  - Two or three shallow bowls or saucers to sort the pieces
substitutions:
  - "No colored paper? Use junk mail, catalog pages, paper napkins or tissue paper."
  - "No card? Use the plain side of a cereal box or a paper plate."
senses: [sight, touch, hearing]
steps:
  - "Draw a large, simple shape on the card together, such as a leaf, a house, a fish or a circle."
  - "Tear a few pages into strips, then into pieces about the size of a postage stamp."
  - "Sort the pieces into bowls by color, or leave them mixed if you both prefer."
  - "Rub glue over a small part of the shape."
  - "Press pieces onto the glued area, taking turns or working side by side."
  - "Talk about the colors as you go, such as which ones remind you of a garden, the sea or a favorite coat."
  - "Glue the next small area and keep filling the shape until it feels finished."
  - "Leave it flat to dry and choose a place to show it."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Tear all the pieces in advance and offer one bowl of a single color at a time."
  - kind: seated
    text: "Work on a lap tray with a raised edge so the pieces do not slide off."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Skip the tearing if the sound or feel is unpleasant, and use a small set of quiet, muted colors."
  - kind: more-collaborative
    text: "One person tears and hands over pieces while the other places them, then swap roles if they like."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use a bold, thick outline on pale card and strongly contrasting colors, such as dark blue on yellow."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child tears and sorts the pieces by color while the grandparent chooses where each one goes. Small pieces stay on the table."
safety:
  - "Keep small torn pieces away from anyone who may put paper in their mouth, and clear them away afterward."
  - "Use a glue stick rather than liquid glue; check for skin sensitivity first."
  - "Glossy magazine edges can occasionally nick skin, so offer softer paper if that is a concern."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop when only part of the shape is filled. A half-finished mosaic still looks good, and you can add more another day or leave it as it is."

Tearing paper needs no scissors and no drawing skill, and the uneven edges are part of the look. Filling a shape piece by piece is unhurried, easy to share, and easy to pause at any point.
