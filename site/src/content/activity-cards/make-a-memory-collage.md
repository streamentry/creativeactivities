activityMinutes: [15, 30]

supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A few printed photos or copies (never the only print)
  - Old magazines, calendars or seed catalogs
  - A sheet of thick paper or card, about letter size
  - A glue stick
  - Scissors for the helper
substitutions:
  - "No photos to spare? Use postcards, greeting cards or pictures cut from catalogs."
  - "No card? The inside of a cereal box works well."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Clear a table and lay out the paper, the glue stick and a small pile of pictures."
  - "Look through the pictures together and talk about any that catch an eye."
  - "Ask which picture should go in the middle. Any answer is the right one."
  - "Glue the back of that picture and press it down together."
  - "Add more pictures around it, taking turns choosing and placing."
  - "Stop when it feels finished, or when you have had enough for today."
  - "Agree on a place to keep or show the collage, such as a fridge or a frame."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Cut the pictures out before you start and offer two at a time to choose from."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Use a lap tray or a clipboard, a glue stick only, and five or six pictures."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Choose large, high-contrast pictures and work on dark card so each one stands out."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child cuts and glues while the older adult chooses and tells the story behind each picture."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Hold up two pictures and watch where their eyes or hand go. Pointing and nodding are full answers."
safety:
  - "Keep scissors with the helper if cutting is unsafe or tiring."
  - "Use copies of photos so an original is never cut or glued by mistake."
  - "A glue stick is less messy than liquid glue; check for skin sensitivity first."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one picture. Put the collage somewhere safe and offer to carry on another day, or simply enjoy looking at the pictures."

A collage has no wrong answer. Placing pictures side by side often opens up conversation about places, people and seasons, and the finished sheet gives you something to look at together again later.
