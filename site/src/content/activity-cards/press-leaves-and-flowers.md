summary: Gather flat leaves and flowers, then press them between paper in a heavy book together.
type: nature
prepMinutes: 5
activityMinutes: [15, 30]
cost: free
mess: low
energy: low
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated, outdoors]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A handful of flat leaves and small flowers, checked as safe to handle
  - A large, heavy book you do not mind marking
  - Plain paper, paper towels or coffee filters
  - A few more books or a brick to add weight
  - A small basket or bag for collecting
substitutions:
  - "No garden? Use leaves from a park, a store-bought bunch, or herbs from the kitchen."
  - "No spare heavy book? Stack two pieces of cardboard and weigh them down with cans."
senses: [sight, touch, movement]
steps:
  - "Walk outside together or bring a few leaves and flowers to the table."
  - "Lay them out and look at the shapes and colors together."
  - "Ask which ones they would like to keep. Flat, dry pieces press best."
  - "Open the book near the middle and lay a sheet of paper on the page."
  - "Place the leaves and flowers on the paper with a little space between them."
  - "Lay a second sheet of paper on top."
  - "Close the book gently and stack the other books on top."
  - "Write the date on a slip of paper and tuck it in the cover."
  - "Agree on a day, about two weeks away, to open the book together."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Collect and dry the pieces beforehand. At the table, the only choice is which leaf goes in the book next."
  - kind: seated
    text: "Bring a tray of leaves and flowers indoors and press them at the table, with no walk needed."
  - kind: for-a-group
    text: "Each person chooses one leaf or flower for a shared book, then you open it together on a set date."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Hold up two leaves and watch where their eyes or hand go. A point or a nod is a full answer."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Choose large leaves with clear outlines, such as maple or oak, and press them on dark paper."
safety:
  - "Some plants are toxic or irritate skin, such as foxglove, poison ivy and giant hogweed. Check every plant you pick and wash hands afterward."
  - "Avoid holly, roses with thorns, and prickly or stinging plants."
  - "Dried petals and seed heads are small enough to swallow. Supervise if anything might be put in the mouth."
  - "Outdoors, choose level paths, wear sturdy shoes, and take a hat and water in the sun."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one leaf. Close the book on whatever is in it, and you will still have something to open together later."

Pressing leaves and flowers splits into two pleasant moments: collecting and opening the book weeks later. The pressed pieces can go on a card, a bookmark or a collage, or simply stay in the book to look at again.
