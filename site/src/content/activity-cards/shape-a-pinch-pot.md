materials:
  - "Air-dry clay, about the size of a tennis ball for each person"
  - "A plastic tablecloth or oilcloth to protect the table"
  - "A damp cloth or sponge"
  - "A small bowl of water"
  - "A tray or board on which to leave the finished pot to dry"
substitutions:
  - "No air-dry clay? A non-baking modeling compound works, but it will not harden."
  - "No plastic tablecloth? Use a baking tray or a board with newspaper on it."
senses: [touch, sight, movement]
steps:
  - "Cover the table with a plastic cloth and put the damp cloth and the water bowl within reach."
  - "Give each person a ball of clay about the size of a tennis ball."
  - "Roll the clay gently between the palms until the surface is smooth."
  - "Press a thumb into the middle to make a dip, then stop and look at it."
  - "Turn the clay slowly and press the walls out with the thumbs, a little at a time, keeping the base flat."
  - "Keep the walls an even thickness, about as thick as a pencil, and dampen the fingers only when the clay feels dry."
  - "Smooth the rim with a damp finger, then wipe the fingers on the cloth."
  - "Set the finished pot on a tray and leave it to dry for a day or two, away from direct heat."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Flatten the ball into a disc first, then press the edges up with the thumbs."
  - kind: seated
    text: "Work on a tray in the lap or on a low table, with the clay supported on a folded towel."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Guide the clay with your hands over theirs, and watch for any sign they want to keep going or stop."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Choose a dark clay and work on a white tray so the shape stands out, and describe the shape aloud as you press."
safety:
  - "Use non-toxic air-dry clay, and keep clay and fingers away from the mouth."
  - "Clay can dry out the skin. Keep a bowl of water nearby, and offer hand cream afterward if needed."
  - "Keep the finished pot away from the mouth until it is fully dry, and check the rim for sharp edges."
  - "Wipe up dried clay with a damp cloth rather than sweeping, so no dust is breathed in."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop once the clay feels tiring. Wrap it in a damp cloth inside a plastic bag and carry on another day; stopping early is fine."

A pinch pot needs no wheel, no kiln and no skill, only patience with the clay. Pressing it open with the thumbs is a steady, hands-on task, and each small bowl is a finished object by the end of the session.
