materials:
  - "A sheet of thick card, about letter size, with parallel slits cut by the helper about one inch apart, stopping one inch short of the top and bottom edges"
  - "Colored paper or thin card in two or three colors, cut by the helper into strips about one inch wide"
  - "Scissors and a ruler for the helper"
  - "A glue stick or low-tack tape"
substitutions:
  - "No colored paper? Use strips of old magazine pages, wrapping paper or colored paper bags."
  - "No card base? Use the flat side of a cardboard box, slit in the same way."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Lay the slit card on the table, with the slits running up and down and the strips within reach."
  - "Choose the first strip together. Any color is fine."
  - "Start at the bottom and push the strip over the first upright strip of card, then under the next, and so on across."
  - "Pull the strip gently so it lies flat, but not so tight that the card bends."
  - "For the next row, start the opposite way: under the first upright, then over the next."
  - "Push each new row up against the last one, so the card is covered."
  - "When a strip runs out, tape its end to the back of the card and start a new strip."
  - "Stop when the card is covered, then tape down the loose ends on the back."
variations:

  - kind: bedside
    text: "Use a clipboard on a lap tray, with the strips already cut and a few rows woven together."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use high-contrast strips on a dark card base, and describe each over and under aloud."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Place a strip in their hand and guide it gently over and under, pausing to see if they want to go on."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child cuts the strips in advance, and the two take turns on each row."
safety:
  - "Scissors are for the helper only. The person weaves with their hands alone."
  - "Check the slit edges for sharp corners, and round them off with the helper before weaving."
  - "Use a glue stick or low-tack tape, and keep small pieces away from the mouth."

Weaving is a repeating, tactile task where each row is visible as it grows. The over-and-under rhythm is simple to learn after two or three strips, and the finished piece makes a useful card or place mat.
