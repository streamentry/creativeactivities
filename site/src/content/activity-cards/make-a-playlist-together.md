summary: Choose a few favorite songs together on a phone, play each one and write the ones they like on a card.
enjoy: "People who have a favorite singer, band or radio station, and anyone who likes telling the stories behind a song. Suits a day for listening more than doing."

activityMinutes: [15, 40]

settings: [tabletop, seated, bedside]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - "A phone, tablet or music player with a few songs saved, or a music service set up in advance"
  - "A small card, index card or sheet of paper"
  - "A thick pen or marker"
  - "Headphones or a small speaker, if you have them"
substitutions:
  - "No music service? Use a radio, a CD player or songs saved on a USB stick."
  - "No card? Write the list in a notebook or on the back of an envelope."
senses: [hearing, sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Sit together with the phone, the card and the pen in reach."
  - "Ask what kind of music they enjoy, or offer two familiar styles to choose from."
  - "Find one song they name, or pick one from the choices you offered."
  - "Play the song at a comfortable volume and listen together."
  - "Ask whether they like it."
  - "Write the title on the card if they say yes."
  - "Choose the next song together, and repeat until the card has five to eight titles."
  - "Read the list aloud and ask which song they would like to hear first."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Choose three or four songs in advance and offer them as simple yes or no choices. The helper handles the phone and writes the titles."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Prop the phone on a stand or pillow at a low volume, and keep the card on a clipboard. The helper handles the screen."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Write the titles in large, high-contrast letters, or read them aloud from the screen. Choose songs by sound rather than by picture."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A grandchild finds the songs and plays them, while the older adult says what they like and the child writes the titles on the card."
safety:
  - "Set the volume low and check it with them. Sudden loud music can startle or hurt."
  - "Check hearing aids are in and working. Use headphones only if they fit comfortably and do not pinch the ears."
  - "Some songs bring back sad memories. Pause, listen kindly, and offer another song or a break."
  - "Turn off autoplay video and ads before you start, so the music does not change to something unexpected."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one or two songs. Leave the card as it is, play something gentle, or simply sit and listen together for a while."

A playlist is a shared choice, not a test of names or lyrics. Listening comes first, so anyone can take part, and the finished card gives family and visitors a list to play together next time.
