materials:
  - Large beads with big holes, enough for the bracelet

substitutions:
  - "No stretchy cord? A thin ribbon or string, tied in a loose double knot, follows the same steps."

senses: [sight, touch, movement]
steps:
  - "Before you start, the helper cuts the elastic cord to about 12 inches and ties a knot in one end."
  - "Wrap a short piece of clear tape around the other end to make a stiff tip for threading."
  - "Place the beads in the tray, inside the raised edges, and sit with the tray in front of you."
  - "Take one bead at a time and slide it onto the taped end of the cord."
  - "Keep going until the bracelet is long enough to go around the wrist, leaving a few inches free."
  - "Hold the cord firmly and tie a knot at the free end, so no bead can slide off."
  - "The helper trims the extra cord with the scissors."
  - "Put the beads back in the tray, counting them as you go."
plainSteps:
  - Before you start, the helper cuts the cord to about 12 inches.
  - The helper ties a knot in one end.
  - Wrap clear tape around the other end.
  - Put the beads in the tray.
  - Take one bead at a time.
  - Slide it onto the taped end of the cord.
  - Keep going until the bracelet fits your wrist.
  - Leave a few inches of cord free.
  - Hold the cord firmly.
  - Tie a knot at the free end.
  - The helper trims the extra cord with the scissors.
  - Count the beads back into the tray.

safetyFlags: [small-parts, sharp-tool, ingestion, adhesive]
safety:
  - "Beads and small pieces are a choking hazard for children under three and for anyone who mouths things. Do not use beads with an under-three or with anyone who mouths objects."
  - "Use large beads only after the person's safety has been considered. Keep loose beads in the tray, and count them out and back in."
  - "Elastic cord can snap back. Tie the knot before you let go, and keep the cord away from the face and eyes."
  - "Do not use resin or epoxy to hold or seal beads. The fumes and skin contact are a hazard."
  - "Scissors are for the helper only."
  - "An adult stays nearby while beads are in use, and a finished bracelet is never worn on the neck."

A beaded bracelet is a simple, repeated rhythm: one bead, then the next. Working at a table with the beads in a tray keeps the pieces in one place, so the work is easy to pause and easy to pick up again.
