settings: [tabletop, seated, bedside]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - "A copy of a favorite photo, printed or photocopied. Never use the only print."
  - "A postcard-sized piece of thick card, or a blank postcard"
  - "A glue stick"
  - "A pen with a thick grip"
  - "An envelope, a stamp and the address, if sending"
substitutions:
  - "No photo copy? Use a picture from a magazine or calendar, or a drawing."
  - "No card? Use the front of an old greeting card, trimmed to size."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Look through the photo copies together and choose one to send."
  - "Place the photo on the card to see how it fits, and ask the helper to trim it if needed."
  - "Glue the back of the photo and press it onto the card."
  - "Press the photo flat with a hand and hold it for a few seconds."
  - "Ask who the card is for, and what one line they would like to write."
  - "Write the line on the back of the card, in the words the person chooses, and sign it together."
  - "If sending, put the card in an envelope with the address and a stamp."
variations:
  - kind: easier

  - kind: bedside
    text: "Work on a clipboard or lap tray, with a card already cut to size."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Choose a photo with strong contrast and large details, and write the line in a thick dark marker."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child picks the photo and writes the name, while the older adult says the line to write."
safety:
  - "Use photo copies only, so an original is never cut, glued or lost."
  - "Use a glue stick, which is less messy than liquid glue, and check for skin sensitivity first."
  - "Check the address before sealing the envelope. Never write bank details or passwords on a card."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after choosing the photo. Keep the copy somewhere handy and carry on another day, or simply look at it together."

A photo postcard turns an old picture into something new to send or give. The person chooses the photo and the words, and the finished card often starts a conversation with the person who receives it.
