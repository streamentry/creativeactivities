name: Make a pine cone bird feeder
seoTitle: "Pine Cone Bird Feeder: A Hands-On Craft to Share"
description: "Make a pine cone bird feeder together with fat and birdseed, then hang it where you can watch. Low cost, seated at a table, with nut-free and easier options."
summary: Spread fat on a pine cone, roll it in birdseed and hang it outside to watch the birds.
enjoy: "People who enjoy watching birds from a window, spending time outside or feeding the wildlife in a garden. A good fit for anyone who likes a hands-on job that leads to something to watch."
type: craft
prepMinutes: 10
activityMinutes: [15, 30]
cost: low
mess: medium
energy: low
supervision: hands-on
settings: [tabletop, seated, outdoors]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A large, open, dry pine cone
  - Vegetable shortening, or peanut butter if nobody has a nut allergy
  - Wild birdseed in a shallow dish
  - A blunt knife or spoon for spreading
  - String or garden twine, about 18 inches
  - Newspaper or a tray to work on
  - Wet wipes or a damp cloth
substitutions:
  - "No pine cone? Use a toilet paper tube or a slice of stale bread with a hole for the string."
  - "No shortening? Lard or suet also hold seed well and contain no nuts."
  - "No birdseed? Unsalted oats or raisins work for many garden birds."
senses: [touch, sight, hearing]
steps:
  - "Cover the table and set out the pine cone, the fat and the dish of seed."
  - "Tie the string around the top of the pine cone. The helper can do this."
  - "Spread fat into the gaps of the cone with the spoon or knife."
  - "Roll the cone in the dish of seed together, pressing it in."
  - "Sprinkle any bare patches with more seed by hand."
  - "Choose a branch or hook that can be seen from a window or chair."
  - "Hang the feeder there together, or let them watch while you hang it."
  - "Sit by the window and see which birds arrive, if you like."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "The helper spreads the fat first. The only job is rolling the cone in seed, or sprinkling seed on with fingers."
  - kind: seated
    text: "Make the whole feeder at the table, then hang it outside a window that can be seen from a favorite chair."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Wear thin gloves or use a spoon to press seed on, so hands stay clean and the fat is never touched."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "A child does the rolling and sprinkling while the older adult chooses the spot and names the birds that visit."
  - kind: for-a-group
    text: "Make one feeder each and hang them all on the same tree, then watch together from indoors."
safety:
  - "Peanut butter can cause a severe allergic reaction. Ask about nut allergies first, and use vegetable shortening if anyone is unsure."
  - "Birdseed is not food for people, and small seeds can be a choking risk. Supervise if anything might be put in the mouth."
  - "Keep any knife blunt, or use a spoon. Wash hands after handling seed and fat."
  - "Hang the feeder from the ground or a stable step, never a chair or ladder. Keep paths clear outdoors to prevent falls."
  - "Pine cones collected outdoors can have sharp scales and sap. Choose dry, open ones and check them first."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop once the cone has a little seed on it. The birds will not mind. Hang it as it is, or leave it on a tray and finish another day."

A pine cone feeder is a short, hands-on job with a result you can keep watching. Hanging it where it can be seen from a chair gives you both a reason to sit by the window together over the next few days.
