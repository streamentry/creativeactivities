summary: Listen to music together and let lines, shapes and colors follow the sound.
type: music
prepMinutes: 5
activityMinutes: [10, 30]
cost: low
mess: low
energy: low
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated, bedside]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - Large sheets of plain paper
  - Crayons, colored pencils or washable markers
  - A phone, radio or music player
  - A short list of three or four pieces of music with different moods
  - Masking tape to hold the paper still
substitutions:
  - "No large paper? Tape two sheets of printer paper together or use the back of wrapping paper."
  - "No markers? A soft pencil or a ballpoint pen works; chunky crayons are easier to grip."
senses: [hearing, sight, touch, movement]
steps:
  - "Tape a sheet of paper to the table so it does not slide."
  - "Lay out the crayons or markers and offer a choice of color."
  - "Play the first piece of music at a comfortable volume."
  - "Draw lines or shapes together that follow the music, fast or slow, big or small."
  - "Swap colors whenever either of you likes."
  - "Stop the music and look at the drawing together. Talk about it only if they want to."
  - "Try a different piece of music on a new sheet, or stop there."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Use one chunky crayon and one slow piece of music. Simply moving the crayon back and forth is a full way to take part."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Tape the paper to a clipboard or lap tray and play music quietly from a phone close by."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use thick black or dark markers on white paper, and describe aloud the shapes you are drawing."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Choose soft, steady music at low volume, one color and a small sheet. Stop if it feels like too much."
  - kind: with-children
    text: "Take turns choosing the music. A child and an older adult draw on the same sheet from opposite sides."
  - kind: more-collaborative
    text: "Draw on one shared sheet. One person draws while the music plays, the other adds color when it pauses."
safety:
  - "Keep the volume comfortable and check hearing aids are in and switched on. Avoid sudden loud music."
  - "Use washable, non-toxic markers or crayons. Marker on skin usually washes off with soap and water; avoid drawing on skin."
  - "Keep pens and crayons away from the mouth if the person may chew or taste things."
  - "Some music brings up strong feelings. If they seem upset, pause, offer comfort, and switch to something calm."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after a few marks or a single song. Keep listening together without drawing, or put the music on for a quiet moment instead."

Drawing to music needs no plan and no drawing skill. The music sets the pace, the marks can be anything, and the page simply records a piece of time you spent listening together.
