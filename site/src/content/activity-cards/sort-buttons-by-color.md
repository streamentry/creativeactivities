name: Sort buttons by color
seoTitle: "Sort Buttons by Color: A Simple Hands-On Activity"
description: "Sort a bowl of large buttons into a muffin tin or small dishes by color or size, in whatever order they like. Seated at a table, with easier and low-vision versions."
summary: Sort a bowl of large buttons into a muffin tin or small dishes by color or size, in any order.
type: sensory
prepMinutes: 5
activityMinutes: [10, 30]
cost: free
mess: low
energy: low
supervision: hands-on
settings: [tabletop, seated]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group]
materials:
  - "A bowl or tub of large buttons, in a few colors or sizes"
  - "A muffin tin, or a few small dishes or bowls"
  - "A tray or towel to work on"
substitutions:
  - "No muffin tin? Use a shallow box lid or an egg carton."
  - "No large buttons? Use large bottle caps or plastic lids instead."
senses: [sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Sit at a table with the bowl of buttons, the muffin tin and the tray in reach."
  - "Pour a small handful of buttons onto the tray."
  - "Pick up one button and look at its color and size."
  - "Place it in a muffin cup or dish that matches, or start a new one."
  - "Keep going, one button at a time, in whatever order they like."
  - "Ask what they notice about the colors or sizes as the dishes fill up."
  - "Tip the buttons back into the bowl, one dish at a time."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "The helper pre-sorts two or three colors into dishes, and the person adds one button at a time to the right one."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Use only smooth, plain buttons in two colors, and sort on a soft cloth instead of a hard tray."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use dark dishes for pale buttons, or pale dishes for dark ones, so each button stands out against the dish."
safety:
  - "Use only large buttons that cannot fit in the mouth, and remove any small or loose parts from the bowl."
  - "Never use this with someone who puts things in their mouth. Stay close and keep the bowl on the table."
  - "Count the buttons out at the start and back in at the end, so none are left on the floor or in a chair."
  - "Check buttons for cracks, sharp edges or loose threads before use, and remove any that are damaged."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after a few buttons. Tip the sorted buttons back into the bowl, and leave the dishes out for another time."

Sorting buttons is a hands-on task with no wrong answer, because any sorting is a good sorting. The colors, shapes and feel of the buttons give plenty to talk about without any questions to answer. It works well at a table with a helper nearby.
