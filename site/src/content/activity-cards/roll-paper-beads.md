  - "Before you start, the helper cuts the paper into long, thin triangles, about six inches long."

  - "Place the pencil or straw on the wide end, and roll the paper tightly around it."
  - "Press the glued tip down to seal the bead, then slide it off the pencil or straw."

  - "Tie a knot at each end of the string, so the beads stay on."
plainSteps:
  - Cut the paper into long, thin triangles.
  - Lay one triangle on the table.
  - Put the wide end toward you.
  - Add a line of glue along the narrow end.
  - Place the pencil on the wide end.
  - Roll the paper tightly around the pencil.
  - Press the glued tip down.
  - Slide the bead off the pencil.
  - Let the beads dry for a few minutes.
  - Make more beads.
  - Keep them in the tray.
  - Thread each dry bead onto the string.
  - Count each one as it goes on.
  - Tie a knot at each end.

safetyFlags: [small-parts, sharp-tool, ingestion, adhesive]
safety:
  - "Beads and small pieces are a choking hazard for children under three and for anyone who mouths things. Do not make or use paper beads with an under-three or with anyone who mouths objects."
  - "Use paper beads only after the person's safety has been considered. Keep the beads in the tray, and count them out and back in."
  - "Scissors are for the helper only, and the paper is cut away from the hands."
  - "Do not use resin or epoxy to seal or harden beads. The fumes and skin contact are a hazard."
  - "The glue stick is not for eating or mouthing. Let the glue dry before you thread the beads."
  - "An adult stays nearby while the beads are being made and threaded, and the finished string is never worn on the neck."

Paper beads are made from the simplest materials, and each one is rolled by hand, so no two look quite the same. Rolling is slow and repetitive, which suits a quiet hour at the table, and the beads can be strung into a necklace, a bracelet or a hanging decoration.
