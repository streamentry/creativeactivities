description: "Arrange a jar of flowers together from garden or store stems. Five minutes to set up, seated at a table, low cost, with easier, bedside and low-vision versions."
summary: Choose, trim and place flowers or leafy stems in a jar or vase together.
enjoy: "Anyone who has kept houseplants, enjoyed a garden or liked choosing flowers for the kitchen table. A good pick for a calm, unhurried morning."
type: nature
prepMinutes: 5
activityMinutes: [10, 30]
cost: low
mess: low
energy: low
supervision: nearby
settings: [tabletop, seated, bedside]
groups: [one-to-one, small-group, intergenerational]
materials:
  - A small bunch of flowers or leafy stems, checked as safe to handle
  - A jar, mug or vase that will not tip easily
  - Water in a jug
  - Scissors or pruners for the helper
  - An old towel or newspaper for the table
substitutions:
  - "No flowers? Use leafy branches, herbs from the kitchen, or dried grasses."
  - "No vase? A clean jam jar, a sturdy mug or a cut-down milk carton works."
  - "Silk or paper flowers are fine if fresh ones are not practical."
senses: [sight, touch, smell]
steps:
  - "Spread the towel on the table and lay the stems out side by side."
  - "Fill the jar about halfway with water together."
  - "Look at the stems together and ask which one they would like to start with."
  - "Trim the end of that stem so it fits the jar. The helper can do the cutting."
  - "Place the first stem in the jar, or hand it over so they can place it."
  - "Take turns choosing and adding stems. There is no right shape."
  - "Turn the jar around and look at it from each side together."
  - "Agree on where the flowers should go, such as a windowsill or the table."
variations:
  - kind: easier
    text: "Trim all the stems to the same length before you start and offer two at a time to choose from."
  - kind: bedside
    text: "Use a small, heavy mug on a tray table with three to five short stems. Keep the water low to limit spills."
  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Choose large flowers in strong colors, such as yellow or orange, and use a plain dark jar for contrast."
  - kind: less-sensory
    text: "Pick unscented flowers or plain green leaves, and work in a quiet spot with only a few stems out."
  - kind: for-a-group
    text: "Give each person one stem to add to a shared vase, then talk about the finished arrangement together."
safety:
  - "Some common flowers and plants, such as lily of the valley, foxglove and daffodils, are toxic if eaten. Check every plant before use."
  - "Lilies shed pollen that stains and can irritate skin; remove the pollen tips or choose other flowers."
  - "Remove thorns and prickly leaves from roses and holly before handing stems over."
  - "Keep scissors and pruners with the helper, and wipe up spilled water so nobody slips."
  - "Supervise closely if petals or leaves might be put in the mouth."
stopNote: "It is fine to stop after one or two stems. A single flower in a jar is a finished arrangement. Put it somewhere it can be seen and enjoyed."

Arranging flowers asks for small choices, not skill. Picking a stem, turning the jar and deciding where it should sit are all ways to take part, and the flowers stay on show for days afterward.
