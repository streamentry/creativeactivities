steps:
  - "Before you start, the helper cuts the cord to about 12 inches, and wraps one end in clear tape."
  - "Tie a loose knot in the other end of the cord so the beads stay on."
  - "Place the beads in the tray, inside the raised edges, in front of the person."
  - "Slide one bead at a time onto the taped end, counting as you go."
  - "Stop when the beads fill about half the cord, leaving a few inches free."
  - "Tie a firm knot at the free end, so the beads cannot slide off."
  - "Loop the knotted end through the split keyring, and tie a knot to hold it."
  - "Put the beads back in the tray, and count them out and back in."
plainSteps:
  - Before you start, the helper cuts the cord to about 12 inches.
  - The helper wraps one end in clear tape.
  - Tie a loose knot in the other end.
  - Place the beads in the tray.
  - Slide one bead onto the taped end.
  - Count as you go.
  - Stop when the beads fill about half the cord.
  - Tie a firm knot at the free end.
  - Loop the knotted end through the keyring.
  - Tie a knot to hold it.
  - Count the beads back into the tray.

safety:
  - "Beads and small pieces are a choking hazard for children under three and for anyone who mouths things. Do not use beads with an under-three or with anyone who mouths objects."
  - "Use large beads only after the person's safety has been considered. Keep loose beads in the tray, and count them out and back in."

A beaded keyring is a small project with a clear end: a few beads, one knot and a keyring to carry. Working from a tray keeps the beads together, and the finished piece can be made for yourself or given to someone you know.
