materials:
  - "A phone, radio or music player"
  - "Three or four short tunes they like, in different styles"
  - "Squares of colored paper about the size of a hand, or paint sample cards"
  - "A tray or a sheet of plain paper to lay the colors on"
substitutions:
  - "No colored paper? Use pages torn from an old magazine, fabric scraps or colored napkins."
  - "No music player? Hum or sing a tune yourself, or use the radio."
senses: [hearing, sight, touch]
steps:
  - "Spread the colored squares on the table where they can see them all."
  - "Ask which tune they would like first, or offer two to choose from."
  - "Play the tune at a comfortable volume and listen together."
  - "When it ends, ask which color seems to go with it, and let them pick one or a few."
  - "Lay the chosen colors in a row on the tray. Any color is a good answer."
  - "Play the next tune and start a new row beside the first."
  - "Look at the rows together, and keep the one they like best if they wish."
variations:

  - kind: low-vision
    text: "Use a few large squares in strong, clear colors on a dark tray, and say each color aloud as you lay it down."
  - kind: nonverbal
    text: "Hold up two colors after each tune. They can answer by pointing, nodding, looking or picking one up."
  - kind: for-a-group

  - kind: with-children
    text: "The older adult chooses the tune and the child lays out the colors they pick, then they swap. Each can explain their choice or not."

safety:
  - "Keep the volume low and check it with them. A sudden loud sound can startle."
  - "Turn off autoplay and ads so the music does not change without warning."
  - "There is no right color for a tune. Never correct a choice or ask them to explain it."

Choosing a color for a tune asks nothing of anyone's memory or skill. Each person hears the music their own way, so every row of colors is different, and that difference is something to look at together.
