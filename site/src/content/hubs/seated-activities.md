Seated activities are for the many days when sitting at a table or in a chair is the right level of effort. The goal is to make something together, or to share a task, with the work close at hand. Keep the setup simple and let the person decide how much to do.

## How do you set up the seat and the table first?

A little preparation makes the session easier. Bring the person's chair close to the table, with the backrest supporting them and both feet resting on the floor or a footrest. Put the materials where they can reach them without leaning far forward.

- **Use a lap tray or clipboard** when the table is too high or too far away. The raised edge keeps small pieces from sliding off.
- **Choose a low table** for work that needs both hands flat, such as the leaf prints and the tear-paper mosaic.
- **Keep the good light and the glasses** close to hand. A lamp at the side is better than a glare from above.
- **Set out one color or one item at a time.** A small choice is easier to handle than a full tray.

## When is it time to change to bedside?

If sitting up becomes uncertain, or the person slumps, tires quickly or asks to lie down, the work has moved beyond this hub. Switch to the [bedside activities](/bedside-activities/) hub, where the cards cover bed use and the safety notes that go with it. Ask the nurse or care team about positioning if the person is in hospital.

## Which activities suit a garden chair?

Some cards are made outdoors. The [garden stone](/activity/paint-a-garden-stone/) is painted in a chair in the shade, and the [leaf prints](/activity/print-with-leaves/) are pressed on a lap board. Check the weather and the sun before you go out, and keep water and a hat close by.

Plant work needs care with soil and tools. The [seed pot](/activity/plant-seeds-in-a-pot/) works best in a deep tray on a lap or a table, so that nobody needs to bend or stand. [Folding towels](/activity/fold-towels-together/) is the one steady task on this list, so keep it in short turns with the basket beside the chair.

## How do you keep the work going when the hands tire?

Let the person choose the color, the leaf or the next link, and do the cutting, gluing and holding yourself. The [family tree of hands](/activity/make-a-family-tree-of-hands/) can be done one hand at a time across several visits. The [paper chain](/activity/make-a-paper-chain-garland/) can be added to any day, and the [press leaves and flowers](/activity/press-leaves-and-flowers/) card can be left in a heavy book until the next visit.

The [caregiving guide on playing together](/caregiving/how-to-play-together/) has more on pacing and roles, and the [art supplies guide](/activities/art-supplies-for-older-adults/) explains which materials are easiest to hold.
