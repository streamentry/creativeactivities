Music activities work well because the music itself carries the moment. Nobody has to find the words to start, and a single tune has a natural end. The aim here is enjoyment and time together, not practice or improvement.

## How do you choose the music?

Ask what they would like to hear today, or offer two choices: an old favorite or something new, singing or instruments. A phone, radio or smart speaker works. Turn off autoplay and ads first, so a loud advertisement or a sudden change of song does not break the moment.

- **Keep the volume low** and check it with them. Hearing aids can make loud sound uncomfortable.
- **Have two or three tunes ready** rather than a long list to scroll through.
- **Let them change their mind.** A skipped song is a choice, not a failure.

## How can music be more than listening?

Each card here gives the music a job. A [sing-along](/activity/hold-a-sing-along-at-home/) is the simplest. [Making a playlist](/activity/make-a-playlist-together/) or a [personal songbook](/activity/make-a-personal-songbook/) turns choosing into something to keep. [Tapping a rhythm](/activity/tap-a-rhythm-together/) and a [sealed shaker](/activity/make-a-sealed-shaker/) give the hands something to do. [Moving to music in a chair](/activity/move-to-music-in-a-chair/) adds gentle movement, and [matching colors to a tune](/activity/match-colors-to-a-tune/) or [drawing to music](/activity/draw-to-music/) brings in color.

## What if words are hard today?

Choose a card with no words needed: tapping, moving, the shaker or matching colors. Watch where their eyes, hands and feet go while the music plays. A smile, a tapping foot or leaning closer is an answer. The [guide to music for calm and connection](/creative-calm/music-for-calm-and-connection/) has more ideas.
