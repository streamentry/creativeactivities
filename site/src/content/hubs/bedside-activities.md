Bedside activities are for the days when getting up is hard, or not safe. The goal is company and something to look at together, not a finished piece. Keep the materials on a lap tray, keep the session short and let the person decide what happens next.

## How should you set up the bed or chair first?

Most of the difficulty is in the setup, so check it before you begin. Ask the nurse or care team about positioning, swallowing and infection control, and follow what they say over anything here.

- **Sit up with support.** Use pillows or a backrest so the person can see the work without slumping.
- **Put on glasses and hearing aids.** A task is much harder when the person cannot see or hear the helper.
- **Use good light.** A lamp at the side of the bed helps more than a bright overhead light.
- **Choose a tray with a rim.** Raised edges keep small pieces from sliding off.

## How long should a session last?

Five to fifteen minutes is enough for most people. Several short sessions in a day usually go better than one long one. Watch for these signs that it is time to stop.

| Sign | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Looking away or closing the eyes | Pause, and offer a drink or a rest |
| Hands that stop or let the materials drop | Take the work away and say what got done |
| Answers that get shorter or slower | Switch to listening, or stop |
| Restlessness or asking to leave | Let it end without an argument |

Leave the work where it is so you can come back to it tomorrow. A [songbook](/activity/make-a-personal-songbook/) works well in small pieces: one song, one story, then rest. [Draw to music](/activity/draw-to-music/) can be a few lines at a time.

## How do you let the person lead while your hands do the work?

The person chooses the color, the song, the photo or the word. The helper cuts, glues, holds and writes. This keeps the activity theirs even when they cannot manage the materials. Offer simple choices that have no wrong answer, such as "this one or that one?", and accept whatever comes back.

- [Make a photo postcard](/activity/make-a-photo-postcard/): the person picks the photo, and you glue it while they say what to write.
- [Write a letter together](/activity/write-a-letter-together/): you hold the pen and they say the words.
- [Sketch what is on the table](/activity/sketch-what-is-on-the-table/): they look at a cup or a brush and say where the line should go.

For a person who is very late in the illness, the [late-stage guide](/activities/late-stage/) explains how to offer choices without pressure. If music is a strong way in, the [music activities guide](/activities/music/) has more ideas to try in bed.

## How do you keep the bed and materials clean and safe?

Wash your hands before and after each session, and again after tissues or bedding. Keep the work on a clipboard or tray rather than on the blanket. Keep beads, buttons, seeds and loose glitter off the bed, and put scissors away when the session ends. Keep liquid glue and hot drinks well away from the tray.

Prepare pieces before you come to the bedside. A [texture board](/activity/make-a-texture-board/) is easier when the glue is already dry and the person only explores it by touch. The [sensory activities guide](/activities/sensory/) has more ideas for touch and sound that suit a bed.
