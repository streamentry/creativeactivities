Arts and crafts are a natural fit for many older adults, because they use skills people already have: patience, taste, a steady hand for a long task. The projects here are for adults in general, not only for one kind of need. Each card has its own materials list and steps, so this page concentrates on choosing and setting up.

## How do I choose a project for stiff or painful hands?

Look for work that uses large movements and avoids fine control. Tearing, sweeping a brush and tracing a hand all use the whole hand, and each can be paused at any point.

- [Tear paper mosaic](/activity/tear-paper-mosaic/) uses torn pieces, so there are no scissors and no fine lines to follow.
- [Weave paper strips](/activity/weave-paper-strips/) keeps the cutting with the helper, who slits the card in advance.
- [Make a family tree of hands](/activity/make-a-family-tree-of-hands/) needs only a pencil and a light touch.

If grip is weak, use a thick brush or a sponge, and check the adjustments for arthritis in the [art supplies guide](/activities/art-supplies-for-older-adults/).

## What changes for low vision?

Bold shapes and strong contrast do most of the work. Choose one bright color, use dark card for pale pieces or white paper for dark ones, and work on large sheets. Describing each step out loud helps, and so does a lamp placed to the side so shadows do not fall on the work.

- [Make a color collage](/activity/make-a-color-collage/) starts with one strong color, which keeps the choices small.
- [Paint a shared picture](/activity/paint-a-shared-picture/) uses a large sheet and thick, bright marks that show from across the table.

## Which projects are the least messy?

Pencil, crayon and washable paint on a taped sheet make the least mess. Glue sticks are cleaner than liquid glue. Lay a plastic cloth under the work, keep water in a covered cup, and sort materials into trays so nothing rolls off the table. Match the project to the mood of the day:

| If... | Try... |
| --- | --- |
| Hands are stiff or painful | [Tear paper mosaic](/activity/tear-paper-mosaic/) or [weave paper strips](/activity/weave-paper-strips/) |
| Eyesight is limited | [Paint a shared picture](/activity/paint-a-shared-picture/) or [make a color collage](/activity/make-a-color-collage/) |
| They dislike mess | [Draw repeating patterns](/activity/draw-repeating-patterns/) |
| They want something useful | [Make a greeting card](/activity/make-a-greeting-card/) or [decorate a keepsake box](/activity/decorate-a-keepsake-box/) |
| They want to look closely | [Sketch what is on the table](/activity/sketch-what-is-on-the-table/) |

## How should I set up the table?

Set up before the person arrives. Put one tray in front of each person, with pieces already cut or torn and sorted into bowls, and the next step ready to hand. Keep the work at table height rather than near the face, and leave a clear place where a finished piece can rest.

For a group project, the [group art guide](/activities/group-art-activities/) explains how to give each person a useful role. A creative art activity is for enjoyment and time together, and art therapy is a separate profession. The [art therapy or creative activity guide](/activities/art-therapy-or-creative-activity/) explains the difference. If someone says they cannot draw, the [drawing and painting guide](/activities/drawing-and-painting-to-relax/) covers how a helper can start the first stroke together.

End the session when it stops being fun. A half-finished card is still a card, and the project can wait for another day.
