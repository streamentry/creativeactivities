This hub is for the afternoons when the person can get up and go outside, or when a standing job or a shared table suits them better than a quiet task alone. The cards were chosen for that, so they are not a list of things to do in bed or a chair. If the person is staying in bed, the [bedside activities](/bedside-activities/) hub is the right place to start.

## Which situation is this for?

Use this list when the person has enough energy to move about, even slowly, and enjoys being somewhere other than indoors. It also suits people who like a job to do on their feet, such as folding or watering, and families who want a project to share over a few visits.

- **Outdoor time.** Gardens, patios and a sheltered window box. Plant, print, paint or hang something where the weather and the light are good.
- **A job to stand for.** Short standing tasks with a clear end, such as a stack of towels.
- **A group at one table.** Several people can add to one picture, one recipe book or one card without any single person carrying it.

## How do you plan an outdoor card?

Check the weather before you go out, and pick a shaded spot with a seat close by. Carry water, a hat and any tools in one bag so that nobody has to carry them back and forth. Keep the work low, on a tray or a potting bench, so the person can sit while they do it.

If the card uses soil or paint, pick a place where spills do not matter and you can wash hands afterwards. Plan for a short session that ends before anyone is tired, and leave the rest for another day. A pot that is still growing or a stone that is still drying gives the next visit something to look at.

## How do you make a group work for everyone?

A group works best when each person has a part that suits them, and when nobody is asked to keep up with the fastest hands at the table. Give the quieter people a job that is easy to start, such as choosing the colors or holding the paper, and let them join in at their own pace.

- Take turns in a set order, so each person knows when they are next.
- Agree on a finish point before you start, so the group can stop together.
- Keep one spare seat or a quiet corner for anyone who needs a break.

## Where should I start?

For a week-long project, try [plant seeds in a pot](/activity/plant-seeds-in-a-pot/) and check on it each day. For a walk and a hanging task, try [make a pine cone bird feeder](/activity/make-a-pine-cone-bird-feeder/). For a short standing job, [fold towels together](/activity/fold-towels-together/) keeps the hands busy.
