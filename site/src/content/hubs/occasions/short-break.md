A short break is a small window: waiting for a kettle, the gap before a meal, the quiet half hour before a visitor arrives. These cards are chosen so you can start inside that window and finish, or not, without any fuss.

## How do you pick one fast?

Look at what is already to hand. If there is a phone, try the [sing-along](/activity/hold-a-sing-along-at-home/) or [draw to music](/activity/draw-to-music/). If there is paper and a pen, try a [slow line pattern](/activity/draw-a-slow-line-pattern/) or [repeating patterns](/activity/draw-repeating-patterns/). If the washing is in, [fold towels together](/activity/fold-towels-together/) needs nothing else.

## How do you keep it short on purpose?

Set a rough limit before you start, such as "one song" or "one page", and say it out loud. A clear small limit is easier to agree to than an open-ended activity. If the person wants to carry on when you reach it, carry on; if not, you have a clean finish.

- Keep materials in one tray or bag so setup takes a minute.
- Use the same two or three cards again. Familiar is easier than new.
- Let the person choose between two things, such as this song or that one.

## When should you stop?

Stop at your limit, or sooner if the person looks away, tires or becomes restless. Say what got done: "We sang three songs." Put the materials away together so the end of the activity is as simple as the start.

This hub is not for a day when even five minutes feels like too much. On those days, sitting together with a drink is enough, and nothing here needs to happen.
