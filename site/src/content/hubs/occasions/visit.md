A visit has its own shape. You arrive, there is a gap while everyone settles, and the next hour depends on how the person is that day. Something small to do together fills the gap without making the visit into a task. Choose one card before you go, bring what it needs, and let the person decide how far it goes.

## What should you bring?

Pack for one activity and keep a second in reserve. A pen, a few sheets of paper, a glue stick and a handful of printed photos cover most of these cards. If the person is in a care home, ask staff before you bring scissors, glue or anything small.

## How do you start without making it a task?

Hold the materials where the person can see them and say what you have. "I brought some old postcards. Would you like to look at them with me?" is easier to answer. "Do you want to do an activity?" is harder. If the answer is no, put them away and carry on with the visit.

- [Look through a photo album](/activity/look-through-a-photo-album/) with the person choosing which page to open.
- [Write a letter together](/activity/write-a-letter-together/): they say the words, you hold the pen.
- [Make a playlist together](/activity/make-a-playlist-together/) from songs they ask for, to play next time.
- [Hold a sing-along](/activity/hold-a-sing-along-at-home/) with a few songs they ask for.

## When should you stop?

Stop when the person looks away, tires, gets restless or turns to something else. Leave what you started where it is, and say so: "We can finish this next time." A half-finished letter is a good reason to come back.

This hub is not for a visit that has gone badly. If the person is unwell, distressed or does not know who you are, set the activity aside and give them your company. The [guide to visiting someone in care](/together/visiting-someone-in-care/) covers those moments.
