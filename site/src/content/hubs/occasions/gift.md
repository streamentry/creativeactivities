Giving something you made carries more than the object. It says someone was thought of. These cards are chosen so the person can make a real gift without needing craft skills, and so the result is small enough to wrap, carry and hand over.

## How do you choose what to make?

Start with who it is for, and let the person decide. A card for a friend, a sachet for a daughter, a painted pot for a neighbor. If they cannot decide, offer two choices and go with the one they point to. The decision belongs to them even when your hands do the work.

## What should you check before you give it?

Look at the card's safety notes, then at who will receive the gift. Dried lavender and small decorations are not for young children. Anything with a plant or food in it needs to be safe for the person who receives it. Wrap and label it so it is clear what it is.

- [Make a greeting card](/activity/make-a-greeting-card/) from pictures they choose.
- [Wrap a small gift](/activity/wrap-a-small-gift/) with paper, ribbon and a tag in their writing.
- [Make a photo postcard](/activity/make-a-photo-postcard/) from a picture they pick, to send.
- [Decorate a keepsake box](/activity/decorate-a-keepsake-box/) with paper they like.

## When should you stop?

Stop when the person tires, loses interest or is happy with it as it is. A gift does not have to be finished in one sitting, and a plain card is still a card. Put the unfinished piece somewhere safe and pick it up another day.

This hub is not for buying gifts, and it does not recommend products. It is also not for someone who finds gift-giving upsetting, perhaps because of a loss or a hard date. In that case, make something for the pleasure of making it and leave the giving out.
