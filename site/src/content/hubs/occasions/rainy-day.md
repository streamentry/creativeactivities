Rain changes the plan, and a plan with materials is easier to follow than one without. Put one of these on the table before anyone asks what there is to do. The aim is a shared afternoon, not a perfect result.

## What do you need on the table?

Most of these use paper, a glue stick and something to draw or paint with. Lay everything out before you start, cover the table, and keep a damp cloth and a bowl of water close by. Setting up first keeps the activity from stalling while someone fetches a missing piece.

## How do you make it work for two ages?

Give each person a job they can do well and swap if they like. A child can tear, press and choose; an older person can fold, arrange and suggest. Nobody should be corrected. [Weave paper strips](/activity/weave-paper-strips/) and [tear a paper mosaic](/activity/tear-paper-mosaic/) both work this way: a helper cuts the slits for the weaving beforehand, and everyone tears.

- [Draw a comic strip together](/activity/draw-a-comic-strip-together/): take turns adding a box.
- [Print with sponge shapes](/activity/print-with-sponge-shapes/): one stamps, one chooses the color.
- [Shape a pinch pot](/activity/shape-a-pinch-pot/): everyone has their own lump of clay.

## When should you stop?

Stop when interest fades, hands tire or someone starts to squabble. Put the work somewhere flat to dry and say you will look at it later. Wet paint and clay can wait overnight, and a half-made garland is still a garland tomorrow.

This hub is not for anyone who cannot sit at a table for a while. For someone in bed or a chair without a table, the [bedside activities hub](/bedside-activities/) has cards that fit on a lap tray.
