A rainy day indoors can feel long. A few crafts made from things you already have can fill an afternoon, and the child can change them as they go. This guide is for parents, grandparents and carers of children from about 3 upward. It gives four crafts, each with a list of household items, a few steps and simple variations.

All four crafts use materials most homes have: cardboard tubes from kitchen roll and toilet roll, egg cartons, paper plates and old newspaper. You may need some tape, non-toxic glue and washable paint.

## Who this suits

These crafts suit children who like to make things with their hands, to decorate, or to tell stories with puppets and characters. They suit children who enjoy a clear set of steps, and children who enjoy changing a plan. Younger children, from about 3, usually need help with scissors and glue. Older children can often do most of each craft on their own, with an adult nearby to help with the harder parts.

## Safety first

- **Adult nearby.** Stay close for scissors, glue, tape and paint.
- **Blunt scissors.** Use blunt scissors for children under about 6, and let an adult cut any shape that needs a sharp blade.
- **Non-toxic materials.** Look for a clear non-toxic label on paint, markers and glue. The Art and Creative Materials Institute uses an Approved Product seal on reviewed art materials.
- **Small pieces.** Under-3s should not have small pieces, such as cut-off egg cups, bottle caps, buttons, small tape rolls or beads. The Nemours KidsHealth choking guide lists arts and crafts supplies and small toy parts among common choking hazards. Remove any small pieces from the table when the craft is done.
- **Cords.** Keep string, yarn and cord away from under-3s, and never tie cord around a child's neck.
- **Latex.** Do not use latex balloons, and check that rubber bands and erasers are latex-free if a family member is allergic to latex. The American Academy of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology notes that natural rubber latex is often found in balloons, rubber bands and erasers.
- **Food and dye.** Some families avoid food dyes. Use washable paint or colored paper instead of food coloring.

## Cardboard tube puppets

**What you need:** three or four cardboard tubes from kitchen roll or toilet roll; paper or card; crayons or washable markers; tape or glue; scissors for an adult to use; googly eyes only if the child is 5 or older, because they are a small part.

**Steps:**

1. Choose one tube as the body. Decorate it with crayons or paint, and let it dry.
2. Draw a face on a piece of paper. An adult can cut out the face, or the child can cut it out with blunt scissors.
3. Tape or glue the face to one end of the tube.
4. Add arms from folded paper, taped to the sides of the tube.
5. Put your hand inside the tube and make the puppet talk. Let the child do the same.

**Easier version:** Ask an adult to cut the tube and the face in advance. Let the child decorate with stickers or stamps, using large stickers that peel easily.

**Calmer version:** Use one color of paint, and make a single puppet. A quiet puppet show with one character can be as satisfying as a longer one.

## Egg carton caterpillars

**What you need:** an egg carton with at least six cups; washable paint or markers; pipe cleaners for antennae, cut into short pieces by an adult; glue or tape.

**Steps:**

1. An adult cuts the carton into strips of three or four cups. Keep the cut edges smooth.
2. Let the child paint or color each cup. Let them choose the colors.
3. Once the paint is dry, glue the cups in a line, with the openings facing upwards.
4. Add a face to the first cup, and fix the pipe cleaner antennae at the front. Keep the pipe cleaners short and blunt-ended.
5. Put the caterpillar on a windowsill, and make a second one if the child wants.

**Easier version:** Use a carton that is already cut into strips, and let the child decorate with a sponge or a brush.

**Calmer version:** Use one color for the whole caterpillar. Leave out the antennae.

## Paper plate masks

**What you need:** paper plates; scissors for an adult to use for the eye holes; string or elastic for the back, cut by an adult; crayons, markers or washable paint; paper scraps.

**Steps:**

1. An adult cuts two eye holes in the plate, at the right height for the child. Make the holes large enough to see through.
2. Let the child decorate the plate. Offer paint, markers and paper scraps for ears, hair or a nose.
3. Let the paint dry fully before the mask is worn.
4. An adult ties the string or elastic at the back, loose enough to slip on and off.
5. Let the child wear the mask to play an animal or character for a short time.

**Easier version:** Use a plate with the eye holes already cut. Offer stickers or stamps instead of paint.

**Calmer version:** Make the mask without ears, hair or extra pieces. Keep the plate plain and quiet.

**Safety note:** A mask can make it harder to see, so keep the child close and stop if they cannot see clearly. Do not leave a child alone wearing a mask.

## Newspaper collage

**What you need:** old newspaper or magazine pages; a sheet of card; a glue stick or tape; scissors for an adult to use, or tearing by hand.

**Steps:**

1. Tear the newspaper into strips and pieces. Let the child tear as much as they like.
2. Draw a simple outline on the card, such as a house, a boat or a shape.
3. Glue or tape the pieces inside the outline, one at a time.
4. Let the child choose where each piece goes. Overlapping is fine.
5. Let it dry flat, and show it to someone in the family.

**Easier version:** Pre-tear the paper, and use a thick glue stick so pieces stick easily.

**Calmer version:** Use only one color of paper, such as blue, and make a single shape.

## Doing it with a grandparent

A grandparent can make a puppet or mask alongside the child. Each can show their finished piece, and the two can talk to each other through the puppets. A grandparent who is less able to sit on the floor can work at a table with the child. For more ideas to try together, see the [together ideas](/together/) section.

If your family is far away, you can make the same craft in both places and show each other the finished pieces on a call. For a longer idea, [Make a paper chain garland](/activity/make-a-paper-chain-garland/) gives a shared result to hang in each home.

## If it isn't working

- **The child wants to change the plan.** Let them. A puppet with an extra arm may be better than the one you planned.
- **The child loses interest after one part.** Stop there. Leave the parts to dry, and come back another day.
- **The glue is everywhere.** Use tape, or put the glue on a tray and let the child press pieces onto the glue.
- **The child is frustrated by cutting.** Let an adult do the cutting, and let the child decorate, tear and glue.

If you have a question about a child's health or allergies, ask your child's own clinician.
