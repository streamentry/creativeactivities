A small, low-cost kit is easier to use than a full studio, and most people never need more than about ten items. This page lists what to buy, cheap substitutes, adaptations for arthritis, low vision and working in bed, what to avoid, and how to store the kit so it is ready at a moment's notice.

## What belongs in a basic art kit?

The table below lists the core items. Each one is common, inexpensive and useful across many activities. The substitutes are ones you can find at most discount or hardware stores.

| Item | Why it helps | Low-cost substitute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thick brushes, 1 inch or wider | Big strokes, easy to hold | Foam paint brushes |
| Watercolor pan set, 12 colors | Colors are ready to use, no mixing | Store-brand pan set or a few tubes |
| Heavy paper, 140 lb or 300 gsm | Holds water without buckling | Heavy card or a mixed-media pad |
| Glue stick | Clean, no-drip gluing | Any non-toxic glue stick |
| Soft pencils, 2B or 4B | Dark marks with little pressure | Standard graphite pencils |
| Oil pastels | Rich color, no water or brush needed | Chunky crayons |
| Air-dry clay | Squeezing and shaping, no kiln | Modeling dough, labeled non-toxic |
| Masking tape | Holds paper still | Painter's tape |
| Tray with a rim | Keeps supplies together, catches spills | A baking sheet with sides |
| Apron or old cotton shirt | Protects clothing | An old shirt worn backward |

Check the labels on glue, clay and paint for allergens, and look for a non-toxic mark. Activity cards such as [Tear paper mosaic](/activity/tear-paper-mosaic/) and [Paint with watercolors](/activity/paint-with-watercolors/) use only these basics.

## What adaptations help with arthritis, low vision or working in bed?

**Arthritis.** Wrap handles in foam tape to make them thicker and easier to grip. Choose light brushes and markers. Use a clip or tape to hold paper, so the person does not have to press down. Support the forearm on the table.

**Low vision.** Use high-contrast colors, such as a black marker on white paper, and bold, simple subjects like a single flower. Place a good lamp to the side to reduce glare. The [Arrange flowers together](/activity/arrange-flowers-together/) card includes a low-vision version.

**Working in bed.** Use a lap tray or a clipboard propped on pillows. Put a non-slip mat under the paper. A small clamp light that attaches to a headboard or shelf helps. Keep the kit to a few items, such as oil pastels, a glue stick and a card to work on.

## What should you avoid?

- Glitter, loose beads and small stickers, which can go in the mouth.
- Solvent-based paints, spray adhesives and varnish without good ventilation.
- Craft knives and hot glue guns. Do any cutting yourself, ahead of time.
- Open jars of liquid that can tip over.
- Children's art sets with cartoon designs. Adults often find them off-putting.
- Large kits you feel pressure to use in full.

## How do you store the kit so it is ready in a minute?

Use one clear, lidded box or tray for everything. Stand brushes in a cup with the bristles up. Keep pencils and pastels in a shoebox section, and lay paper flat under the lid. Store the box where it is easy to reach and out of the way of pets and small children. Check clay and paint every month, and replace anything that has dried out. Tape a short list inside the lid, so anyone can tell what is missing. When it is time to paint, lift the tray, and you are ready to start.
