A life-story book is a small album about everyday things from someone's life: a favorite cup, the street where they shopped, a tool they used. Families often try to make one by sitting down and asking questions. This guide takes a different route. You make the book together, one page per session, by handling, drawing and making things. Nobody has to answer anything.

## Why one page at a time?

Short sessions suit most people living with dementia, and a single page is a complete session. You finish a page, put the book away, and that is a success. The book grows over weeks, not in one long sitting.

## What goes on a page?

Each page holds one thing. Choose one of these:

1. **A photo or drawing.** A copy of a photo, or a quick sketch of a cup, a chair or a window.
2. **A rubbing.** Lay a leaf, a textured tile or a piece of lace under thin paper and rub gently with the side of a crayon.
3. **A few words.** A caption of five words or fewer, in large print. "Blue kitchen. Kettle on the stove."
4. **A small object.** A ribbon, a seed packet or a ticket. Use a copy of anything precious, and keep the original safe.

## Prompts about things, not feelings

Use these as invitations. The person may skip any of them.

- **A cup:** "If they like, hold it up and ask, 'What do you put in a cup like this?'"
- **A kitchen:** with a picture of a kitchen, "Which of these would you want on the table?"
- **A street:** with a picture of shops, "Which one would you go into first?"
- **A tool:** "What would you use this for? Show me how you would hold it."
- **A recipe:** "Is there a dish you like the smell of?"
- **A song title:** "Would you like to hear this one? You can hum along or just listen."
- **A map:** "Would you like to draw the way to a park you like?"

Never quiz: do not ask for names or dates, ask who a person in a photo is, or say whether an answer is right. Each prompt is about the thing in front of you, not about the past. A page is not a test. If the person says something different from what you expected, write it down as they said it.

## Who keeps the book?

One family member keeps the book and writes the captions, while the person chooses what goes on each page. The book lives at home, in a binder with sheet protectors or a simple box, where the person can take it out whenever they want.

## How to make it, step by step

1. **Choose a binder or box**, with a few blank pages or cards.
2. **Agree a first topic together.** Pick one everyday object or place.
3. **Make one page in one sitting.** Stop when the person tires, even halfway through.
4. **Add the page and show it** at the next visit, before you start a new one.
5. **Leave gaps.** Blank pages, or pages that stay unfinished, are fine.

## If a page brings up sadness

Some objects and pictures bring up sadness. If that happens, stop. Sit together and say something warm, such as "Let's just look at this for a moment." Put the page away and come back another day, or never. Do not ask the person to explain what they feel, and do not add a caption about it.

## Privacy

The photos, drawings and the finished book stay with the family. Making the book needs no uploading, and this guide does not ask you to send anything anywhere. Use copies of precious photos, keep originals safe, and ask the person before sharing any page with other people.
