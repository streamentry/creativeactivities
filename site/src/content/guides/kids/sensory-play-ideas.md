Sensory play is play with the senses: touch, sight, sound, temperature and movement. A child who pours warm water, squeezes a wet sponge or shakes a bottle is doing sensory play. It does not need special equipment. Most of the ideas on this page use things already in the kitchen, the bathroom or the recycling.

This guide is for parents, grandparents and carers of children from about 1 year old. Each idea says who it suits and gives a lower-mess version, so you can choose what works in your home on the day.

## Who this suits

Sensory play suits children who enjoy exploring materials, and it can also be a calm, easy activity for a child who likes to feel things with their hands or feet. Some children prefer to touch very little. Some like a lot of movement. Some prefer to look and listen. All of these are fine. This page does not sort children into types. It offers choices, and you know your child best.

## Safety first

- **Adult within arm's reach.** Always stay close to a child under 5 during water play. A child can drown in a small amount of water, so never leave them alone with a bowl or bucket, even for a moment.
- **No small parts for under-3s.** Keep beads, buttons, coins, dried beans, rice, pasta and small toys out of reach. The Nemours KidsHealth choking guide lists small toy parts, coins and batteries among common choking hazards.
- **Dried beans for 5 and up only.** A dry bean tray is for children aged 5 and up, with an adult close by. Use dried rice or pasta with the same care.
- **No edible play.** Do not use flour, cloud dough or salt dough for mouthing, and do not offer food as a material. Wheat, egg and milk are among the common food allergens listed by Food Allergy Research and Education, so check before any flour or egg is used. Ask the family before using food of any kind.
- **Sealed bottles only.** A sensory bottle with glitter, water and a sealed cap is not for under-3s. Use strong glue on the cap, and check the seal before each use.
- **No plastic bags.** Do not use bags as a sensory material for children of any age who might put them over their face.
- **Check for allergies and skin reactions.** Some families avoid food dyes, so use non-food color in water or dough. Check that any soap, bubble mix or lotion is safe for the child's skin.
- **Keep chemicals locked away.** Cleaning products, bubble bath and dish soap can be poisonous if swallowed. Keep them out of reach. The HealthyChildren.org poison prevention guide has more on this.

## Warm and cool bowls

**What you need:** two shallow bowls; a jug of warm water (checked on the inside of your wrist); a jug of cool water; a towel; a few plastic cups and spoons.

**Steps:**

1. Sit the child on the floor or at a low table with a towel under the bowls.
2. Let them feel the warm water with a hand, or a spoon if they prefer. Name what they feel: "That's warm."
3. Offer the cool water. Ask, "Is it the same, or different?"
4. Let them pour water between cups, or move cups from one bowl to the other.
5. Stop when the child stops. Tip the water into a bucket, not the sink, and wipe the table.

**Lower-mess version:** Use a single bowl and a small amount of water. Put the bowl in a tray with raised sides.

## A feely bag

**What you need:** a fabric bag or pillowcase; four or five objects too large to fit through a toilet roll tube, such as a wooden spoon, a soft toy, a sponge, a bath toy and a pine cone.

**Steps:**

1. Put the objects in the bag, and tie it closed. Make sure nothing small can slip out.
2. Let the child reach in and pick one object without looking.
3. Ask, "What does it feel like?" and wait. Offer words such as "soft," "bumpy" or "smooth."
4. Let the child take the object out to see it.
5. Put it back and try again, or stop.

**Lower-mess version:** Use dry objects only. Keep the bag on your lap, and let the child touch the bag rather than reach in.

## Bubble wrap stomping

**What you need:** a sheet of bubble wrap, about a meter square, laid flat on a hard floor; a sheet of plain paper to lay on top, if you prefer a quieter feel.

**Steps:**

1. Lay the bubble wrap bubble-side up on a clear floor.
2. Take off shoes and socks if the child likes that. Ask them to walk, stamp or hop on the bubbles.
3. Name the sound: "Pop, pop." Let them choose how to move.
4. Fold the sheet and do it again if the child wants more.
5. Put the bubble wrap away when the child has had enough, and keep it off the floor afterwards.

**Calmer version:** Lay a towel over the bubble wrap, so the sound is softer. Stomp slowly, or let the child press one bubble at a time with a finger.

## A sealed shaker bottle

Sensory bottles are good for children who like to look at things move. Make one with an adult, and keep it for children aged 3 and up with supervision.

**What you need:** a clear plastic bottle with a strong, screw-on cap; water; a few drops of washable, non-food color; a little glitter or sequins that are large enough not to be swallowed; strong waterproof glue to seal the cap.

**Steps:**

1. Fill the bottle about three-quarters full with water, and add the color and the glitter.
2. Screw the cap on tightly. Run a line of strong glue around the join, and let it dry fully.
3. Let the child shake the bottle and watch the glitter drift.
4. Put it away after use, and keep it out of reach of under-3s.

**Lower-mess version:** Use a smaller bottle, and let the child hold it on a tray. Check the seal before each use, and throw the bottle away if it leaks.

## Clean-up is part of the plan

Plan clean-up before you start. Keep a towel, a bin and a bucket close by. Put wet materials on a tray so you can carry them to the sink in one trip. Wipe the table with a damp cloth and let it dry. For children, a short clean-up song or a simple task, such as putting the spoons in a cup, can make the end of play feel like part of the game.

If mess is hard for your child, start with the lowest-mess version and add more later. Keep a damp cloth on the child's chair so they can wipe their hands whenever they like.

## Doing it with a grandparent

A grandparent can run the warm and cool bowls while the child decides where to pour. Grandparents who have more time can sit on the floor for the feely bag and talk through the textures. A grandparent who is less mobile can sit at a table and hand over objects instead. For more ideas to try together, see the [together ideas](/together/) section.

## If it isn't working

- **The child cries or pulls away.** Stop, wipe their hands with a towel, and offer a quiet activity instead. Try again another day, with less to touch.
- **The child puts things in their mouth.** Remove the object and the small parts. Use only large, safe items, and keep the session close to the adult.
- **The child wants only to watch.** That is fine. Hold the materials and show them, or let them watch from a chair.
- **The mess is too much.** Use one tray with raised edges, or move the activity outdoors if the weather allows.

If you are unsure about a child's skin, allergies or any health question, ask your child's own clinician.
