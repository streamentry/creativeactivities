A creative journal is a place to put small things down on paper: a color you noticed, a leaf, a line from a song, a list of what the weather was like. It is a creative activity, not therapy. It does not ask you to explain yourself. If you or the person you care for has a health concern, talk with your own doctor or a licensed professional.

## Who is this for?

Creative journaling suits adults who like making things by hand and want a steady, private habit. It suits people who find writing hard but enjoy pictures, and people who prefer a short list to a long page. It can also be a shared activity, such as a family collage book that several people add to. These are suggestions. Use whatever feels comfortable.

## What do you need?

- A notebook with plain or thick pages, or loose sheets in a folder
- Glue stick, scissors with rounded tips, and masking tape
- Old magazines, catalogs, wrapping paper, fabric scraps or photo prints you choose
- Pens, pencils, crayons or markers
- Dried leaves, ticket stubs or other flat items you already have

## Three simple kinds of journal

**The collage journal.** Each page has a theme, such as soft things, blue things or things you can hear. Tear or cut shapes from paper you already own and arrange them. Glue them down once you are happy with the layout. The [make a color collage](/activity/make-a-color-collage/) card has setup steps.

**The drawing journal.** Draw one object each day, in pencil or pen, with a few lines and a little color. The drawing does not need to be finished. The [sketch what is on the table](/activity/sketch-what-is-on-the-table/) card works well here.

**The list and sketch journal.** Write a short list, such as three sounds you heard today, or sketch a single view from a window. A line or two is enough.

## Prompts that do not ask for feelings

Choose one prompt when you open the journal, and leave the rest for another day.

1. Draw or write down the first thing you see when you wake up.
2. Glue five pieces of paper or fabric, each with a different texture, and label them by touch only.
3. Name three colors you noticed in the room, outside or on the way home.
4. Sketch the shape of one plant, one cup or one piece of fruit.
5. Collect a leaf, a bus ticket and a piece of wrapping paper, and arrange them on one page.
6. Write one sound you heard today and where it came from.
7. Choose one photo you like, and describe only the light in it.
8. Draw the outline of your hand, then fill it with pattern.

If a prompt feels like a demand, pick another one, or make a page with no prompt at all.

## Keeping it private

Privacy is part of the practice. Keep the journal somewhere only you can reach, and be clear with family or friends that it is yours. If you use a phone or tablet for digital notes, use a passcode, and check which apps can see your photos. If you share a journal with someone, decide together what goes in it and who can read it. Nobody should be asked to show a page they do not want to show.

## Easier versions

- **Seated or in bed.** Keep the journal on a lap tray or clipboard, with glue and scraps in a small box.
- **Easier to grip.** Use a thick marker, a big glue stick and a child-safe pair of scissors with rounded tips, or tear paper instead of cutting it.
- **Low vision.** Use bold colors, large pieces and dark markers on white or cream paper. Ask for a large-print list of prompts.
- **Fewer choices.** Offer two colors of paper and one prompt. Put the glue and scraps out on the table before you start.
- **Shorter.** One sticker, one color or one line is a complete page.

## Safety notes

- Use a glue stick or non-toxic glue. Avoid small loose items that could be swallowed, such as beads or buttons, if anyone in the room might put things in their mouth.
- Check the edges of scissors and paper. Tear paper if cutting is hard.
- Keep scented sprays and strong glues away from the work area if someone has breathing problems or allergies.
- Do not use photos of other people unless they have agreed to it.

## If it isn't working

If a page feels heavy, leave it alone. You can turn it over, put it in a folder, or begin a new page on a different theme. If a prompt brings up something painful, stop. Put the journal away, and do something calm and familiar, such as a warm drink or a walk. If the feeling stays with you, reach out to someone you trust, or to a helpline.

The [finish the saying](/games/finish-the-saying/) game is another light way to put words on a page without a heavy question. Most of all, remember that a journal can stay half finished. That is a good outcome too.

If you are in distress or thinking about harming yourself, do not wait. Use the crisis route shown on this page, or call your local emergency number.
