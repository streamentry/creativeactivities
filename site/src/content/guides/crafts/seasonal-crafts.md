A season gives you a reason to make something. The leaves turn and there is a print to make. The days shorten and a garland brightens a room. The first warm weeks suit a pot of seeds on the windowsill. Seasonal crafts are simple to plan, and the materials are often free. This page covers ideas for each part of the year, and for celebrations in many traditions, along with how to keep the making calm and safe.

These ideas suit families, friends, care settings and community groups. They suit someone who enjoys the seasons, someone who has a holiday they want to mark, and someone who would simply like a reason to sit together and make something. Choosing a craft is about what the person enjoys, not a judgment about what they can do.

## How do you choose a seasonal craft?

Start with what is around you. A walk to gather leaves, or a look at what is blooming, often gives the best project. Then think about the person:

- **Do they enjoy the outdoors?** Choose something that uses nature, such as a leaf print or a seed pot.
- **Do they enjoy paper and cards?** A folded card for someone they care about, or a paper garland, may suit.
- **Do they enjoy birds or animals?** A pine cone bird feeder can be made and hung in view of a window.
- **Would they prefer something quiet?** A texture board or a sealed shaker gives a calm, hands-on result.

## Seasonal crafts across the year

### Spring

- **Plant seeds in a pot.** Large, easy seeds in a pot of compost, watered gently. The [seed pot card](/activity/plant-seeds-in-a-pot/) has seated and easier versions.
- **Paint a garden stone.** A smooth stone painted with a simple repeated dot or wave, for a path or a window box. See the [garden stone card](/activity/paint-a-garden-stone/).

### Summer

- **Make a lavender sachet.** Fill and tie a small fabric pouch with dried lavender. It needs no sewing, and there are bedside and unscented versions. See the [lavender sachet card](/activity/make-a-lavender-sachet/).
- **Print with leaves.** Paint the back of a leaf and press it onto paper to show the veins. This can be done outdoors or at a table. See the [leaf print card](/activity/print-with-leaves/).

### Autumn

- **Press leaves and flowers.** Press them in a heavy book for a week, then glue them onto a card. See the [pressing card](/activity/press-leaves-and-flowers/).
- **Make a memory collage.** A seasonal collage from magazine pictures and photos, for a shared table. See the [memory collage card](/activity/make-a-memory-collage/).

### Winter

- **Make a paper chain garland.** Link strips of colored paper, one at a time. It can be hung in a room, and it is easy to share. See the [paper chain card](/activity/make-a-paper-chain-garland/).
- **Make a pine cone bird feeder.** Press fat and birdseed into a pine cone, then hang it where you can watch the birds. The [bird feeder card](/activity/make-a-pine-cone-bird-feeder/) includes a nut-free option.
- **Make a greeting card.** A folded card for a winter occasion, sent to someone the person cares about. See the [greeting card card](/activity/make-a-greeting-card/).

## Holidays in many traditions

Many people mark celebrations at this time of year, and many do not. Some festivals use paper lanterns, cut-paper banners, red envelopes or painted patterns. Some use none of these. This site does not assume one holiday, one faith or one family shape.

When you plan a holiday craft:

1. **Ask first.** Ask what the person would like to mark, and which days matter to them. Let them choose the occasion, or choose none.
2. **Use materials, not symbols.** Paper, leaves, fabric and seeds work for almost any season. Avoid religious or cultural symbols unless you know their meaning and the person would welcome them.
3. **Ask someone from that tradition.** If you do want to make a craft linked to a particular festival, ask someone who knows it well how it is marked and what is appropriate. A translation alone is not enough.
4. **Keep the choice open.** Offer two or three options, and accept a no.

## What easier versions can you try?

- **Seated.** Most seasonal crafts work at a table. Set the materials in reach.
- **Easier to grip.** Use thicker paper and wider tools. Pre-fold the card, or pre-cut the chain strips.
- **Low vision.** Choose bright paper with strong contrast, and one large picture on each card.
- **Fewer choices.** Offer two colors, or one leaf and one pot. Use a finished sample to copy.
- **Shorter.** Pre-glue the first link or the first leaf, so the person adds only the last piece.
- **Nonverbal.** Show two finished examples, and watch for a reach or a look.

## Safety notes for seasonal crafts

- **Lights.** Use LED or battery lights in paper lanterns and garlands. Do not use candles near paper, fabric or dried plants.
- **Decorations.** Do not use candy, or artificial fruit or food, as decoration. Some people living with dementia may mistake them for real food. The Alzheimer's Association [holidays guidance](https://www.alz.org/help-support/caregiving/holidays) also notes that blinking lights may be confusing.
- **Small parts.** Keep buttons, beads, small ornaments and loose seeds out of reach of under-threes. The [HealthyChildren.org choking prevention page](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/pages/choking-prevention.aspx) lists small parts among the common causes of choking.
- **Plants.** Do not taste or handle plants or berries you cannot identify. Wash hands after handling soil or sap.
- **Outdoors.** Check for ticks, sun and insect bites after time outside.

## If it isn't working

- **The day is busy.** Move the craft to a quiet room, and keep it short. A single leaf or one card is a complete session.
- **Frustration shows.** Stop, and offer a rest or a walk. A seasonal craft can be finished another day.
- **The person does not want to make anything.** Sit together and look at the season. Talk about what you see outside. That counts.

## Making it calm for the day

Holidays can bring noise, crowds and a change to the usual routine. Plan around the person's usual routine, and keep the day smaller if you can. Hand out simple jobs, such as folding napkins or washing vegetables, and keep a quiet room ready if the day becomes too much. Video calls can let distant family take part without joining a crowd.

## Doing it with an older adult or someone living with dementia

Seasonal crafts can be a gentle way to mark a time of year with an older adult, including someone living with dementia. Choose a familiar craft, such as a card or a leaf print, and keep it to one step. Let them choose the colors or press the leaf. Old photographs of past seasons can start a conversation, and the [reminiscence guide](/activities/reminiscence/) explains how to talk about the past without quizzing. Singing a seasonal song, which the Alzheimer's Association [suggests](https://www.alz.org/help-support/caregiving/holidays), is another way to take part.

If the person has been recently diagnosed, or is still getting used to a new care setting, our guide on [after a dementia diagnosis](/caregiving/after-dementia-diagnosis/) may help.

Creative Activities is not therapy. Seasonal crafts are a way to notice the time of year and make something together.
